Vitamin A (Retinol)

Labs & Biomarkers · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Vitamin A is a fat-soluble vitamin the body uses for vision, immune function, and cell growth and differentiation.[1] On a lab panel it's measured as serum retinol, the vitamin's main circulating form, from a standard blood draw. It usually appears on nutritional or micronutrient panels rather than routine screens.

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Vitamin A Measures

A vitamin A blood test measures retinol, the form of the vitamin that circulates in blood.[1] Retinol is the parent compound of a small family of molecules the body builds from it. The best known is retinal, the light-sensing piece of the visual cycle. Retinal sits inside the pigment rhodopsin in the retina's rod cells, and when light hits it, it changes shape and starts the signal your brain reads as sight.[2] The names aren't a coincidence. Retinol and retinal are both named after the retina, where the vitamin's role was first worked out.

Vision is the famous job, but not the only one. Vitamin A also supports immune function and directs how cells grow and specialize, which is why it matters for skin and the linings of the gut and airways.[1]

It reaches the body in two forms. Preformed vitamin A, meaning retinol and retinyl esters, comes from animal foods like dairy, fish, and liver. Provitamin A carotenoids like beta-carotene come from colorful plants, and the body converts them to retinol.[1]

How It's Measured

Serum retinol comes from a standard venous blood draw, usually ordered as part of a nutritional or micronutrient panel rather than a routine screen. Labs report their own reference interval with each result, and that interval varies by lab and testing method.

Why People Track Vitamin A

People who get micronutrient panels done, on their own or through a clinician, end up with retinol results scattered across PDFs from different labs and different years. Keeping them in one timeline is what makes a repeat draw comparable to the last one.

One honest caveat belongs here: serum retinol is a blunter marker than most lab values. The body holds circulating retinol within a tight band, drawing on stores to keep the blood level steady, so the number mainly shifts when reserves are very low or very high.[3] The liver holds roughly 70% of the body's vitamin A.[1] A single serum retinol result is one dated data point, not a running readout of those reserves.

What the Research Shows

The liver is the body's main vitamin A depot, and research is still mapping how the vitamin moves between the liver cells that take it up and the cells that store it.[4]

A 2016 review of vitamin A biomarkers concluded that serum retinol is under homeostatic control, partly because the vitamin is toxic at high concentrations, which limits how well the blood level reflects status outside the extremes.[3]

High intakes of preformed vitamin A in early pregnancy were linked to birth defects in a 1995 New England Journal of Medicine study of over 22,000 pregnancies, which is why vitamin A intake during pregnancy is a topic clinicians manage.[5]

Related Terms

The other fat-soluble vitamin that shows up on nutritional blood panels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

What's the difference between vitamin A and retinol?

Vitamin A is the family name for a group of fat-soluble compounds. Retinol is the main form that circulates in blood, which is why a vitamin A blood test is usually reported as serum retinol. Preformed vitamin A comes from animal foods, and the body also makes retinol from provitamin A carotenoids like beta-carotene in plants.

Does a serum retinol test show total body stores?

Not directly. The body holds circulating retinol within a tight band, drawing on liver stores to keep the blood level steady. Because of that homeostatic control, serum retinol mainly shifts when stores are very low or very high, so a single result is one dated data point rather than a running readout of reserves.
