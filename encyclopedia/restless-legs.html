Restless Legs

Symptoms · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Restless legs describes an urge to move the legs that starts at rest, often with uncomfortable sensations. Moving or stretching eases it, and it tends to be worse in the evening or at night.[1] Researchers estimate restless legs syndrome affects between 5% and 15% of the population.[1]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Restless Legs Describes

The defining features in the research literature are consistent: an uncomfortable urge to move the legs while at rest, relief when you move or get up and walk, and worse severity at night.[2] The sensations start at rest and are described as abnormal but non-painful.[1] That daily rhythm is part of the definition itself: studies describe a diurnal pattern in which symptoms worsen at night.[1]

How It's Recorded

Restless legs is defined by what you feel, so it's recorded as a self-report: you log the evenings or nights it showed up. A sleep tracker records what happened to your sleep separately, which is what makes the two worth charting together.

Why People Track Restless Legs

The symptom lands at the worst possible time for sleep, so people log it to see how often it happens and whether rough nights line up with logged episodes. Iron is the other reason: some people who log the symptom also chart it alongside iron panel results like ferritin and serum iron — see What the Research Shows below for what population-level research says about that connection.

What the Research Shows

At the population level, neuropathology and imaging studies report decreased iron in specific brain regions, including the substantia nigra and thalamus, among people with restless legs syndrome, and an international task force review likewise states that brain iron deficiency has been implicated in the condition's pathophysiology.[1][3] This is a population-level research association, not a diagnostic marker — it does not mean an individual's restless legs indicate their personal iron status.

Several studies also report an association between restless legs and pregnancy. A 2022 narrative review found hormonal fluctuations and iron metabolism were the most-studied hypotheses for that link, and concluded the evidence is still incomplete.[4]

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

What does restless legs mean?

Restless legs describes an urge to move the legs that starts at rest, often with uncomfortable but non-painful sensations. Research describes movement or stretching partially or fully relieving it, and a daily pattern where it's worse in the evening or at night.[1]

Is restless legs connected to iron?

See What the Research Shows above for what population-level research says about restless legs and iron — including an explicit note that the association is not diagnostic for any individual. That's why some people who log the symptom also chart it alongside iron labs like ferritin and serum iron.

Why do people log restless legs with their sleep data?

Research describes restless legs following a daily pattern that worsens at night, right when people are trying to fall asleep.[1] Logging the symptom next to measured sleep data shows whether restless nights and logged episodes line up over time.
