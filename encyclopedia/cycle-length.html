Cycle Length

Cycle & Fertility · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Cycle length is the number of days from the first day of one period through the day before the next period starts. Day 1 of a cycle is the first day of menstrual flow.[1] In a study of 612,613 real-world cycles, the mean cycle length was 29.3 days.[2]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Cycle Length Measures

Cycle length measures one full pass through the menstrual cycle, from the start of one period to the start of the next. Under the day count sit two phases: the follicular phase, which runs from day 1 to ovulation, and the luteal phase, which runs from ovulation to the next period.[1]

How Cycle Days Are Counted

No device measures cycle length. It's counted. Day 1 is the first day of menstrual flow.[1] From there the days count up until the next period starts, and that day becomes day 1 of a new cycle. Most trackers, Go Go Gaia included, treat the first day of real flow as day 1 rather than any light spotting that shows up beforehand.

Why People Track Cycle Length

Cycle length is the backbone of every period prediction. An app that knows your typical length can estimate your next start, and more logged cycles make that estimate better. Length history is also how people spot their own patterns, like cycles that run longer during stressful months or gradually shorten with age.[2]

What the Research Shows

The largest real-world dataset comes from a 2019 study of 612,613 cycles logged by 124,648 app users. Mean cycle length was 29.3 days with a standard deviation of 5.2 days, and mean period length was 4.0 days.[2]

The same study found cycles shorten and settle with age. Mean cycle length dropped by 3.2 days from age 25 to 45, mostly because the follicular phase got shorter, while the luteal phase stayed fairly stable.[2]

Cycle-to-cycle variability lives mostly in the first half of the cycle. A pooled analysis of more than 3,300 cycles found the follicular phase averaged 18.5 days with a standard deviation of 6.5 days, against 11.7 days and a standard deviation of only 2.8 for the luteal phase.[3] A separate study of over 1,000 cycles from 141 healthy women reached the same conclusion: the follicular phase contributes most to overall cycle variability.[4] That's why predictions drift, and our follicular phase entry covers the mechanism.

Related Terms

Follicular Phase

The first half of the cycle, and the source of most cycle-length variability.

Luteal Phase

The second half of the cycle, and the more consistent of the two phases.

Period Flow

How much menstrual bleeding happens on the days that start each cycle.

Spotting & Intermenstrual Bleeding

Light bleeding between periods, logged separately so it doesn't reset the day count.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

What counts as day 1 of a cycle?

Day 1 is the first day of menstrual flow.[1] The count runs until the next period starts, which resets it to day 1 of a new cycle. Most trackers, Go Go Gaia included, ask you to log from the first day of real flow rather than light spotting beforehand.

Is a 28-day cycle the average?

Not quite. A study of 612,613 real-world cycles from app users found a mean cycle length of 29.3 days, with a standard deviation of 5.2 days.[2] The familiar 28-day figure is close to the average but it isn't a standard individual cycles are expected to match.

Why does my cycle length change from month to month?

Most of the change comes from the follicular phase, the stretch between day 1 and ovulation. A pooled analysis of more than 3,300 cycles found the follicular phase varied far more than the luteal phase, with a standard deviation of 6.5 days versus 2.8 days.[3] When a cycle runs long or short, the first half is usually where the difference happened.
