Sodium (Serum)

Labs & Biomarkers · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Sodium is the major cation in extracellular fluid, where it helps set blood volume and supports nerve and muscle signaling.[1] The kidneys keep sodium levels in tight balance, largely through the hormone aldosterone.[1] On a lab panel it's measured as serum sodium, and it's also a dietary nutrient logged from food.[2]

What Sodium Measures

Sodium is one of the essential electrolytes in the fluid that surrounds your cells, where it's the main ion responsible for holding that fluid's volume steady.[1] Because water follows sodium across cell membranes, sodium is also central to how much fluid stays in your bloodstream versus your tissues, and to the electrical gradient nerve and muscle cells use to fire.[1] The kidneys are the main regulator. Most sodium reabsorption happens in the proximal tubule, and the hormone aldosterone fine-tunes how much gets reclaimed further along, through sodium-chloride transport in the kidney.[1]

How It's Measured

Serum sodium comes from a standard blood draw and is reported in milliequivalents per liter. It's rarely ordered by itself, since it's one of the eight values on a basic metabolic panel and one of the electrolytes on an electrolyte panel, tests ordered together specifically so fluid balance and kidney function can be read as a set.[2][3] Sodium is also tracked a completely different way: as milligrams consumed, added up from the foods and drinks you log rather than measured in blood.

Why People Track Sodium

Sodium shows up on almost every routine blood panel, so anyone uploading lab results ends up with a sodium history without seeking one out. Dietary sodium is a different kind of number, one that reflects what you ate rather than how your kidneys are managing fluid, and it's tracked the way any other logged nutrient is: totaled per meal and per day.

The two are worth keeping in view together because sodium handling shifts across the menstrual cycle. Progesterone rises through the luteal phase, and a 2006 study found progesterone directly correlated with both basal serum aldosterone and the aldosterone response to a standard stimulus, a relationship estrogen didn't show.[4] Since aldosterone is the hormone that governs how much sodium the kidneys hold onto, that's a mechanism connecting where you are in your cycle to how your body is managing sodium and fluid.

What the Research Shows

Sodium's role in extracellular volume and the kidney's aldosterone-driven control of it are established physiology, and they're the basis for reading serum sodium alongside potassium, chloride, and bicarbonate as a set.[1]

A 2006 study in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism measured aldosterone and progesterone together across the menstrual cycle and found progesterone directly correlated with urinary aldosterone, basal serum aldosterone, and the aldosterone response to infused angiotensin II, independent of the renin-angiotensin system.[4]

Pregnancy shows sodium's role at a larger scale. A 2017 review in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition describes a 30 to 50 percent expansion of plasma volume by late pregnancy, driven substantially by increased sodium and water reabsorption in the kidney.[5]

Related Terms

Potassium (Serum)

The main intracellular electrolyte, tracked from both food logs and lab uploads like sodium.

Chloride

The extracellular anion that pairs with sodium on the same lab panel.

Bicarbonate (CO2)

The electrolyte on the same panel that reflects acid-base balance rather than fluid volume.

Creatinine

The kidney-function value ordered alongside sodium on a basic metabolic panel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Does Go Go Gaia run sodium blood tests?

No. Go Go Gaia doesn't draw blood or run labs. You get a serum sodium result from bloodwork ordered through a clinician or lab service, then upload it to your Go Go Gaia account so it's stored and charted alongside your other tracked data.

Is dietary sodium the same as the sodium on a blood panel?

They're related but not the same measurement. Serum sodium is the concentration circulating in your blood at the moment of a draw, held in a tight range by the kidneys. Dietary sodium is the amount you eat, which Go Go Gaia's food log totals from logged meals as its own nutrient, separate from any uploaded lab result.

Why is sodium grouped with potassium, chloride, and bicarbonate on a lab report?

Those four are commonly read together on a basic metabolic panel, and they're also among the electrolytes measured on a broader electrolyte panel, since together they help assess fluid balance and acid-base status.
