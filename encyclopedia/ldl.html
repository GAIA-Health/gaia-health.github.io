LDL Cholesterol

Labs & Biomarkers · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

LDL cholesterol is the cholesterol carried by low-density lipoprotein, the particle that delivers most of the cholesterol circulating in blood to tissues around the body.[1] It's reported on a standard lipid panel in milligrams per deciliter, and labs either measure it directly or estimate it from the panel's other values.[3]

What LDL Cholesterol Measures

Cholesterol doesn't dissolve in blood, so it travels packed inside lipoprotein particles. The HDL entry covers how those particle types differ. LDL, short for low-density lipoprotein, is the main delivery particle. It carries the majority of the cholesterol in circulation and moves it from the liver out to the body's tissues.[1] An LDL cholesterol result reports the mass of cholesterol riding in those particles, not the number of particles themselves. ApoB is the test that counts particles, and that entry covers the distinction. A related particle, lipoprotein(a), gets its own separate test.

How It's Measured

LDL cholesterol comes from a standard lipid panel, a blood test that also reports total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides.[5] Many labs don't measure it directly. They estimate it from the other three values in the same draw, using an equation Friedewald and colleagues published in 1972.[2] Direct LDL assays exist too, and labs switch to them when the estimate turns unreliable, such as when triglycerides run very high.[3] The same blood can give slightly different LDL numbers depending on which method the lab used.

Why People Track LDL Cholesterol

LDL cholesterol shifts with hormonal life stages, which is why it belongs in a women's health tracker at all. The SWAN study followed US women across the menopause transition and found that total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, and apolipoprotein B rose substantially within the year surrounding the final menstrual period. The authors read that pattern as menopause-induced change rather than ordinary aging.[4] A single LDL result can't show a shift like that. A series of results kept over years, next to cycle and menopause data, can.

What the Research Shows

LDL carries the majority of the cholesterol in circulation, which is why lipid panels report it on its own line instead of stopping at total cholesterol.[1]

In SWAN data, LDL cholesterol showed a substantial increase within the one-year window before and after the final menstrual period, alongside total cholesterol and apolipoprotein B. The pattern held across ethnic groups, which is what separated it from the slow, steady drift expected from aging alone.[4]

The Friedewald estimate has documented limits, and laboratory references describe the situations where direct measurement replaces it.[3]

Related Terms

Total Cholesterol

The sum of the cholesterol carried across all lipoprotein particles, LDL included.

HDL Cholesterol

The high-density lipoprotein side of the panel, with a primer on lipoproteins themselves.

Triglycerides

The lipid panel's fat measurement, and one of the inputs to a calculated LDL.

ApoB

The test that counts cholesterol-carrying particles instead of weighing their cargo.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Is LDL cholesterol measured or calculated?

Both happen. Many labs estimate LDL cholesterol from the total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglyceride values measured in the same blood draw, an approach published by Friedewald and colleagues in 1972. Direct LDL assays also exist, and labs use them when the estimate becomes unreliable, such as when triglycerides are very high. Your lab report reflects whichever method your lab used.

Why does a cycle tracking app include LDL cholesterol?

LDL cholesterol shifts with hormonal life stages at the population level. In the SWAN study, LDL cholesterol rose sharply within the year surrounding the final menstrual period, a pattern the authors read as driven by menopause itself rather than ordinary aging. That makes it a lab value worth keeping on the same timeline as cycle and menopause data.

What's the difference between LDL cholesterol and ApoB?

An LDL cholesterol result reports the mass of cholesterol carried inside LDL particles. ApoB counts the particles themselves, since each cholesterol-carrying particle holds one apolipoprotein B molecule. They're related but distinct measurements, and a lipid panel can include both.
