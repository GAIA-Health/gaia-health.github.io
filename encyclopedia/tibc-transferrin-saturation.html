TIBC & Transferrin Saturation

Labs & Biomarkers · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) measures how much iron the proteins in your blood, mainly transferrin, can carry in total.[1] Transferrin saturation is the percentage of that carrying capacity actually occupied by iron.[1] Both come from a standard blood draw and are usually reported together on an iron panel alongside serum iron and ferritin.[5]

What TIBC & Transferrin Saturation Measure

Both tests are built around one protein. Transferrin is the blood's main iron carrier, a plasma protein that picks up iron and delivers it to tissues around the body.[2] TIBC measures the total amount of iron all that circulating transferrin could bind if every seat were filled. Because transferrin does almost all of the binding, TIBC in practice reflects how much transferrin is in the blood, with a small contribution from other proteins like albumin.[3]

Transferrin saturation answers a different question: how much of that capacity is in use right now. Labs calculate it from serum iron and TIBC and report it as a percentage.[1]

How They're Measured

Both values come from a standard venous blood draw, usually ordered together with serum iron and ferritin as an iron panel.[5] Each lab sets and reports its own reference interval with the result, and those intervals vary by lab and method.

Why People Track TIBC & Transferrin Saturation

Each piece of an iron panel answers its own question. Ferritin reflects the iron the body holds in storage. TIBC reflects the blood's capacity to transport iron. Transferrin saturation reflects how much of that capacity is loaded at the moment of the draw, and serum iron is the raw amount circulating right then. Clinicians and researchers read the pieces together because no single value tells the whole iron story on its own.[4]

What the Research Shows

Transferrin's role as the principal plasma transport protein for iron is long established and is the basis for both tests.[1][2]

A 2002 analysis in Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine confirmed that TIBC and measured transferrin concentration correlate well, while noting that TIBC methods can run slightly high because iron also binds other plasma proteins, mainly albumin.[3]

A 2016 review in Advances in Clinical Chemistry describes transferrin saturation as a useful body-iron biomarker precisely because it combines plasma iron with its transport protein in one index, while cautioning that inflammation and other settings limit what any single measurement can say.[4]

Related Terms

Serum Iron

The iron circulating in blood at the moment of the draw, one input to transferrin saturation.

Ferritin

The iron-storage protein, the "reserves" piece of the same iron panel.

Hemoglobin

The iron-containing protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are TIBC and transferrin saturation the same test?

No. They're two separate values that usually appear on the same iron panel. TIBC measures the blood's total capacity to bind iron, while transferrin saturation reports the percentage of that capacity that's actually carrying iron at the time of the draw.

What's the difference between TIBC and transferrin?

Transferrin is the protein itself, the main carrier that moves iron through blood. TIBC is a functional measurement of how much iron the blood can bind in total. Because transferrin does most of that binding, TIBC in practice reflects the amount of transferrin present, with a small contribution from other proteins like albumin.
