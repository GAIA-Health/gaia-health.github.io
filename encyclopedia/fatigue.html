Fatigue

Symptoms & Logged Metrics · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Fatigue is a feeling of tiredness or low energy that doesn't fully lift with rest. It has many possible causes. In cycle tracking, it's logged as a daily symptom because fatigue is one of the most commonly reported premenstrual symptoms in studies of healthy menstruating women.[1]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Fatigue Is

Fatigue is tiredness that goes beyond the ordinary sleepy-at-bedtime kind: low energy, heaviness, trouble getting going. Honest framing matters here. Fatigue has many possible causes, from sleep to workload to health conditions, and a tracking app can't tell you which one is yours. What tracking can show is timing. Fatigue appears on the standard list of premenstrual symptoms,[1] and in a survey of 156 healthy women, 53% reported fatigue premenstrually and 49% during menses.[2] Subjective sleep quality is also lowest around menses, even though the timing and composition of sleep stay relatively stable across the cycle in healthy women, aside from a minor decrease in REM sleep and an increase in spindle activity during the luteal phase.[3]

How It's Measured

There's no lab test for day-to-day fatigue. Studies and tracking apps measure it the same way: a self-rated daily score. Rated consistently over a few cycles, the scores show whether your tired days cluster in one phase, follow your sleep, or ignore both.

Why People Track Fatigue

Because "I'm always tired" is rarely true, and the pattern is the interesting part. A few months of daily ratings show whether tiredness lands on the same cycle days, follows short sleep, or has no pattern at all. Each of those answers is useful, and each points a conversation with a doctor in a different direction. Researchers also study links between iron stores and fatigue in menstruating women, including a randomised trial in non-anaemic women with unexplained tiredness.[5] See ferritin for what that marker measures.

What the Research Shows

Fatigue is one of the most commonly reported symptoms around menstruation. In healthy women it showed up premenstrually for 53% and during menses for 49%,[2] and it appears on the symptom list for premenstrual syndrome, which has a pooled worldwide prevalence of 47.8% among women of reproductive age.[1]

The performance data is more modest than the headlines. A meta-analysis of 78 studies found exercise performance might be trivially reduced in the early follicular phase, with small effect sizes and mostly low-quality evidence, and the authors explicitly recommended a personalised approach over blanket phase-based rules.[4]

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Is fatigue linked to the menstrual cycle?

Often, but not always. Fatigue is one of the commonly listed premenstrual symptoms,[1] and in a survey of healthy women 53% reported it premenstrually and 49% during menses.[2] Fatigue also has many causes unrelated to the cycle, which is why logging it daily is more informative than assuming.

Does cycle phase affect energy and exercise performance?

A meta-analysis of 78 studies found exercise performance might be trivially reduced in the early follicular phase, but the effects were small, the evidence quality mostly low, and the authors recommended a personalised approach because responses vary so much between individuals.[4]

Why log fatigue every day?

A daily 1-to-5 rating over a few months shows whether your tiredness follows a cycle pattern, tracks your sleep, or does neither. That dated record is more useful to you, and to a doctor, than trying to remember how tired you were three weeks ago.
