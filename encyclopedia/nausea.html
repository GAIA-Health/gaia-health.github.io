Nausea

Symptoms & Logged Metrics · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Nausea is the queasy, unsettled stomach sensation that can precede vomiting, produced by brain circuits that integrate signals from the gut, inner ear, cortex, and bloodstream.[1] In health tracking it's logged as a subjective symptom, recorded across everyday, cycle-related, and early-pregnancy contexts.

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Nausea Measures

Researchers describe nausea as a protective mechanism that warns the body away from things it shouldn't have swallowed.[1] The sensation is built in the brainstem's nucleus tractus solitarius, which receives input along four routes: the vestibular system of the inner ear, the cortex and limbic system, the area postrema (which samples the bloodstream), and vagal nerve signals from the gut.[1] That's why such different things, from motion to strong emotion to hormones, can all end in the same queasy feeling.

How It's Measured

By self-report. There's no lab test or wearable signal for nausea, so tracking means logging whether it showed up on a given day. What turns those single entries into something useful is the date attached to each one, because the pattern only appears once entries stack up across weeks.

Why People Track Nausea

Nausea turns up in more contexts than most logged symptoms. Some people notice it around their period: in a study of 156 healthy women, 17% reported nausea premenstrually and 14% during menses, and 73% reported at least one gastrointestinal symptom around their period.[2] Others log it during early pregnancy, where nausea and vomiting typically begin within weeks of a missed period and peak between weeks 10 and 16.[3]

Nausea on its own doesn't show whether someone is pregnant. It has too many everyday causes for that. A dated log is simply a record you can set next to other signs, and our guide Am I Pregnant? walks through what early signs can and can't tell you.

What the Research Shows

Nausea and vomiting of pregnancy is common worldwide. A meta-analysis pooling 79 studies from 13 countries put the overall rate at 69%, with individual studies ranging from 35% to 91%.[4]

The hormone most often studied in that context is hCG, because its peak roughly coincides with peak nausea. But the evidence is unsettled, and some studies find no relationship between hCG levels and how strong symptoms are.[3]

Outside pregnancy, gastrointestinal symptoms around menstruation are a normal population-level finding in healthy women, with nausea among the less frequent complaints compared with bloating and altered bowel habits.[2]

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Is nausea around a period common?

It's reported by a meaningful minority. In a study of 156 healthy women, 17% reported nausea in the premenstrual days and 14% during menses, and 73% reported at least one gastrointestinal symptom around their period.[2]

How common is nausea in pregnancy?

A meta-analysis pooling 79 studies from 13 countries estimated that 69% of pregnancies involve nausea and vomiting, with reported rates ranging from 35% to 91% across studies.[4]

Does logging nausea tell you whether you're pregnant?

No. Nausea shows up in many contexts, from cycle-related gastrointestinal changes to motion and illness, so a nausea log on its own doesn't indicate pregnancy. A dated record is simply useful context to bring to a pregnancy test or a doctor's visit.
