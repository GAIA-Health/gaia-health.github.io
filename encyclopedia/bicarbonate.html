Bicarbonate (CO2)

Labs & Biomarkers · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Bicarbonate is the main chemical buffer in blood, resisting large swings in pH as carbon dioxide is produced and cleared.[1] On a lab panel it's reported as total CO2, measured from a standard blood draw as part of a basic metabolic or electrolyte panel.[3] The lungs and kidneys regulate it on different timescales.[1]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Bicarbonate Measures

Cellular metabolism constantly produces carbon dioxide, which combines with water to form carbonic acid, and carbonic acid then splits into bicarbonate and a hydrogen ion, a reaction sped up by the enzyme carbonic anhydrase.[1] That equilibrium is one of several buffer systems in the body, and it works by shifting toward whichever side of the reaction the current pH calls for, soaking up excess acid or releasing more of it as needed.[1] Most of the carbon dioxide carried in blood, roughly 80 percent, travels in this bicarbonate form rather than as dissolved gas or bound to hemoglobin, which is why a blood test for "CO2" is really reading bicarbonate.[2]

Two organs keep the buffer in balance on different clocks. The lungs adjust how much CO2 gets exhaled, which can shift blood pH within minutes to hours. The kidneys work over days, adjusting how much bicarbonate they reabsorb versus how many fixed acids they excrete.[1]

How It's Measured

Bicarbonate is measured from a standard venous blood draw and reported in milliequivalents per liter, usually under the label "CO2" or "total CO2" rather than "bicarbonate" itself. It's one of the eight values on a basic metabolic panel and one of the electrolytes on an electrolyte panel, tests that group it with sodium, potassium, and chloride specifically because the four are read together to assess fluid and acid-base status.[3][4]

Why People Track Bicarbonate

Bicarbonate appears on nearly every routine blood panel as one line among several, so a bicarbonate history builds up for anyone who uploads lab results, whether or not that was the point. It's rarely meaningful in isolation. Its value comes from sitting next to sodium, potassium, and chloride on the same draw, since together the four give a fuller picture of fluid and acid-base status than any single value on its own.[3]

What the Research Shows

The bicarbonate buffer system's chemistry, and the separate roles the lungs and kidneys play in regulating it, are documented in the StatPearls review of acid-base physiology, including the minutes-to-hours pace of respiratory adjustment versus the multi-day pace of renal adjustment.[1]

The StatPearls review of carbon dioxide transport describes how CO2 actually moves through blood: about 10 percent stays dissolved and another 10 percent binds to hemoglobin as carbaminohemoglobin. The remaining majority, commonly cited as roughly 80 percent, travels as bicarbonate, the dominant form.[2]

MedlinePlus describes the basic metabolic panel's eight components as a set built to read fluid balance, metabolism, and kidney function together, including the body's balance of acids and bases, which is the role bicarbonate specifically plays among the panel's electrolytes.[3]

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Does Go Go Gaia run bicarbonate blood tests?

No. Go Go Gaia doesn't draw blood or run labs. You get a bicarbonate, or total CO2, result from bloodwork ordered through a clinician or lab service, then upload it to your Go Go Gaia account so it's stored and charted alongside your other tracked data.

Is bicarbonate the same thing as CO2 on a lab report?

On a routine blood panel, yes, practically speaking. The test is usually labeled "CO2" or "total CO2," but most of the carbon dioxide it captures exists in blood as bicarbonate rather than as dissolved gas, so the result is read as a measure of bicarbonate.

Why does bicarbonate show up on a basic metabolic panel?

A basic metabolic panel is built to read fluid balance, kidney function, and acid-base status together. Bicarbonate is the electrolyte on that panel tied specifically to acid-base balance, alongside sodium, potassium, and chloride.
