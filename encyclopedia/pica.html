Pica & Ice Craving

Symptoms & Logged Metrics · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Pica is a persistent urge to eat or chew items with no nutritional value.[1] Pagophagia, an urge specifically for ice, is its most studied form, and research consistently documents it in a meaningful share of people with iron-deficiency anemia.[2]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Pica & Ice Craving Describe

Pica covers a range of non-food cravings, but ice is by far the most studied and most commonly reported item. A 2023 scoping review of the research literature reports that pagophagia is common in the United States, affecting around 25% of iron-deficient patients across the studies it reviewed, and cites a separate study in which ice was the single most common pica item, reported by 87.3% of the patients with pica in that sample.[1] A StatPearls clinical reference on iron-deficiency anemia lists pica specifically among the features it describes as more particular to iron deficiency than to anemia generally, alongside hair loss and decreased attention.[2]

The association between pica and iron deficiency has been documented in research since at least 1968, when a study of patients with pagophagia and iron-deficiency anemia first described the pattern in detail.[3] Even so, researchers haven't settled on a single confirmed mechanism. The 2023 scoping review describes two competing adaptive hypotheses that have been proposed in the literature, one centered on correcting a nutrient deficiency and another on protection from parasites or toxins, and concludes plainly that there's no definite conclusion on the cause of pica.[1]

How It's Logged

There's no lab test for pica or an ice craving. It's a logged symptom, recorded as a yes/no or a note for the day, the same way any other craving gets tracked.

Why People Track Pica & Ice Craving

An urge to chew ice can feel like an odd, isolated habit until it's logged over time and shows up as a pattern rather than a one-off. Some people who get iron panels drawn chart these logs against lab results like ferritin to see how closely the two move together. Pica is tracked as its own symptom, separate from food cravings, since it involves items with no nutritional value rather than a pull toward a specific food.

What the Research Shows

The 2023 scoping review, published in Cureus, examined case studies of pica resolving with iron treatment and found that all 20 of the articles it reviewed reported symptom resolution after iron repletion, with some patients reporting the craving stopped within five to eight days of starting treatment.[1] That's a pattern the review documents across the cases it examined, not a claim about how quickly or reliably it resolves for any one person.

The classic 1968 paper that first documented the pagophagia-iron deficiency link, published in Annals of Internal Medicine, remains one of the most cited studies in this area of research.[3]

More recent research has continued to document the pattern in specific populations. A 2019 study looked at pagophagia specifically in men with iron-deficiency anemia,[4] and a 2025 paper examined possible brain effects tied to iron-deficiency-related pagophagia,[5] both adding to a research base that treats the ice-iron connection as well established even without a single agreed mechanism.

Related Terms

Iron-Deficiency Anemia

The condition most consistently studied alongside pica and pagophagia.

Ferritin

The iron-storage marker most often tracked alongside iron-deficiency anemia.

Food Cravings

A pull toward a specific food, tracked separately from non-food pica cravings.

Hemoglobin

The oxygen-carrying protein whose shortfall defines anemia, the condition pica research is centered on.
