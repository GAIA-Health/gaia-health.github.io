Fertile Window

Cycle & Fertility · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

The fertile window is the roughly six-day stretch of the menstrual cycle when intercourse can lead to conception, ending on the day of ovulation.[1] Sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for days while the egg lives about a day, so the window sits almost entirely before ovulation, not after it.[2]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What the Fertile Window Measures

The six-day figure comes from a prospective study that followed 221 women across 625 cycles while estimating ovulation from urinary hormones. Conception occurred only when intercourse took place during a six-day period ending on the estimated day of ovulation.[1] The probability of conception wasn't flat across those days either. It ran from 0.10 five days before ovulation up to 0.33 on ovulation day itself.[1]

Why It's Six Days

Two lifespans set the window's shape. Sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for roughly three to five days, which is what opens the window days before the egg exists.[2] The egg itself lives about one to two days after ovulation, which is what closes it.[2] Long sperm survival was the exception in practice: only 6% of the pregnancies in the timing study could be firmly attributed to sperm three or more days old.[1]

Why People Track the Fertile Window

Because its position moves. A follow-up BMJ study by the same research group found the fertile window fell entirely within days 10 to 17, the range clinical guidelines used to quote, in only about 30% of women.[3] On every day between days 6 and 21, at least 10% of women were in their fertile window.[3] The window tracks ovulation day, and ovulation day varies. A study of 612,613 ovulatory cycles found the follicular phase averaged 16.9 days with a 95% range of 10 to 30 days.[4]

That's why people estimate the window from body signs rather than the calendar: cervical mucus turning clear and stretchy, a rising E3G reading, or a positive ovulation test catching the LH surge. Each of those entries covers its own signal.

What the Research Shows

The window's timing is less predictable than its length. The BMJ study concluded that the timing of the fertile window can be highly unpredictable even in women whose cycles are usually regular.[3]

Regular cycles don't pin it down because cycle-length averages hide follicular-phase variation. The 612,613-cycle study reported a mean cycle length of 29.3 days but wide spread in when ovulation fell, and its authors stress tracking physiological signs like basal body temperature rather than cycle length alone to identify the fertile period.[4]

Related Terms

Ovulation

The egg-release event the fertile window ends on.

Ovulation Test (OPK)

The urine test that flags the late, highest-probability part of the window.

Cervical Mucus

The sign that shifts through the fertile window as estrogen rises.

LH Surge

The hormone spike that marks the window's final stretch before ovulation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

How many days long is the fertile window?

About six days. In a prospective study of 221 women across 625 cycles, conception occurred only when intercourse took place during a six-day period ending on the estimated day of ovulation.[1] The length comes from biology: sperm can survive roughly three to five days, while the egg lives about one to two days.[2]

Does the fertile window fall on the same cycle days for everyone?

No. A prospective BMJ study found the fertile window fell entirely within days 10 to 17 in only about 30% of women, and on every day between days 6 and 21 at least 10% of women were in their fertile window.[3] Its position tracks ovulation day, which varies widely between people and cycles.[4]

Why does the fertile window end on ovulation day?

Because the egg is short-lived. Estimates put its lifespan at about one to two days after release, and in the classic conception-timing study no conceptions came from intercourse after the estimated ovulation day.[1][2] Sperm survival is what opens the window early, days before the egg exists.
