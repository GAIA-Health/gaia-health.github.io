Cyclical Low Back Pain

Symptoms & Logged Metrics · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Cyclical low back pain is lower-back pain that shows up in a pattern tied to the menstrual cycle, most often right before or during a period.[1] It's driven by the same prostaglandins that trigger uterine contractions and cramps, and that pain can radiate from the pelvis to the lumbar region and upper legs.[1]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Cyclical Low Back Pain Is

Menstrual cramps aren't confined to the lower abdomen. Prostaglandins released as the uterine lining breaks down cause the uterus to contract, and prostaglandin F2α in particular drives high-amplitude contractions and reduced blood flow to the uterine muscle.[1] Research on dysmenorrhea describes that pain as located in the midline of the pelvis, radiating to the lumbar region of the back and to the upper legs.[1] That's why back pain so often shows up alongside cramps rather than as a separate complaint. The two share a cause, they just aren't always felt with the same intensity in the same place.

How It's Logged

There's no lab test or wearable reading for cyclical back pain. It's a logged symptom, recorded on the days it happens and rated by how much it affects the day. Logged over several cycles, it turns a symptom that's easy to write off as "just a bad day" into a pattern you can actually see.

Why People Track It

Back pain that shows up the same few days of every cycle looks different from back pain that doesn't follow any pattern at all, and the only way to tell the two apart is to log it against cycle day. Some people notice their back pain arrives a day or two before cramps do, others notice it fades faster than cramps once flow starts. Logging both together, alongside cramps and where you are in your menstrual phase, shows whether back pain is riding the same wave as the rest of your period symptoms or showing up on its own timeline.

It also matters for anyone deciding whether a new ache is worth mentioning to a doctor. A log that shows three cycles of back pain starting on day 26 every time is a more useful starting point than trying to remember it from memory.

What the Research Shows

A study of 192 women found that 95% reported pain during menstruation, and among those with period pain, 72% also reported back pain, making it one of the most common accompanying symptoms alongside bloating and digestive changes.[2]

Pregnancy-related low back pain is studied as its own, separate area of research, with a different mechanism than menstrual back pain. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 28 studies covering nearly 13,000 pregnant women found an overall prevalence of about 40.5%, rising from 28.3% in the first trimester to 47.8% in the third.[3] Researchers attribute that pain mainly to weight gain shifting the body's center of gravity forward and the postural changes that follow, along with ligament laxity that some studies tie to rising relaxin and others do not, rather than to the prostaglandin pathway behind menstrual pain.[4]

Related Terms

Cramps (Dysmenorrhea)

The lower-abdominal pain that shares the same prostaglandin-driven cause as cyclical back pain.

Bloating

Another symptom that commonly clusters with cramps and back pain around a period.

Menstrual Phase

The bleeding phase of the cycle when prostaglandin-driven pain is usually strongest.

Period Flow

Flow logged alongside back pain often shows whether the two rise and fall together.

Pelvic Pain

Discomfort in the lower abdomen or pelvis that can radiate toward the same lower-back region as cyclical back pain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Why does my back hurt during my period?

Research points to prostaglandins, the same compounds that cause uterine contractions and cramps, as the driver. Elevated prostaglandin levels intensify uterine contractions, and the pain that results can radiate from the pelvis to the lumbar region of the back and the upper legs.[1]

Is back pain during pregnancy the same thing as period-related back pain?

No, researchers study them separately. Pregnancy-related low back pain is generally attributed to postural changes, weight gain shifting the body's center of gravity, and ligament laxity, with relaxin's role still debated, a different mechanism than the prostaglandin-driven pain linked to menstruation.[4]

How common is back pain during your period?

In a study of 192 women, 95% reported pain during menstruation, and 72% of those with period pain also reported back pain as one of their symptoms.[2]
