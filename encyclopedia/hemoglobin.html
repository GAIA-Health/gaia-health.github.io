Hemoglobin

Labs & Biomarkers · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Hemoglobin is the iron-containing protein inside red blood cells that picks up oxygen in the lungs and carries it to the rest of the body.[1] A hemoglobin blood test reports how much of this protein is present per volume of blood, and it's a standard part of a complete blood count (CBC).[2]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Hemoglobin Measures

Each hemoglobin molecule is built from four protein subunits, and each subunit holds a heme group with an iron atom at its center. Oxygen binds reversibly to that iron, which is what lets red blood cells load oxygen in the lungs and release it in the tissues.[1] The lab value itself is a concentration: how many grams of hemoglobin are present per deciliter of blood.[7] So the test describes the blood's oxygen-carrying protein supply at the moment of the draw, not oxygen levels directly.

How It's Measured

Hemoglobin is measured from a standard venous blood draw, almost always as one line of a complete blood count.[3] The CBC reads it alongside its neighbors: hematocrit, the red blood cell count, and indices like MCV that describe the size of those cells.[2] Each value covers a different angle on the same red-cell picture, which is why labs report them together. Every result comes with the lab's own reference interval, and that interval can vary by lab and method.

Why People Track Hemoglobin

The body needs iron to build hemoglobin, and menstruation is a recurring, measurable form of blood loss. That connection shows up in population data: in a 2024 study of 473 premenopausal blood donors, menstrual blood loss independently predicted both hemoglobin and ferritin levels.[4] For someone who already logs period flow, a hemoglobin result is one more number tied to a process their tracker records anyway. Ferritin covers the storage side of that same iron story, so the two values are often studied together.

What the Research Shows

Menstrual blood loss was an independent determinant of hemoglobin and ferritin levels in a cohort of 473 premenopausal blood donors, alongside factors like time since a person's last donation.[4]

Hemoglobin also has a documented afterlife. When red blood cells reach the end of their lifespan, roughly 80% of the body's bilirubin is produced from the breakdown of their hemoglobin.[5] A bilirubin value on a metabolic panel is, in large part, retired hemoglobin.

Glucose in the bloodstream attaches to hemoglobin as red blood cells circulate, and because those cells live about three months, the glycated fraction reflects average glucose over roughly that window. That fraction is reported as its own test, HbA1c.[6]

Related Terms

Hematocrit

The share of blood volume made up of red blood cells, read beside hemoglobin on a CBC.

Red Blood Cells

The cells that carry hemoglobin, counted per volume of blood on the same panel.

MCV

The average size of red blood cells, another line of the CBC.

Ferritin

The iron-storage protein, the reserve side of the iron picture hemoglobin draws on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Go Go Gaia measure hemoglobin?

No. Go Go Gaia doesn't draw blood or run labs. You get a hemoglobin result from a blood draw ordered through a clinician or lab service, then upload it to your Go Go Gaia account so it's stored and charted alongside your other tracked data.

How is hemoglobin different from ferritin?

Hemoglobin is the working protein that carries oxygen inside red blood cells, while ferritin is the protein that holds iron in storage inside cells. They sit at different points in the same iron story, which is why studies of iron status in people who menstruate often measure both together.

What does hemoglobin have to do with HbA1c?

HbA1c is hemoglobin that glucose has attached to. Because red blood cells live about three months, the share of hemoglobin carrying glucose reflects average blood glucose over roughly that window. It's the same protein measured from a different angle.
