Appetite Changes

Symptoms & Logged Metrics · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Appetite changes are shifts in hunger and how much someone eats compared to their usual pattern.[1] Across the menstrual cycle, research documents a dip in energy intake around ovulation and a rise through the luteal phase, tracking with shifting estradiol and progesterone.[2]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Appetite Changes Describe

Appetite change is a broader category than a single food craving. It covers feeling hungrier or less hungry than usual overall, eating more or less at meals, or noticing that food interest itself has shifted. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis pooling 15 datasets and 330 participants found average daily energy intake was significantly higher in the luteal phase than the follicular phase, a pooled effect the authors describe as consistent enough across the literature to treat as a real cycle effect rather than noise. The ovulatory phase itself was excluded from that analysis for lack of available intake data.[1]

An earlier review of menstrual-cycle appetite research described a periovulatory dip in intake and a luteal-phase peak, and reported that carbohydrate cravings specifically are more often reported in the premenstrual window, especially by people who also report other premenstrual symptoms.[2] Progesterone is one of the hormones studied here: a 2023 study using daily symptom ratings found mid-luteal progesterone was inversely associated with the intensity of food cravings.[3]

How It's Logged

There's no lab test for appetite change. It's a logged symptom, recorded as a rating or note for the day, often alongside nutrition entries so the two can be compared side by side.

Why People Track Appetite Changes

Hunger that seems to swing for no reason is easier to make sense of once it's logged against cycle phase. Pairing an appetite log with calorie and nutrition entries also shows whether a hungrier day lines up with lower intake earlier in the day, rather than with cycle phase alone. Appetite changes and food cravings are related but distinct: a craving is a pull toward one specific food, while appetite change is a broader shift in hunger overall.

What the Research Shows

The 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis, published in Nutrition Reviews, pooled 15 datasets and 330 participants and found a statistically significant pooled difference in intake between the luteal and follicular phases (SMD 0.69, P = .039), with an average difference of about 168 kilocalories a day. Of the 15 datasets, 8 found a statistically significant luteal-phase increase, averaging about 275 kilocalories a day. The rest found no significant difference, and one found higher intake in the follicular phase instead. The review did not analyze the ovulatory phase separately, since intake data for it was too limited.[1]

A 1997 review of menstrual-cycle appetite control found the same broad pattern and noted that premenstrual carbohydrate cravings are reported more often among people who also report other premenstrual symptoms, suggesting appetite shifts don't happen in isolation from mood and other cycle-linked symptoms.[2]

A 2023 study using daily symptom ratings across two to three cycles in 37 women found mid-luteal progesterone was inversely associated with food-craving intensity, adjusted for BMI, while estradiol showed no significant effect in the same model, pointing to progesterone as a specific hormone worth watching in appetite research.[3]

Related Terms

Food Cravings

A pull toward one specific food, related to but distinct from broader appetite change.

Calories

Logged energy intake, the measured counterpart to a felt sense of appetite change.

Luteal Phase

The post-ovulation phase where research most consistently documents higher energy intake.

Blood Glucose

The circulating sugar level whose swings can influence how hunger feels day to day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

Does appetite really change across the menstrual cycle?

Research supports it. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found energy intake significantly higher in the luteal phase than the follicular phase, a pattern reported consistently enough across studies to describe as a real cycle-linked effect.[1] An earlier review also found a periovulatory dip in intake alongside that luteal-phase rise.[2]

Which cycle phase has the biggest appetite increase?

The luteal phase, the stretch after ovulation and before a period, is where research most consistently documents higher energy intake.[1] Progesterone rises through this phase, and earlier research found progesterone levels inversely associated with the intensity of food cravings specifically.[3]

How is appetite change logged if it's not a lab test?

It's logged as a symptom, recorded as a rating or note for the day. Paired with nutrition logs and cycle-phase data, that turns a feeling of being hungrier than usual into a pattern you can actually look back on.
