Painful Intercourse (Dyspareunia)

Symptoms & Logged Metrics · Reviewed August 2026

Definition

Dyspareunia is persistent or recurrent genital pain that occurs before, during, or after sexual intercourse.[1] Estimates of how common it is vary widely by population and definition, from about 8% to 56% of women worldwide.[1]

Educational content, not medical advice. For personal or fertility concerns, please consult your doctor.

What Painful Intercourse Is

Dyspareunia is now classified under the broader term genitopelvic pain-penetration disorder, which covers pain during penetration alongside pelvic floor muscle tension and avoidance behaviors that can develop around it.[1] Pain can be superficial, near the vaginal opening, or deep, felt further inside with penetration, and research ties different patterns of pain to different underlying causes.[1]

How It's Logged

There's no device or lab test for painful intercourse. It's logged as a private, dated symptom entry, a rating of whether and how much it hurt, which turns something rarely said out loud into a record instead of a memory.

What the Research Shows

Endometriosis is a leading, well-studied cause of deep dyspareunia. In one study of patients with surgically diagnosed endometriosis, almost 45% reported deep pain during intercourse, and deep dyspareunia was found to correlate with bladder and pelvic floor tenderness.[1]

Genitourinary syndrome of menopause is described as the most frequent cause of dyspareunia in postmenopausal women, affecting an estimated 40% to 84% of that group.[1] Research on genitourinary syndrome of menopause ties this to the roughly 95% drop in estrogen production after menopause, which thins the vaginal mucosa and makes intercourse increasingly painful for many, alongside progressive changes in the labia and vaginal opening.[2]

The postpartum period shows its own pattern. Research describes dyspareunia in roughly 35% of women during the first year after childbirth, with about 42% reporting pain specifically in the 2 to 6 month window, alongside associations with pelvic floor dysfunction and postpartum tissue trauma.[1]

Related Terms

Endometriosis

The condition most closely tied to deep pain during intercourse.

Vaginal Dryness

A closely related symptom that shares the same estrogen-driven mechanism during menopause.

Pelvic Pain

A broader pain category that can overlap with, but isn't limited to, pain during intercourse.

Perimenopause

The transition during which genitourinary syndrome of menopause typically begins to develop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources. Not medical advice. For personal concerns, please consult your doctor.

How common is painful intercourse?

Estimates vary widely by population and definition, ranging from about 8% to 56% of women globally and 7% to 46% in the United States.[1] It's markedly more common in specific groups, affecting an estimated 40% to 84% of postmenopausal women with genitourinary syndrome of menopause.[1]

Is painful intercourse linked to endometriosis?

Yes. Endometriosis is a well-studied cause of dyspareunia, particularly deep pain during intercourse. In one study of patients with surgically diagnosed endometriosis, almost 45% reported deep dyspareunia.[1]

Why does menopause cause painful intercourse?

Genitourinary syndrome of menopause is described as the most frequent cause of dyspareunia in postmenopausal women.[1] Research ties it to falling estrogen, which thins and reduces the elasticity of vaginal tissue, with one estimate putting the associated drop in estrogen production at around 95%.[2]
