What should a perimenopause tracking app actually do?

Tracking perimenopause is different from tracking a regular cycle. Here's what to look for in an app:

Irregular Cycle Handling

This is the biggest deal-breaker. During perimenopause, periods may become irregular: longer, shorter, heavier, lighter or skipped, sometimes within the same year. An app that panics when you skip a period or insists on predicting your next one based on averages won't cut it. You need something that tracks what's actually happening, not what a 28-day template says should be happening.

Symptom Breadth

Hot flashes get all the attention, but perimenopause involves a long list of possible symptoms. Night sweats, brain fog, mood changes, sleep disruption, vaginal dryness, joint pain, weight changes, anxiety, heart palpitations, and more. A good app lets you track a wide range of these, not just the obvious ones.

HRT and Medication Tracking

Some women start hormone replacement therapy (HRT) during perimenopause. Noting when you started, what you were prescribed, and how your symptoms respond over time gives you something to discuss with your doctor. The same goes for any supplements you take.

Pattern Recognition Over Time

Perimenopause symptoms change over months and years, not days. An app that shows you weekly or daily snapshots won't reveal the bigger picture. You need something that helps you spot trends over longer periods: "I logged 8 hot flashes a week in January and 3 a week in March."

Data for Doctor Appointments

Some women find perimenopause symptoms hard to get taken seriously at appointments. Walking in with tracked data, specific symptom counts, patterns over time, and medication notes gives you something concrete to talk through with your clinician.

Wearable Integration

Wearables can capture sleep, temperature and heart rate data automatically. These are general wellness readings, not a way to diagnose perimenopause or any condition. If you already wear an Apple Watch, Oura Ring, or Garmin, an app that pulls in that data saves you from manually logging things you're already tracking.

How do these apps handle your data?

Perimenopause data is deeply personal: symptom patterns, HRT use, mood changes, fertility status. Here's what we could verify about each app:

  • Veya: its App Store listing states "No ads. No selling your data."
  • Clue: headquartered in Berlin, and its listing says data is protected by European privacy law (GDPR).
  • Balance and Health & Her: both are UK-based companies, so UK data protection law applies. We didn't find public data-sharing controversies for either, but we haven't audited their policies.
  • Caria and Perry: we didn't find public data-sharing controversies, but we haven't audited their policies. Read each privacy policy before you log health data.
  • Flo has a documented privacy history. In 2021 the FTC finalized an order after alleging that Flo shared health data with outside analytics firms, including Facebook and Google, after promising to keep it private. Separately, in the class action Frasco v. Flo Health, $59.5 million in proposed settlements with Flo, Google and Flurry received preliminary approval in April 2026, and the court-approved notice sets the final approval hearing for October 29, 2026. Meta did not settle, and a jury found Meta liable on August 1, 2025. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, per its App Store listing.
  • Midday: we couldn't find a current listing or privacy policy to review, so we can't say how it handles data.

How do you get the most out of perimenopause tracking?

Whichever app you choose, here's how to make it actually useful:

  1. Give it a few months. Perimenopause patterns emerge slowly, so a couple of weeks of logs rarely shows much. Consistency matters more than detail.
  2. Log more than hot flashes. Mood, sleep, brain fog, joint pain, anxiety: they all matter. The symptoms you think are unrelated may turn out to be connected.
  3. Note your triggers. Caffeine, alcohol, stress, poor sleep. Tracking what happened BEFORE a symptom is just as important as logging the symptom itself.
  4. Track HRT and supplements if you're taking them. This is how you see whether they're actually helping over time. "I feel better" is less specific than "my weekly hot flash count dropped from 12 to 4 after I started the treatment my doctor prescribed."
  5. Bring your data to your doctor. Several months of tracked symptoms, patterns, and medication notes gives your healthcare provider something concrete to work with.
  6. Be patient. The NHS says symptoms usually last 7 to 9 years, sometimes longer. Your symptoms will change. Your tracking needs will change. That's normal. Start simple and adjust as you go.

Pro Tip

If you're not sure which app to start with, pick one that covers your biggest need. If you want symptom management tools, try Health & Her or Caria. If you want to see how lifestyle lines up with your symptoms, try an app with correlation features. If community matters most, try Perry. You can always switch later.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I look for in a perimenopause tracking app?

Look for an app that handles irregular or missing cycles without breaking, tracks a wide range of perimenopause symptoms (not just hot flashes), supports HRT or medication tracking, shows patterns over months (not just days), and lets you export data for doctor appointments. Wearable integration is a bonus for capturing temperature shifts and sleep disruption automatically.

Can period tracker apps handle perimenopause?

Some can, but results vary. Standard period trackers predict your next cycle based on past regularity, and during perimenopause cycles can get longer, shorter or skip. Clue and Flo both offer perimenopause features (Clue has a dedicated Perimenopause mode; Flo has Flo for Perimenopause). Others, like Balance and Health & Her, were built for the menopause transition.

Is there a free perimenopause app?

Yes, several are free to download. Health & Her has no in-app purchases listed on the App Store and includes CBT exercises, pelvic floor training, and meditation alongside symptom tracking. Balance offers a free tier with symptom tracking and educational content. Veya's core tracking is free, including perimenopause symptom tracking, nutrition logging, fitness tracking, and wearable integration; Pro adds Ask Veya, doctor-report export and unlimited habits and symptoms. Perry is free to download with community access.

What's the difference between a menopause app and a period tracker?

Period trackers focus on predicting your next cycle and fertile window. Menopause apps focus on tracking symptoms like hot flashes, night sweats, brain fog, and mood changes. They also often include HRT tracking, educational content about hormone changes, and tools for doctor conversations. During perimenopause, you may need features from both categories, since your period is still happening (unpredictably) while menopause symptoms are starting.

How can tracking help me manage perimenopause symptoms?

Tracking can reveal patterns you'd miss on your own. You might notice that your hot flashes cluster after alcohol, that your brain fog eases on days you exercise, or that a supplement seems to line up with fewer symptoms. This data can be useful at doctor appointments, since it gives you something specific to talk through. A few months of consistent tracking gives both you and your healthcare provider more to work with.

Should I track perimenopause with a wearable?

It can help. Wearables automatically capture sleep, temperature and heart rate data, and sleep disruption is hard to self-report accurately. These are general wellness readings, not a way to diagnose perimenopause or any condition. If you already own an Apple Watch, Oura Ring, or Garmin, look for an app that syncs with your device so you don't have to log that data manually.

Flo's main advantage is familiarity if you already use it, not menopause-specific depth.

It depends what you need. Flo's perimenopause features are newer (announced July 2025) and sit inside the app most people already know, which is the main reason to stay with it. Apps built specifically for the menopause transition, such as Balance and Health & Her, offer symptom-management tools and menopause-focused content.
