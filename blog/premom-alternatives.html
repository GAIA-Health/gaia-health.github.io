Premom Alternatives: Ovulation Tracking Apps for 2026

For a privacy-minded mainstream tracker, try Clue. For an FDA-cleared contraception method, Natural Cycles. For LH logging beside mood and sleep, Veya.

Why do people look for Premom alternatives?

Two different groups end up here. The first is happy with Premom's strip-scanning feature but wants a broader app once they're past the daily OPK-testing phase, since Premom's symptom tracking, content, and non-fertility features are thinner than a general cycle tracker's.

The second group read the news. In May 2023, the FTC announced a proposed order alleging Premom's developer had shared sensitive health data with third parties, including China-based analytics firms, without telling users, and "is Premom safe" has stayed a live question ever since. That's a reasonable prompt to look around, and it doesn't mean Premom is a bad app. The question is whether the company's data practices, then and now, are something you're comfortable with.

Both are legitimate reasons to read this guide, and neither one requires you to leave Premom if it's working for you.

What did the FTC's 2023 order against Premom say?

Here's the factual record, with dates, so you can decide for yourself what it means for you.

May 17, 2023: The FTC announced that Premom's developer, Illinois-based Easy Healthcare Corporation, shared users' sensitive health data with AppsFlyer and Google, and shared social media account information, precise geolocation and device and Wi-Fi identifiers with two China-based analytics firms, Umeng and Jiguang, through SDKs built into the app. The FTC said Premom failed to notify users, violating the Health Breach Notification Rule.

The proposed order: Easy Healthcare would pay a $100,000 civil penalty and a total of $100,000 to Connecticut, the District of Columbia and Oregon. It would be permanently barred from sharing users' personal health data with third parties for advertising, would need consent before sharing health data for other purposes, and must seek deletion of data already shared. The FTC's announcement said the order needed federal court approval to take effect.

What it doesn't cover: The FTC's announcement concerned background data sharing, not the strip-scanning feature. Read Premom's current privacy policy yourself if this is a deciding factor for you.

When does staying with Premom make sense?

Staying is a legitimate choice, and for a lot of users it's the right one. Here's where Premom earns a real recommendation.

  • Strip scanning is your main reason for using the app. No alternative here photographs and quantifies an OPK strip the way Premom does. If that's the feature you actually need, switching means giving it up.
  • Your cycle is irregular. Premom's same-day hormone reading doesn't depend on predictable cycle history the way calendar-based apps do.
  • You value the TTC-specific community. Premom's forums, including PCOS-focused discussion, are a more targeted fit for some users than a general cycle-tracking community.
  • You've read the current privacy policy and you're comfortable with it. The company has made real, verifiable changes since 2023, and reasonable people land in different places on how much that history matters to them going forward.

If most of that describes you, there's nothing here that should push you off Premom. The rest of this guide is for everyone else.

The Alternatives Compared

Here are four apps matched to different reasons for looking around, in no particular order of quality. Each profile covers features, strengths, honest limitations, and price.

For broad tracking and a very large user base: Flo

Best if you want: A widely used period tracker with symptom, pregnancy and perimenopause tracking and about 2 million App Store ratings (as of October 2026), alongside manual OPK logging.

Key Features

  • Cycle tracking with calendar and algorithm-based predictions
  • Manual LH test and basal body temperature logging: you enter your own result, with no strip photography
  • Daily insights and expert videos in Premium
  • Secret Chats (anonymous community discussions), listed as a Premium feature
  • A pregnancy mode with weekly development updates

Strengths

  • Secret Chats for talking with other users (Premium)
  • Daily insights and expert videos in Premium
  • A pregnancy mode, useful once you're past the TTC stage
  • Cross-platform (iOS and Android)

Limitations

  • No strip scanning. You read and enter your own OPK result
  • Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing health data with marketing and analytics firms including Facebook and Google. A related class action against Flo, Google and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final approval hearing on October 29, 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, and Mozilla's July 2026 review scored Flo 7 out of 10.
  • The Health Assistant, Secret Chats and daily insights sit behind Flo Premium

Who Should Choose This

  • Feature depth and a large user base matter more to you than strip scanning
  • You want a pregnancy mode once you conceive
  • You're comfortable manually entering your own OPK results

Pricing: Free to download; Flo Premium is a paid subscription. Its App Store listing shows several price options without labeling monthly or annual, so check the current price in the app (as of October 2026).

Download: Available on iOS and Android

For a privacy-minded mainstream tracker: Clue

For an FDA-authorized contraception method: Natural Cycles

Best if you want: A fertility app with a specific regulatory clearance behind it, built around a temperature-based algorithm rather than LH strips.

Natural Cycles is FDA-authorized as a software application for contraception (FDA De Novo review DEN170052), and its App Store listing states 93% effectiveness with typical use. That belongs specifically to Natural Cycles' temperature-based method, not to fertility or ovulation trackers as a category, and it's worth reading in that narrow context rather than as a general statement about tracking apps.

Key Features

  • Daily basal body temperature logging (manual or via a compatible thermometer or wearable)
  • An algorithm that determines fertile and non-fertile days from your temperature pattern
  • A fertility-awareness algorithm built on temperature and cycle data
  • FDA authorization for contraception, under its own specific claims

Strengths

  • FDA authorization for contraception, a regulatory bar the other apps here have not described
  • Sponsor is Natural Cycles Nordic AB of Stockholm, per the FDA review
  • Works whether you're trying to conceive or trying not to, using the same underlying method

Limitations

  • No OPK strip scanning, and the listing doesn't describe LH test logging
  • Requires daily temperature discipline, which is a bigger habit shift than scanning a strip
  • Subscription-based, with monthly and annual plans
  • Effectiveness claims are specific to Natural Cycles' own clearance and shouldn't be generalized to other apps, including this one

Who Should Choose This

  • You want an FDA-authorized method, not just a logging tool
  • You're willing to take your temperature daily
  • Regulatory clearance matters more to you than strip-scanning convenience

Pricing: Subscription-based. The App Store listing shows $21.99 monthly and $149.99 annual (as of October 2026); check current pricing in the app.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

How do you move your data off Premom?

If you've decided to switch, do it in this order: get your data out, set up the new app, then delete. Here's the practical sequence.

  1. Check the app for an export option first. Look under Settings for a data export or a share/save feature for your cycle and test history. If you don't see one, contact support directly and ask for a copy of your logged history before you delete anything.
  2. Write down your key numbers in the meantime. Record your last several period start dates and your most recent LH readings (including any peak days) by hand. That's a backup you control regardless of what the app's export tools support.
  3. Set up the new app before deleting the old one. Enter your last 2 or 3 period start dates and any recent test results by hand. That gives any tracker a working baseline immediately, and most need about 2 cycles of fresh logging to calibrate fully.
  4. Delete your account when you're ready. Go to the app's account settings and confirm deletion. If you pay for Premom Premium through the App Store or Google Play, deleting your account doesn't automatically cancel the billing, so cancel that separately in your phone's subscription settings.

Be honest with yourself about what you'll miss. You lose the camera-based strip scanning, which no alternative here replicates exactly. You lose the TTC-specific community if you used it. And your new app starts with a thin history, so predictions take a couple of cycles to settle in. None of that is a reason to stay if you want to go. It's just better to know before you delete than after.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Premom safe to use in 2026?

In May 2023, the FTC announced that Premom's developer, Easy Healthcare Corporation, shared users' health data and device information with third parties, including AppsFlyer, Google and two China-based analytics firms, without notifying users. The proposed order included a $100,000 civil penalty, $100,000 to three states, a bar on sharing health data for advertising, and a consent requirement. Whether that history changes your comfort level is personal; read Premom's current privacy policy and decide with that in hand.

What's the best alternative to Premom for OPK tracking?

It depends on what you value most. Flo has broader tracking and a very large user base, but you enter OPK results manually rather than scanning the strip. Clue is a privacy-minded mainstream tracker (8/10 in Mozilla's July 2026 review). Natural Cycles is an FDA-authorized contraception method built around temperature rather than LH strips. Veya logs LH tests alongside mood, sleep and nutrition data. None of them replicates Premom's camera-based strip scanning, since that's a Premom specialty.

Do any period tracking apps work as birth control?

Natural Cycles is FDA-authorized as a software application for contraception (FDA review DEN170052), and its listing states 93% effectiveness with typical use. That applies specifically to Natural Cycles and its temperature-based algorithm, not to period or ovulation trackers generally. Flo, Clue, Premom and Veya are tracking tools, not cleared contraceptive methods, and none of them should be relied on for pregnancy prevention.

How do I export my data from Premom?

Premom lets you view your logged cycle and test history inside the app, and you can request a copy of your data through in-app support or by emailing the company if you don't see a self-serve export option in your current version. Before switching apps, it's worth writing down your last several LH test results and period start dates by hand as a backup, since manual notes are something you control regardless of what the app supports.

Can Veya scan OPK strips like Premom does?

Veya lets you log LH test results manually, the same way Flo does, but it doesn't photograph and analyze the test strip itself the way Premom does. What Veya adds is that your LH readings sit next to your cycle, mood, sleep, and nutrition data, which can surface patterns a fertility-only app doesn't have the data to show.

Will I lose my history if I switch away from Premom?

Your test results and cycle logs won't transfer automatically to a new app, so it's worth exporting or writing down your history before you switch. In the new app, enter your last few period start dates and any recent LH readings by hand to give predictions a head start. Most apps need a cycle or two of fresh logging to calibrate to you.

Final Thoughts

Premom's strip scanning is a genuinely useful feature, and its 2023 FTC settlement was a real, documented issue that the company has since responded to with actual changes. Both things are true at once. If strip scanning is the reason you're there and you've read the current privacy policy, staying is a fine answer.

If you're moving, match the app to your actual reason. Want broad tracking and a very large user base? Flo. Want a privacy-minded mainstream tracker? Clue. Want a method with FDA authorization behind it? Natural Cycles, on its own specific terms. And if your reason for restlessness was wanting your LH readings connected to the rest of your health picture, that's the gap Veya was built for.

Still deciding? Pick one and log for 2 cycles. That's enough to know if it fits, and you can always switch back.
