Why People Look for Premom Alternatives

Two different groups end up here. The first is happy with Premom's strip-scanning feature but wants a broader app once they're past the daily OPK-testing phase, since Premom's symptom tracking, content, and non-fertility features are thinner than a general cycle tracker's.

The second group read the news. In May 2023, the FTC found that Premom's developer had shared sensitive health data with third parties, including China-based analytics firms, without proper disclosure, and "is Premom safe" has stayed a live question ever since. That's a reasonable prompt to look around, and it doesn't mean Premom is a bad app. Its core scanning feature is genuinely well-built. The question is whether the company's data practices, then and now, are something you're comfortable with.

Both are legitimate reasons to read this guide, and neither one requires you to leave Premom if it's working for you.

Premom's 2023 FTC Order: What Actually Happened

Here's the factual record, with dates, so you can decide for yourself what it means for you.

May 2023: The FTC announced that Premom's developer, Illinois-based Easy Healthcare Corporation, had shared users' sensitive health and device data with third parties without proper disclosure or consent. That included sharing data with AppsFlyer and Google, as well as with two China-based analytics firms, Umeng and Jiguang, through SDKs integrated into the app. The shared data reportedly included health information, social media account details, precise geolocation, and device and Wi-Fi network identifiers. The complaint also cited a violation of the Health Breach Notification Rule for failing to notify consumers about the unauthorized disclosures.

The settlement: Easy Healthcare agreed to pay a $200,000 penalty. Under the order, the company is barred from sharing users' personal health data with third parties for advertising purposes, and it must obtain user consent before sharing health data for any other purpose. It's also required to tell consumers clearly how their data will be used.

Since the settlement: Premom has updated its data-sharing practices and agreed to delete the data that was previously shared. To be clear, the strip-scanning feature itself wasn't the subject of the complaint. The issue was what happened to data in the background, not how the scanning technology works. Read Premom's current privacy policy yourself if this is a deciding factor for you, since this article reflects what was published as of August 2026.

When Staying With Premom Makes Sense

Staying is a legitimate choice, and for a lot of users it's the right one. Here's where Premom earns a real recommendation.

  • Strip scanning is your main reason for using the app. No alternative here photographs and quantifies an OPK strip the way Premom does. If that's the feature you actually need, switching means giving it up.
  • Your cycle is irregular. Premom's same-day hormone reading doesn't depend on predictable cycle history the way calendar-based apps do.
  • You value the TTC-specific community. Premom's forums, including PCOS-focused discussion, are a more targeted fit for some users than a general cycle-tracking community.
  • You've read the current privacy policy and you're comfortable with it. The company has made real, verifiable changes since 2023, and reasonable people land in different places on how much that history matters to them going forward.

If most of that describes you, there's nothing here that should push you off Premom. The rest of this guide is for everyone else.

The Alternatives Compared

Here are four apps matched to different reasons for looking around, in no particular order of quality. Each profile covers features, strengths, honest limitations, and price.

For the Deepest Feature Set and Biggest Community: Flo

Best if you want: The most-used period tracker in the category, with the widest symptom list, free pregnancy mode, and a large community, alongside manual OPK logging.

Key Features

  • Cycle tracking with calendar and algorithm-based predictions
  • Manual ovulation test logging: you enter your own result, with no strip photography
  • Thousands of health articles reviewed by medical professionals
  • Secret Chats: anonymous community discussions
  • Pregnancy mode included free, with week-by-week tracking

Strengths

  • The largest community of any tracker in the category
  • An extensive, medically reviewed content library
  • Free pregnancy mode, useful once you're past the TTC stage
  • Cross-platform (iOS and Android)

Limitations

  • No strip scanning. You read and enter your own OPK result
  • Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing health data with Facebook and Google without proper consent. A related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode and open-sourced it
  • Free users see ads, and the AI Health Assistant sits behind Flo Premium

Who Should Choose This

  • Feature depth and community size matter more to you than strip scanning
  • You want a free pregnancy mode once you conceive
  • You're comfortable manually entering your own OPK results

Pricing: Free (with ads), Flo Premium ~$59.99/year.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

Key Features

  • Science-backed content reviewed by researchers

For FDA-Cleared Accuracy: Natural Cycles

Best if you want: A fertility app with a specific regulatory clearance behind it, built around a temperature-based algorithm rather than LH strips.

Natural Cycles is the only fertility app that's FDA-cleared, with a stated 93% typical-use effectiveness rate for birth control under its own clearance. That claim belongs specifically to Natural Cycles' temperature-based method, not to fertility or ovulation trackers as a category, and it's worth reading in that narrow context rather than as a general statement about tracking apps.

Key Features

  • Daily basal body temperature logging (manual or via a compatible thermometer or wearable)
  • An algorithm that determines fertile and non-fertile days from your temperature pattern
  • Optional LH test logging alongside temperature
  • FDA clearance for use as birth control, under its own specific claims

Strengths

  • The only fertility app with FDA clearance, a meaningful regulatory bar the others in this guide haven't cleared
  • GDPR-compliant (Sweden-based) with a stated no-data-selling policy
  • Works whether you're trying to conceive or trying not to, using the same underlying method

Limitations

  • No OPK strip scanning. LH logging is manual and secondary to the temperature method
  • Requires daily temperature discipline, which is a bigger habit shift than scanning a strip
  • Subscription-only, with no meaningful free tier
  • Effectiveness claims are specific to Natural Cycles' own clearance and shouldn't be generalized to other apps, including this one

Who Should Choose This

  • You want an FDA-cleared method, not just a logging tool
  • You're willing to take your temperature daily
  • Regulatory clearance matters more to you than strip-scanning convenience

Pricing: Subscription-based, roughly $99.99/year as of 2026. Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

Worth Noting: Femometer

Femometer is another app built around scanning OPK strips, using its own branded test kits. Its app reads a strip and returns a low, high, or peak result, similar in concept to Premom's approach, and it's paired specifically to Femometer-brand strips. We haven't profiled it in full here since it's a narrower, strip-specific tool rather than a general cycle app, but it's worth knowing about if strip scanning is the one feature you don't want to give up.

How to Move Your Data Off Premom

If you've decided to switch, do it in this order: get your data out, set up the new app, then delete. Here's the practical sequence.

  1. Check the app for an export option first. Look under Settings for a data export or a share/save feature for your cycle and test history. If you don't see one, contact support directly and ask for a copy of your logged history before you delete anything.
  2. Write down your key numbers in the meantime. Record your last several period start dates and your most recent LH readings (including any peak days) by hand. That's a backup you control regardless of what the app's export tools support.
  3. Set up the new app before deleting the old one. Enter your last 2 or 3 period start dates and any recent test results by hand. That gives any tracker a working baseline immediately, and most need about 2 cycles of fresh logging to calibrate fully.
  4. Delete your account when you're ready. Go to the app's account settings and confirm deletion. If you pay for Premom Premium through the App Store or Google Play, deleting your account doesn't automatically cancel the billing, so cancel that separately in your phone's subscription settings.

Be honest with yourself about what you'll miss. You lose the camera-based strip scanning, which no alternative here replicates exactly. You lose the TTC-specific community if you used it. And your new app starts with a thin history, so predictions take a couple of cycles to settle in. None of that is a reason to stay if you want to go. It's just better to know before you delete than after.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Premom safe to use in 2026?

In May 2023, the FTC found that Premom's developer, Easy Healthcare Corporation, had shared users' health and device data with third parties, including two China-based analytics firms, without proper disclosure. The company paid a $200,000 penalty and is now barred from sharing health data with third parties for advertising without consent. It has since updated its data practices and agreed to delete the data that was shared. Whether that history changes your comfort level is a personal decision worth making after reading Premom's current privacy policy yourself.

What's the best alternative to Premom for OPK tracking?

It depends on what you value most. Flo has the deepest general feature set and largest community, but you enter OPK results manually rather than scanning the strip. Clue has the strongest privacy record if that's your main concern. Natural Cycles is the only FDA-cleared fertility app, built around temperature rather than LH strips specifically. Go Go Gaia logs LH tests alongside mood, sleep, and nutrition data. None of them replicate Premom's camera-based strip scanning exactly, since that's a distinctive Premom feature.

Do any period tracking apps work as birth control?

Natural Cycles is the only app in this category that's FDA-cleared for use as birth control, with a stated 93% typical-use effectiveness rate under its own clearance. That claim applies specifically to Natural Cycles and its temperature-based algorithm, not to period or ovulation trackers generally. Flo, Clue, Premom, and Go Go Gaia are tracking and logging tools, not cleared contraceptive methods, and none of them should be relied on for pregnancy prevention.

How do I export my data from Premom?

Premom lets you view your logged cycle and test history inside the app, and you can request a copy of your data through in-app support or by emailing the company if you don't see a self-serve export option in your current version. Before switching apps, it's worth writing down your last several LH test results and period start dates by hand as a backup, since manual notes are something you control regardless of what the app supports.

Can Go Go Gaia scan OPK strips like Premom does?

Go Go Gaia lets you log LH test results manually, the same way Flo and Clue do, but it doesn't photograph and analyze the test strip itself the way Premom does. What Go Go Gaia adds is that your LH readings sit next to your cycle, mood, sleep, and nutrition data in one place, which can surface patterns a fertility-only app doesn't have the data to show.

Will I lose my history if I switch away from Premom?

Your test results and cycle logs won't transfer automatically to a new app, so it's worth exporting or writing down your history before you switch. In the new app, enter your last few period start dates and any recent LH readings by hand to give predictions a head start. Most apps need a cycle or two of fresh logging to calibrate to you.

Final Thoughts

Premom's strip scanning is a genuinely useful feature, and its 2023 FTC settlement was a real, documented issue that the company has since responded to with actual changes. Both things are true at once. If strip scanning is the reason you're there and you've read the current privacy policy, staying is a fine answer.

If you're moving, match the app to your actual reason. Want the deepest feature set and free pregnancy mode? Flo. Want the strongest mainstream privacy record? Clue. Want a method with FDA clearance behind it? Natural Cycles, on its own specific terms. And if your reason for restlessness was wanting your LH readings connected to the rest of your health picture, that's the gap Go Go Gaia was built for.

Still deciding? Pick one and log for 2 cycles. That's enough to know if it fits, and you can always switch back.
