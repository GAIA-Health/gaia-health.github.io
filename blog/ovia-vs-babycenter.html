Ovia vs BabyCenter in 2026: Tracking vs Community

Ovia and BabyCenter show up together in nearly every pregnancy app roundup, but they're not solving the same problem. Ovia, owned by Labcorp since 2021, is a data-forward tracker spanning cycle tracking, trying to conceive, pregnancy, and postpartum, and it folded its three separate apps into one Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker in March 2026. BabyCenter, owned by Everyday Health Group, a Ziff Davis division, is a free, ad-supported app built around week-by-week content, 3D fetal visuals, and Birth Clubs grouped by due date. Here's what each is actually built for at every stage, what's free versus paid, how each handles your data, and who tends to be happiest with which one.

Quick Answer

Ovia and BabyCenter solve different problems. Ovia is a Labcorp-owned tracker spanning cycle tracking, TTC, pregnancy, and postpartum in one consolidated app. BabyCenter is a free, ad-supported content and community app built around week-by-week guides, 3D fetal visuals, and due-date Birth Clubs. Here's how they compare:

  • If you want structured tracking across TTC, pregnancy, and postpartum in one place: Go with Ovia.
  • If you want free week-by-week content, 3D visuals, and a due-date-matched community: Go with BabyCenter.
  • If what you actually want is cycle tracking plus whole-health logging that continues before and after pregnancy: that's outside what either app is built for. A broader tracker like Go Go Gaia covers that ground instead.

What Each Is Built For, By Stage

The clearest way to compare these two is stage by stage, since neither one is trying to be everything at once.

Trying to Conceive (TTC)

Ovia is the deeper tool here, logging basal body temperature and OPK results and generating fertile-window and ovulation-time predictions with a daily fertility score.[4] BabyCenter includes only an Ovulation Calculator and general conception tips,[8] a useful starting point but not built for day-to-day charting. Our fertility tracking app comparison covers more TTC-specific options.

Pregnancy

Both track a pregnancy week by week, but differently. Ovia carries your cycle and TTC history straight into pregnancy tracking in the same app since the March 2026 consolidation,[3] while BabyCenter centers on 3D fetal development videos and due-date Birth Clubs.[8] Either way, due dates are estimates, and it's normal for apps and providers to land on slightly different weeks. See our guide on why pregnancy apps disagree on your week.

Postpartum

Postpartum is one of Ovia's tracked stages alongside TTC, pregnancy, and perimenopause,[5] continuing structured tracking into recovery. BabyCenter's postpartum experience leans on parenting content and community rather than a tracking tool, since its distinctive features are pregnancy-specific. See our postpartum tracking app comparison for what's worth logging after birth.

Ovia: What You Get

Ovia, now branded Ovia Health by Labcorp, is built around one continuous record from cycle tracking through TTC, pregnancy, postpartum, and perimenopause.[2] Since March 2026, that record lives in a single app, the Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker, replacing what used to be three separate apps.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with basal body temperature and OPK logging
  • TTC tools: fertile-window predictions, ovulation timing, and a daily fertility score
  • Pregnancy tracking that carries over cycle and TTC history in the same app
  • Postpartum, perimenopause, and menopause tracking
  • Unlimited 1:1 Care Team support, available at no direct cost to members

Strengths and Limitations

One continuous record across life stages, with real support built in. History carries from cycle or TTC tracking straight into pregnancy and postpartum, and Care Team access offers unlimited 1:1 support at no added cost, which most content-first pregnancy apps don't provide.[2] The March 2026 consolidation kept that history intact rather than losing it.[3]

No 3D visuals or due-date community, and pricing isn't published up front. Ovia doesn't offer BabyCenter's interactive imagery or Birth Club structure, and its own community is smaller and more tracking-adjacent. Its site and App Store listing also don't list a fixed consumer price. It's free to download, with full features commonly available through an employer or health-plan benefit, so check current pricing in the app if you're paying yourself.

BabyCenter: What You Get

BabyCenter is built around free content, visual tools, and community structured by due date, with 3D fetal development visuals as its most recognizable feature.[8]

Features

  • 3D fetal development videos and daily updates tied to your due date
  • Birth Clubs: community groups organized by due-date month
  • BabyCenter Community for broader discussion
  • Ovulation Calculator and general TTC tips
  • Pregnancy calendar for appointments and symptoms

Strengths and Limitations

Detailed 3D visuals, plus a due-date community. BabyCenter's fetal development videos go further than the static graphics most pregnancy apps use, and Birth Clubs grouped by due-date month get you talking to people due the same time as you. It's free with no subscription required, and a complimentary loyalty rewards program is a nice bonus most tracking apps don't offer.

It's ad-supported, and TTC or postpartum tracking isn't the focus. Like most free content apps, BabyCenter's business model relies on advertising and some data use for personalization, worth knowing before you log sensitive information. Its Ovulation Calculator is a useful starting point but not built for day-to-day fertility charting, and its distinctive features are pregnancy-specific, so usefulness narrows once you're past delivery.

Free vs Paid

BabyCenter is the simpler story: free to download and use, funded through advertising, with a loyalty rewards program layered on top and no subscription to weigh.

Ovia is free to download too, with premium features commonly available at no direct cost through an employer or health-plan benefit.[4] Neither Ovia's site nor its current App Store listing publishes a fixed consumer price, so if you're paying yourself, check current pricing in the app.

Privacy and Data Use

Neither app is a completely clean slate, and the tradeoffs differ in kind rather than one being clearly better.

Ovia's privacy policy says it may create de-identified data for research and development, may share that data with research partners like universities, and may be compensated for sharing de-identified or aggregate data.[6] If you receive premium Ovia+ access through an employer or health plan, data sharing extends to your insurer, employer, or their business associates under HIPAA.[6]

BabyCenter is free because it's ad-supported, and free apps in this category generally rely on some data use for targeting and personalization. Read the current privacy policy for whichever app you're using, and don't assume "free" means "no data use."

Which Should You Choose?

This comes down to which job you actually need done. Here's the honest breakdown:

Choose Ovia if:

  • You want structured tracking across TTC, cycle, pregnancy, and postpartum in one continuous record
  • You want access to unlimited 1:1 Care Team support without an extra fee
  • You have access to Ovia through an employer or health plan
  • 3D visuals and a due-date-matched community aren't priorities for you

Choose BabyCenter if:

  • Detailed 3D fetal development visuals matter to you
  • You want a community grouped specifically by due-date month (Birth Clubs)
  • You want a fully free app with no subscription decision to make
  • Deep TTC-specific charting (BBT, OPK) isn't what you're after

Some people use both. BabyCenter covers the content and Birth Club community, and Ovia covers the more structured day-to-day tracking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ovia owned by Labcorp?

Yes. Labcorp acquired Ovia Health in August 2021, and the app is now branded Ovia Health by Labcorp, covering cycle tracking, TTC, pregnancy, postpartum, and perimenopause and menopause.

What happened to Ovia's separate fertility, pregnancy, and parenting apps?

Ovia consolidated them. In March 2026, Ovia folded its standalone Ovia Fertility, Ovia Pregnancy, and Ovia Parenting apps into one, the Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker. Ovia's own announcement described it as a transition with the same login and carried-over history, not a shutdown.

Is BabyCenter free?

Yes. BabyCenter is free to download and use, funded through advertising and a complimentary loyalty rewards program rather than a required subscription.

Are BabyCenter and Ovia owned by the same company?

No. BabyCenter is owned by Everyday Health Group, a Ziff Davis division that also owns What to Expect. Ovia is a separate company, owned by Labcorp since 2021. We cover the What to Expect and BabyCenter shared-ownership angle in a separate guide.

Which is more private, Ovia or BabyCenter?

Neither is a clean slate. Ovia's privacy policy allows sharing or selling de-identified data for research, plus employer or health-plan data sharing under HIPAA for benefit-provided access. BabyCenter is free and ad-supported, which generally means some data use for advertising and personalization. Read each app's current privacy policy before logging sensitive information.
