What matters most when choosing a period tracker?

Four things decide the right period tracker: your goal (cycle prediction, conceiving, birth control or general health), what you want to pay, how you feel about data sharing, and how much you are willing to log. App Store prices in October 2026 run from free to $149.99 a year, so budget narrows the field first. Ask yourself these questions to clarify your priorities:

Your Current Health Goals

  • Are you trying to conceive? → You'll need fertility tracking features (BBT, cervical mucus, OPK logging)
  • Are you pregnant or planning pregnancy soon? → Pregnancy mode is essential
  • Are you using fertility awareness for birth control? → You need highly accurate algorithms and rigorous tracking
  • Do you just want to predict your period? → Basic tracking may be enough
  • Are you tracking symptoms to discuss with a doctor? → Look for export features and comprehensive symptom logging

Accuracy varies a lot depending on what an app actually measures. If that's your main worry, read how accurate period tracker apps really are before you pick one.

If your priority list from above spans cycle, mood, and symptoms rather than just one of them, see our comparison of apps that track all three together instead of juggling separate logs.

Your Privacy Priorities

  • How important is data privacy to you? (1-10 scale)
  • Are you comfortable with targeted ads based on your cycle data?
  • Do you want data stored locally on your device vs. cloud?
  • Do you need to use the app anonymously without an account?

Your Budget

  • Do you want an app that's completely free?
  • Are you willing to pay for premium features? (on the App Store in October 2026: Clue Plus $39.99, Veya Pro $49.99, Flo Premium $47.99-$59.99, Natural Cycles $149.99 per year)
  • Do you want a generous free tier with option to upgrade later?

Your Usage Style

  • Do you want quick 1-click logging or detailed daily journaling?
  • Do you want to learn about women's health through articles?
  • Do you prefer science-focused or lifestyle-focused approaches?
  • Do you want habit tracking integrated with cycle tracking?

Pro Tip

Write down your top 3 priorities. This will help you quickly evaluate which app is right for you. For example: "1) Privacy, 2) Pregnancy tracking, 3) Free" or "1) Fertility awareness, 2) Educational content, 3) Community features"

How private are period tracker apps?

Flo has the most documented privacy history of these apps: a 2021 FTC order and a $59.5 million class settlement with a final approval hearing on October 29, 2026. Clue describes itself as Berlin-based and covered by EU data protection law, and Ovia is owned by Labcorp. Given the sensitive nature of period tracking data, privacy should be a major factor in your decision:

What to Look For

  • Data encryption - End-to-end encryption protects your data
  • Local storage - Data stored on your device vs. cloud servers
  • No third-party sales - Your data shouldn't be sold to advertisers
  • Anonymous use option - Ability to use without creating account
  • Clear privacy policy - Transparent about data usage
  • GDPR compliance - Even if you're not in Europe, this indicates strong privacy practices

Strong Privacy Practices

Veya and Clue have the strongest privacy commitments, with data staying on your device and no third-party data sales. If privacy is your #1 priority, start with these two.

Privacy Concerns

Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 for sharing period and pregnancy data with Facebook and Google, and a related class action got preliminary court approval for a combined $59.5 million settlement in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026. Ovia (owned by Labcorp) has faced scrutiny over employer-sponsored versions that can access de-identified employee health data. Glow reached a $250,000 settlement with California's Attorney General in September 2020 over alleged privacy and security flaws. Always read the privacy policy before entering sensitive health data.

How do you get started with a period tracker?

Log your last few period start dates first, then add symptoms and mood daily for two or three cycles. Predictions improve as the app collects your own history, which matters because only 13% of 612,613 cycles in one large dataset were exactly 28 days long. Once you've chosen an app:

  1. Track consistently for 3 cycles - Patterns emerge over time
  2. Log more than just your period - Symptoms provide valuable context
  3. Set daily reminders - Consistency improves accuracy
  4. Start with basics, add details later - Don't get overwhelmed
  5. Review your data monthly - Look for patterns
  6. Be patient - Predictions improve as the app learns your patterns
  7. You can always switch - Try an app for 2-3 months and switch if it's not working

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a period tracker app cost in 2026?

Most period trackers are free to download with optional paid tiers. On the App Store in October 2026, Clue Plus is $39.99 a year, Veya Pro is $49.99 a year, Flo Premium is listed at $47.99 and $59.99 without billing-period labels, and Natural Cycles is $149.99 a year with no free tier. Ovia lists no in-app purchases. See Flo alternatives and Flo vs Natural Cycles for price-led comparisons.

Do I need a paid period tracker?

Not necessarily! Many apps offer robust free versions. Veya and Clue both provide strong free tiers. Whether you need premium depends on which specific features matter to you—pregnancy mode, advanced analytics, ad removal, etc.

Can I switch apps without losing data?

Yes! Most apps allow you to manually enter past cycle data. Some also offer export features. You won't lose your history when switching.

How accurate are period predictions?

Accuracy depends on how regular your cycles are. In a dataset of 612,613 cycles from Natural Cycles users, only 13% were 28 days long, so a fixed 28-day calendar misses for most people. Apps that learn from your own history tend to adapt, and irregular cycles are harder to predict regardless of app. A 2016 study in Obstetrics & Gynecology scored Clue highest (13 of 15) among 20 free apps on its criteria.

What if I have irregular cycles?

Apps can still help you track patterns, but predictions will be less accurate. Focus on symptom tracking and identifying triggers. Consult your doctor if cycles are consistently irregular.

Should I track every day?

For basic period predictions, tracking period start/end dates is enough. For fertility awareness and detailed insights, daily logging provides more accurate results.

How does the Flo app compare to competitors in 2026?

Flo has the largest user base and a polished, familiar interface, with Flo Premium at about $59.99 per year. Its main trade-offs are privacy (Flo settled with the FTC over sharing health data with Facebook and Google, then added Anonymous Mode) and the fact that many useful features sit behind the paywall. Competitors differentiate in clear ways: Clue is privacy-first and science-led at a similar price, Natural Cycles is FDA-cleared for birth control, and all-in-one trackers add nutrition, fitness, and wearable data. The right pick depends on whether you most value community, privacy, or breadth.

Is Flo Premium worth it compared to free Ovia features?

It comes down to what you track. Flo Premium (about $59.99 per year) adds deeper insights, content, and an AI assistant on top of a large community. Ovia is free and strong for the fertility-to-pregnancy journey, including a food-safety lookup tool, and is often available as an employer health benefit. If you mainly want cycle and pregnancy tracking without paying, Ovia covers a lot for free. If you want Flo's insights and community and don't mind the subscription, Premium adds more. Trying both free tiers for a cycle or two is the simplest way to decide.

What features should I look for when choosing a reproductive health app like Flo?

Start with your main goal, since that determines which features matter. Look at cycle and symptom tracking depth, how the app handles irregular cycles, whether it offers wearable integration for temperature and sleep, what stays free versus paywalled, and the privacy policy (whether the app sells or shares data). If you are trying to conceive or avoid pregnancy, also check whether predictions are FDA-cleared, which only applies to a few apps like Natural Cycles. Matching these to your priority beats picking by brand name.

What should I look for when comparing wearables for period tracking?

For cycle tracking specifically, the most useful wearable signal is overnight skin temperature, which shifts around ovulation, followed by sleep and HRV. Devices like the Oura Ring, Apple Watch (Series 8 and later), and Garmin can capture this automatically, but the wearable itself does not predict your cycle. The app it syncs with does. So check that your tracking app actually integrates with the device you own. For irregular cycles, temperature-based data tends to be more reliable than calendar-only prediction.

So which period tracker should you use?

The "best" period tracker is the one that fits YOUR needs, priorities, and lifestyle. Consider:

  • Your health goals - Trying to conceive? Pregnant? Just tracking?
  • Your privacy comfort level - How important is data security?
  • Your budget - Free vs. paid features
  • Your usage style - Simple or comprehensive tracking?

You can't make a wrong choice—try an app for 2-3 months and see if it works for you. If not, switch! The important thing is to start tracking and understand your body better.
