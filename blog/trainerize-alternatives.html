Trainerize Alternatives for Coaches Who Want More Client Context

If you're evaluating Trainerize alongside other options, this guide covers what Trainerize does well, why coaches go looking anyway, and how TrueCoach, Everfit, PT Distinction, and Veya for Professionals compare on program delivery, pricing, and client data. One of the apps below is ours, and we'll say so plainly and be honest about where it doesn't fit.

What does Trainerize do well?

Trainerize, now operating as ABC Trainerize under ABC Fitness, is one of the most established names in personal training software, and for good reason. It covers the full loop of remote coaching: an AI-assisted workout builder, meal tracking, habit coaching, 1-1 and group messaging, a Custom Branded App option, and Stripe-based payments for selling programs or packages, as of September 2026.[1]

It scales from independent trainers up to gyms and enterprise health clubs, with tiered plans built around client count. A permanent free Basic plan covers one client, and a 30-day free trial with unlimited clients and no credit card requirement lets you test the full platform before committing.[1] Trainerize cites more than 400,000 trainers and coaches and more than 1.6 million clients on the platform, as of September 2026.[1] That range, from a single-client hobby coach up to a 500-plus-client studio operation, is a wide range for one product.

Where it makes sense to look elsewhere is less about quality and more about fit. Some coaches want a simpler plan structure without the tiered client-count math. Some coaches training female clients want to see health context Trainerize was never built to show, like where a client is in her cycle or how her sleep tracked against a hard training week. None of that is a knock on Trainerize. It just means the four options below are worth a look depending on what you're missing.

Why do coaches look for Trainerize alternatives?

Most coaches who search for alternatives to Trainerize aren't unhappy with it. They're checking whether something fits their specific practice better, whether that's price, simplicity, or a kind of client data Trainerize doesn't track.

  • You want a simpler pricing ladder. Trainerize's tiers scale with client count in a way that can feel like a lot of plan-comparing for a small roster. PT Distinction, for example, prices by plan plus a per-additional-client fee.
  • You want to compare branded-app options. Several platforms offer a custom-branded app, and which plan includes it differs, so check each pricing page.
  • You want different payment, scheduling, or messaging tools. Each platform packages these differently, so compare the feature pages for what matters to your practice.
  • You coach clients where cycle, sleep, or lab context would change your programming. As of September 2026, none of the official pages we checked for Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit, or PT Distinction mention menstrual cycle, hormone, lab, or symptom data. Our guide to what the research actually says about training around the cycle covers how much of that context is established versus still uncertain.

None of that makes Trainerize the wrong choice. It just means the four alternatives below are worth a look if any of that sounds like your practice.

The Alternatives Compared

Here are three established program-delivery platforms plus one app that covers a different gap entirely, in no particular order of quality. For a direct head-to-head on two of the biggest names, see our TrueCoach vs Trainerize comparison.

For a One-on-One, Personal-Trainer Focus: TrueCoach

Best if you want: A platform that calls itself software for personal trainers, with plans priced by client tier.

Key Features

  • Drag-and-drop workout builder with a large exercise video library (3,500+ videos) and MyFitnessPal-linked nutrition tracking, as of September 2026[2]
  • Real-time in-app messaging and custom habit tracking, assigned daily, weekly, or monthly
  • Wearable integrations named with Apple Health, Garmin, WHOOP, and Oura, plus Stripe-based payments, as of September 2026[2]

Strengths

  • Describes itself as the "#1 software for personal trainers" on its own site (its claim, not ours)[2]
  • 14-day free trial with no credit card required, as of September 2026[2]

Limitations

  • No permanent free plan the way Trainerize has one for a single client, and plans are priced by client tier (5, 20, or 50 active clients)
  • No menstrual cycle or hormone context for the coach

Pricing (as of September 2026): Starter $26.34/month for up to 5 active clients, Standard $57.99/month for up to 20, Pro $136.99/month for up to 50, all as the annual-billed monthly rate.[2]

Learn more: truecoach.co

For a Larger Free Plan and Native Nutrition: Everfit

Best if you want: A permanent free plan for up to 5 clients, AI-assisted programming, and native nutrition tools, with per-client cost that drops as your roster grows.

Key Features

  • AI-powered workout programming with an exercise library, plus nutrition tracking, meal plans, and macro monitoring, as of September 2026[3]
  • Habit tracking and goal setting, scheduled and automated messages, and forms and questionnaires[3]
  • A white-label branded app, API access, and a success manager on the custom-priced Enterprise tier, as of September 2026[3]

Strengths

  • Free "Forever Free" Starter plan for up to 5 clients, more room than Trainerize's single-client free tier, as of September 2026[3]
  • Per-client cost drops as your roster grows, from roughly $3 per client at 5 clients down to under $1 per client at 300, as of September 2026

Limitations

  • Several features, such as automation, on-demand collections, and meal plans, are priced as separate add-ons, and the white-label app sits on the Enterprise tier
  • No menstrual cycle or hormone context for the coach, and its features page does not name specific wearable platforms

Pricing (as of September 2026): Starter free for up to 5 clients. Pro from about $16 to $290/month scaling with client count (5 to 300 clients). Studio from about $88 to $358/month (50 to 500 clients). Enterprise custom-priced.[3]

Learn more: everfit.io

For Low-Cost Per-Client Pricing: PT Distinction

Best if you want: A low-cost entry plan with a small per-additional-client fee, and a program builder with an AI meal planner.

Key Features

  • Workout builder with circuits, supersets, and custom program types, plus progress charts, as of September 2026[4]
  • Photo food diary, recipe sharing, and an AI Meal Planner, with MyFitnessPal integration[4]
  • Group messaging, scheduling controls, and PayPal and Stripe payments for package sales[4]

Strengths

  • Per-additional-client pricing that falls from $6.00 to $1.60 across the three tiers[4]
  • 1-month free trial, with the option to cancel in the first month and pay nothing, per the company[4]

Limitations

  • Custom-branded iOS and Android apps come with the Pro and Master tiers, not Basic, and there is no menstrual cycle or hormone context for the coach[4]

Pricing (as of September 2026): Basic $19.90/month for up to 3 clients, plus $6 per additional client. Pro $59.90/month for up to 25 clients, plus $2.40 per additional client. Master $89.90/month for up to 50 clients, plus $1.60 per additional client. All tiers come with a 1-month free trial.

Learn more: ptdistinction.com

Which Trainerize alternative fits which coach?

The right pick depends less on which platform is "best" and more on what kind of coaching business you're running and what you already have covered.

  • Solo trainer just starting to take clients online: Trainerize's free Basic plan or TrueCoach's trial are the lowest-friction ways to see if a coaching app fits your workflow before you commit to a paid tier.
  • Growing practice that outgrew spreadsheets and DMs: Everfit's scaling per-client pricing or TrueCoach's tiered plans both work well once you're managing more than a handful of clients but aren't ready for gym-scale software.
  • Coach who wants low per-client cost at small roster sizes: PT Distinction's plans start at $19.90 a month for 3 clients, with per-additional-client fees that fall as you move up a tier.[4]
  • Coach whose clients are mostly women and whose programming gap is health context, not program delivery: Keep whatever platform you're already using for workouts and payments, and add the Veya coach portal alongside it for the cycle, sleep, and lab picture. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best alternative to Trainerize?

There isn't one right alternative, since these apps are built for different coaching businesses. TrueCoach calls itself software for personal trainers, and if it isn't the right fit either, our roundup of TrueCoach alternatives covers more options in that category. Everfit has a free plan for up to 5 clients and native nutrition tools. PT Distinction offers low-cost per-client pricing. Veya for Professionals fits coaches whose gap isn't program delivery but seeing a client's cycle, sleep, and lab data alongside training.

Is Trainerize free?

Trainerize has a permanent free Basic plan for one client, plus a 30-day free trial with unlimited clients and no credit card required, as of September 2026. Paid plans start at $9 a month for the Grow plan, with Pro plans from $23 a month scaling with client count.

What's the difference between Trainerize and TrueCoach?

Both are established coaching platforms with workout builders, messaging, and habit tracking, but they lean different directions. Trainerize cites more than 400,000 trainers and coaches, names gym brands such as Gold's Gym, Life Time, and YMCA, and offers a Studio Plus tier for larger rosters, as of September 2026. TrueCoach calls itself software for personal trainers and prices plans by client tier from 5 to 50 active clients. We cover this comparison in more depth in our dedicated TrueCoach vs Trainerize guide, and if Everfit is the one you're weighing against Trainerize instead, our Everfit vs Trainerize comparison covers that matchup directly.

Do any Trainerize alternatives track a client's menstrual cycle?

Not as far as the platforms' official pages show. As of September 2026, we found no menstrual cycle, hormone, lab, or symptom data on the official pages for Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit, or PT Distinction. Veya for Professionals is built specifically to show cycle phase alongside sleep, workout and habit logs, and labs, but it isn't a program-delivery platform in the way the others are, so some coaches use it alongside one of them rather than instead of it.

Can I switch from Trainerize without losing my client data?

We did not find a migration tool for moving between these platforms on their official pricing or features pages. Ask each company's support team what can be exported and imported before you switch, and plan on a short overlap period while clients finish their current block in Trainerize.

Final Thoughts

Trainerize earns its reputation. It covers program delivery end to end, scales from a single client up to a full gym roster, and its free tier and trial make it easy to test before paying anything. For most coaches already using it well, there's no reason to switch.

If you're looking around anyway, match the alternative to the actual gap. Want a one-on-one, personal-trainer focus? TrueCoach. Want a larger free plan with native nutrition? Everfit. Want low per-client pricing? PT Distinction. If what you're actually missing isn't a better workout builder but a clearer picture of what's going on with a client outside the gym, that's the specific gap the Veya coach portal was built to close, alongside whichever program-delivery tool you already trust.
