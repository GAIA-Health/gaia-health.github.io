What does fertility tracking actually involve?

Before picking an app, it helps to understand the methods. There are three main ways to track fertility, and many apps support more than one.

Basal Body Temperature (BBT) Charting

Your resting body temperature typically rises slightly after ovulation and stays elevated until your next period. By charting your BBT daily, you can confirm that ovulation happened. The catch: BBT confirms ovulation after the fact, so it's most useful over multiple cycles to predict future patterns.

You can take your BBT with a regular thermometer each morning before getting up. Or you can use a wearable like Tempdrop or the Apple Watch temperature sensor, which track automatically overnight.

LH (Ovulation) Test Tracking

Luteinizing hormone surges shortly before ovulation, typically within a day or two. Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) detect this surge in your urine. Some apps let you log OPK results manually. Others, like Premom, let you photograph the test strip and analyze the result.

Cervical Mucus Monitoring

Your cervical mucus changes throughout your cycle. Around ovulation, it becomes clear, stretchy, and "egg-white" in consistency. Tracking this gives you a real-time sign that ovulation is approaching (unlike BBT, which confirms it after).

Why track more than one fertility sign?

Each sign tells you something different: BBT confirms ovulation after the fact, LH tests flag the surge just before it, and cervical mucus changes show the fertile phase building. Fertility awareness approaches (often called the symptothermal method) combine them, so you are not leaning on a single signal. Look for an app that supports at least two of these.

Do you need a hardware fertility monitor too?

Beyond Apps: At-Home Hormone Monitors

If you want measured data on top of app predictions, there's a category of at-home hormone monitors. These devices measure hormone levels from urine (or another signal) and pair with an app to track results. Check each maker's site for current pricing, which changes often.

  • Tempdrop: Wearable worn on the upper arm overnight that measures basal body temperature and filters out disturbances like broken sleep. Syncs to its own app, which integrates with Apple Health
  • Inito: Measures estrogen, LH, FSH and PdG (a urine metabolite of progesterone) in about 10 minutes, per the maker
  • Mira: Uses test wands in several configurations, from LH and E3G (estrogen) to LH, E3G and PdG, plus FSH-only and PdG-only wands
  • Oova: A urine-based kit read through a smartphone app that, according to the company, measures LH and PdG (a progesterone metabolite)
  • Kegg: A reusable vaginal sensor that, according to the company, reads the electrolyte level of cervical fluid using impedance instead of visual mucus checking

Many people use these alongside an app. They're a bigger investment, and they add measured data to what an app predicts. If you've been trying for a while, talk to your doctor about whether extra testing makes sense for you.

How do you get started with fertility tracking?

Whichever app you choose, here are some tips to make fertility tracking actually useful:

  1. Start with the basics. Log your period start and end dates. Add BBT if you can. Even basic data gives you a baseline.
  2. Use at least two methods. BBT alone confirms ovulation after the fact. LH tests flag it a day or two ahead. Cervical mucus gives real-time clues. Combining two or more gives you a fuller picture.
  3. Give it a few cycles. Most apps need a few months of data before predictions settle down. Don't judge an app after one cycle.
  4. Track at the same time daily. BBT is most useful when taken at the same time each morning, before getting out of bed. A wearable sensor removes this requirement.
  5. Don't obsess over the numbers. Fertility tracking should reduce stress, not add to it. If checking the app 10 times a day is making you anxious, scale back to once a day.
  6. Bring your data to appointments. If you've been trying for a while and see a fertility specialist, your tracked data (BBT charts, cycle length history, LH patterns) gives them real information to work with instead of estimates.

Pro Tip

If you're not sure which app to start with, consider what you already own. Have an Apple Watch or Oura Ring? Natural Cycles or Veya can use it for automatic temperature tracking. Already using Easy@Home OPK strips? Premom integrates directly. Already tracking your period in Flo? Switch to TTC mode and keep your history. The right app is the one you'll actually use consistently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most accurate fertility tracking app?

Accuracy depends on the signs you log, not just the app, and no app is accurate for everyone. Natural Cycles is FDA-cleared (De Novo DEN170052) for both contraception and conception, and Clue describes Clue Conceive as a medical device feature in its App Store listing. Apps that let you log BBT, LH tests, and cervical mucus tend to give a fuller picture than calendar math alone, and a wearable thermometer or hormone monitor adds measured data.

Do I need a BBT thermometer to use a fertility app?

You don't need one, but it helps a lot. BBT charting confirms ovulation by showing a sustained temperature rise. Without it, most apps rely on calendar predictions, which can be off by several days. You can use a regular BBT thermometer, a wearable like Tempdrop, or the Apple Watch temperature sensor with a compatible app.

What is the best free fertility tracking app?

Several apps offer strong free features. Fertility Friend's core charting is free, with a 30-day trial of premium features. Veya includes free ovulation tracking, BBT charting, LH logging, nutrition, and wearable integration. Ovia Fertility is fully free with no premium tier at all, though it's owned by Labcorp. Basic Clue tracking is free, while advanced predictions and deeper analysis are part of Clue Plus.

How long should I track before trying to conceive?

You don't need to track for a set period before you start trying. Many people log a few cycles to learn their typical ovulation timing and luteal phase length, and tracking gives you better timing data as you go. Even one cycle of BBT charting and LH testing can show you something about your pattern.

Can fertility apps help with irregular cycles?

Yes, but symptom-based tracking matters more than predictions. Calendar-based apps struggle with irregular cycles because they assume regularity. Focus on apps that let you log LH tests, BBT, and cervical mucus so you detect ovulation in real time. Fertility Friend and Veya are built around your logged data rather than calendar assumptions.

What's the difference between a fertility app and a period tracker?

Period trackers predict when your next period will come. Fertility apps go further by identifying your fertile window, the few days each cycle when conception is possible. They typically include ovulation detection through BBT, LH testing, and cervical mucus tracking. Some period trackers (Flo, Clue) offer fertility modes, but dedicated fertility apps often provide more detailed charting and analysis.

Which fertility apps work with LH test strips (OPKs)?

Premom's App Store listing describes scanning your ovulation test and quantifying your LH levels, which makes it a natural pick if you test daily with Easy@Home strips. Veya, Fertility Friend, Clue, Glow, and Flo support logging LH results manually alongside your other fertility signs; Natural Cycles and Ovia don't mention LH logging in their listings, so check in the app.

What's the best fertility app if you're doing IVF or fertility treatment?

The apps in this guide are built around natural-cycle signs: BBT, LH surges, and cervical mucus. Medicated and IVF cycles are a different job — you need medication schedules, appointment tracking, and treatment-cycle logging instead of ovulation prediction. We compared the apps built for that in our best IVF tracking app guide.

What is a fertility awareness method (FAM) app?

A FAM (or symptothermal) app supports tracking all three fertility signs together: basal body temperature, cervical mucus, and LH tests. Fertility Friend has an extensive educational library that teaches you to read your own charts. Veya logs all three signs and pulls temperature from a wearable. Natural Cycles is temperature-led and FDA-cleared.

Final Thoughts

The right fertility app depends on where you are in your TTC process and what kind of data matters most to you. If you want an FDA-cleared option, look at Natural Cycles (De Novo DEN170052); Clue also describes Clue Conceive as a medical device feature. If you want to become fluent in BBT charting and the fertility awareness method, Fertility Friend has 25 years in the category. If privacy is a priority, Clue is worth a close look.

If you want your partner involved, Glow, Premom, Natural Cycles and Flo all list partner features. If you want a free option, Ovia lists no in-app purchases. If you already track in Flo, just switch to TTC mode. If you use OPK strips daily, Premom's scanner is helpful. And if you want fertility data alongside nutrition, fitness, mood, and wearable data, Veya connects them in a single app.

What matters most is picking one and using it consistently. Fertility tracking gets more useful with every cycle of data. The app you use every day is better than the "perfect" app you forget to open.
