Flo vs Veya: Which One Should You Use?

Flo is one of the most downloaded period trackers, with a large community and content library. Veya is a health tracker that puts your cycle next to your mood, sleep, nutrition, and habits. They overlap, but they're built for different things. Here's an honest comparison so you can pick the right one.

By Veya Team • Published June 8, 2026 • Last reviewed October 9, 2026 • 9 min read • App Comparison

Quick Answer

Pick Flo if you want a focused period tracker with community, a large content library, and an Android app. Consider Veya if you want your cycle tracked next to mood, sleep, nutrition, and habits, you're on iPhone, and you don't need an in-app community. Flo reports 570M+ downloads; Veya is newer and much smaller.

  • If you want a focused period tracker with community and content: Go with Flo. Its App Store listing cites 570M+ downloads (as of October 2026), it offers Secret Chats and expert-backed information, and it's on iOS and Android.
  • If you want cycle, mood, sleep, nutrition, and habits tracked together: Try Veya. It has an ad-free free tier and wearable sync, but it's iOS only.

How are Flo and Veya different?

Here's the part that decides most of this comparison, so it's worth saying plainly before the feature lists.

Flo is built around your cycle. Everything centers on period and fertility tracking, and it does that job extremely well, with predictions, a content library, and a community of other users. You can log mood and symptoms too, but the cycle is the heart of the app.

Veya is built around more than your cycle. Your cycle is one piece, sitting next to mood, sleep, nutrition, fitness, and habits, with the app surfacing how they connect. The trade is breadth for a smaller community and, for now, iOS only.

So the real question isn't "which app is better." It's whether you want a focused cycle tracker with a large community and content library behind it, or a broader tracker that ties your cycle to the rest of your day. Both are valid. They just suit different people.

What does Flo do well?

Flo's App Store listing cites over 570 million downloads worldwide and a 4.7-star rating from about 2 million ratings (as of October 2026). It's a polished, focused cycle tracker, and for a lot of people it's all they need, which is a fair conclusion to reach.

Features

  • Cycle and period tracking with fertile window and ovulation predictions
  • Weekly baby-development updates in Pregnancy Mode, with in-depth features in Premium
  • Health information that Flo describes as backed by health experts, plus daily insights and expert-led videos in Premium
  • 24/7 virtual Health Assistant (Flo Premium)
  • Secret Chats, where members share tips (listed under Premium)
  • Availability on iOS and Android, per its App Store and Google Play listings
  • Apple Watch compatibility
  • Anonymous Mode, described on Flo's site as period tracking without identity

Strengths

The community and content are real strengths. Flo pairs expert-backed information with Secret Chats, where you can find tips from other members. If you want to learn while you track, or just feel less alone with a symptom, that's a lot of value, and Veya doesn't offer an equivalent.

The free tier covers the core tools. Flo's listing says the app is free and includes the core tools you need to track and understand your cycle. For many people, free Flo is plenty.

It's polished and on every phone. Flo is available on both iOS and Android, so it works no matter what you carry, and its 4.7-star rating across about 2 million ratings suggests a lot of people like using it.

Limitations

It's centered on the cycle. If you want to track mood, sleep, nutrition, and habits in real depth and see how they connect, that isn't what Flo is built around. It logs some of those, but the cycle is the focus.

Privacy history. The FTC finalized an order against Flo in 2021 over sharing health data, and a class action over the same 2016 to 2019 sharing reached a proposed settlement. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode. More on this in the privacy section.

Premium paywalls. Its listing puts the Health Assistant, Secret Chats, and in-depth pregnancy features under Flo Premium.

Pricing

Free: Core cycle-tracking tools, per its listing.

Flo Premium: Listed at $47.99 and $59.99 in the US App Store (as of October 2026). Adds daily insights, expert-led videos, the Health Assistant, and Secret Chats.

Veya is the app we make, so take this section knowing that. It's a newer, smaller app than Flo, and it's built for a different goal: tracking more than just your cycle. It's the right pick for some people and the wrong pick for others, and we'll be straight about both.

Should you choose Flo or Veya?

There's no single "best" app here, because they're built for different priorities. Here's the honest breakdown:

Choose Flo if:

  • You want a focused, polished period tracker and don't need it to do more
  • A large community and content library matter to you
  • You're on Android, or you want an app that's on every platform
  • You want pregnancy tracking from an established app
  • Your main goal is knowing what your cycle is going to do

Choose Veya if:

  • You want cycle, mood, sleep, fitness, nutrition, and habits in one app
  • You track with a wearable and want that data connected to your cycle
  • You want to see how your lifestyle affects your cycle and symptoms
  • You want a free tier with no ads
  • You're on iPhone and don't need an in-app community

If you're still torn, there's no harm in trying both. They each have a free tier, so you can track a cycle or two in each and keep whichever fits the way you actually use it.

How do Flo and Veya handle privacy?

Cycle and health data is some of the most sensitive information you can put in an app, so privacy deserves real attention with both.

Flo: A Complicated History, With Real Changes

In June 2021 the FTC finalized an order against Flo after alleging it shared sensitive health data from millions of users with companies including Facebook and Google despite promising privacy. The order requires affirmative consent before sharing health information and an independent privacy review; it carried no fine (FTC). A separate class action over the same 2016 to 2019 data sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (Flo's share: $8M); it received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 29, 2026 (court-approved notice). The settling defendants deny wrongdoing. Separately, a jury found in August 2025 that Meta violated California's privacy law, and Meta denies the claims (Consumer Reports).

Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its site describes as period tracking without identity. Whether the history changes your decision is personal, but it's worth knowing.

Veya: Privacy-Focused, Smaller Scale

Veya doesn't run ads and doesn't sell user data, and you can export or delete your data. It's a newer, smaller, US-based company without the public scrutiny the bigger names have been through, and there's no GDPR certification. The no-ads, no-data-selling approach is a meaningful commitment, just at a smaller scale and with a shorter track record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Veya a good alternative to Flo?

It depends on what you want from the app. Flo is the more established choice, with 570M+ downloads per its App Store listing, Secret Chats, expert-backed information, and apps on both iOS and Android. Veya is a good alternative if you want to track more than your cycle, since it covers mood, sleep, nutrition, fitness, and habits alongside your cycle and shows how they connect. If you mainly want a focused period tracker with content and community, Flo is hard to beat. If you want broader tracking with an ad-free free tier and wearable sync, Veya is worth a look, as long as you are on iPhone.

What does Veya do that Flo doesn't?

The main difference is scope. Veya tracks your cycle plus mood, sleep, nutrition, fitness, and custom habits, then surfaces correlations between them, like how your sleep affects your mood in the luteal phase. It also syncs with Apple Watch, Oura Ring, and Garmin for things like temperature and activity. Flo can log some of those too, but it is built around the cycle as its center, while Veya is built to track more than the cycle. Flo, on the other hand, has community, content, and Android, which Veya does not.

Does Veya work on Android?

Not yet. Veya is iOS only right now, with a web app available, but no native Android version. Flo is on both iOS and Android. So if you use an Android phone, Flo is the practical choice today, and Veya would only fit if you are on an iPhone or mainly use the web app.

Is Veya free like Flo?

Both have a free tier. Flo's listing says the app is free with the core tools for tracking your cycle, and lists Flo Premium at $47.99 and $59.99 in the US App Store (as of October 2026) for extras like the Health Assistant and Secret Chats. Veya's free tier covers cycle, mood, sleep, fitness, nutrition, and habit tracking with no ads, and its premium, at about $9.99 per month or $49.99 per year, adds AI features and advanced insights. So both are free to start. The difference is that Veya's free tier is ad-free and broader in scope, while Flo comes with a bigger content library and community.

Is Flo or Veya better for privacy?

They take different approaches. The FTC finalized an order against Flo in June 2021 over sharing health data with firms including Facebook and Google; a separate class action over the same 2016 to 2019 sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (preliminary approval April 2026, final approval hearing October 29, 2026), and a jury found Meta violated California privacy law in August 2025. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its site describes as period tracking without identity. Veya is a smaller, US-based app that does not run ads or sell user data, but it has not been through that same level of public scrutiny and has no GDPR certification. Read both current policies and decide what you are comfortable with.

Does Veya have a community like Flo?

No. Community is one of Flo's strengths, with Secret Chats for sharing tips among members, and Veya does not have an in-app community like that. If talking with other people and reading a large library of articles is part of why you use a tracker, Flo offers far more there. Veya focuses on your own data and the connections within it rather than on a social experience.
