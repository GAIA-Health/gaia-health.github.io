Everfit vs Trainerize: Which Fits Your Coaching Business?

Both platforms build and deliver programs well. This guide compares who each is built for, how programming, nutrition, and client management actually work, current pricing, and which type of coaching business tends to land where.

By Go Go Gaia Team • Published September 7, 2026 • 9 min read • For Coaches

Educational content for fitness and coaching professionals, not medical or financial advice. Pricing and features are current as of September 2026 and change periodically, so confirm current details on each company's own site before buying.

We compared Everfit and Trainerize using what each publishes about its own features and pricing, and note below where Go Go Gaia fits so you can weigh it as you read.

Quick Answer

Everfit and Trainerize both give you a workout builder, native nutrition tools, a client app, and integrated payments. Everfit leans toward coaches, gyms, and sport programs that want AI-assisted programming and deep nutrition tools bundled together, with several features priced as add-ons. Trainerize leans toward coaches and larger multi-location businesses that want a lower-cost entry point, named wearable integrations, and room to add group and community features as they grow. Neither shows you a client's cycle phase or lab work, since that's outside what either was built to track.

Everfit and Trainerize are two of the names that keep coming up together in coaching-platform comparisons. Both are established, both serve everyone from solo coaches to multi-location gyms, and both build and deliver programs well. The differences show up in how nutrition and automation are priced, whether wearable integrations are named specifically, what it costs as your roster grows, and one thing both leave out: any view into a client's cycle, sleep, or lab data alongside her training.

Who Each Platform Is Built For

Everfit describes itself as "the all-in-one platform for Fitness Coaches, Gyms & Studios, Nutrition Coaches, Sport Coaches," and separately as being for "inspiring coaches to training organizations," as of September 2026.[3] That positioning is broad by design, spanning a solo nutrition coach to a sport-specific training organization running skill drills and performance analytics for a whole roster.

Trainerize (now branded ABC Trainerize) positions itself around scale, citing more than 400,000 trainers and coaches using the platform to power their businesses, as of September 2026.[1] It serves independent coaches on its free and low-cost tiers, and it also names larger, multi-location businesses like gyms, studios, and corporate wellness programs among its customers, which lines up with its Studio Plus tier built specifically for locations with 500 or more clients.

Neither positioning is a hard rule, but going by where each company has invested, Everfit leans hardest into AI-assisted programming and native nutrition depth, while Trainerize offers the clearest path from a free single-client account up to a multi-location studio account.

Program Delivery Compared

Both platforms give you a workout builder, program templates, a video-based exercise library, habit tracking, native nutrition tools, messaging, scheduling, and payments, summarized below. The differences are in how AI-assisted programming is framed, how nutrition is packaged, and whether wearable integrations are named specifically.

Nutrition is where both platforms invest most, though built differently. Everfit's native nutrition tools include meal plan creation, recipe books, and MacroSnap, an AI feature that logs macros from a photo of a meal, with a separate Meal Plans and Recipe Books add-on priced at $33 to $39 a month for more depth, as of September 2026.[3] Trainerize includes meal tracking with photo and barcode food logging, plus a Smart Meal Planner and Flexible Meal Planner, with a more full-featured nutrition tier available as a paid add-on on some plans, as of September 2026.[2] Neither is a substitute for working with a registered dietitian on medical nutrition needs.

Client Management: Messaging, Scheduling, Payments, and White-Label

Everfit offers 1-1 messaging, voice notes, scheduled and automated messages, community forums, broadcast announcements, and client onboarding forms and questionnaires, with automation sequences priced as a separate add-on from $24 to $29 a month, as of September 2026.[3] Trainerize offers 1-1 and group messaging, automated messages, and voice messages, alongside in-app booking, class scheduling, prospect booking, and calendar sync, as of September 2026.[2] Both platforms process payments through Stripe, supporting one-time and recurring charges.

White-label goes to both. Everfit's Enterprise tier, custom-priced and covering 500 or more clients, includes a fully white-label app, API access, and a dedicated success manager, as of September 2026.[3] Trainerize also offers a Custom Branded App, as of September 2026, though its own features page doesn't spell out which paid tier it starts on, so confirm that directly with Trainerize if a branded app is a deciding factor.[1]

Wearables and the Client-Data Gap Both Share

Trainerize is specific about which platforms it syncs with. Its features page names Apple Watch, Withings, Garmin, Fitbit, and MyFitnessPal, and lists the client metrics a coach can see: steps, sleep, resting heart rate, body weight, body fat, lean mass, blood pressure, caloric burn, and daily activities, as of September 2026.[1]

Everfit's features page mentions integrations only in general terms, as a callout to "connect to all your favorite tools and devices," without naming specific wearable platforms or listing which client metrics a coach can view, as of September 2026.[3] That's not proof of no wearable support, just that the device list isn't confirmable from Everfit's own published materials, so ask Everfit directly if a particular device matters to how you coach.

What neither platform does, regardless of which wearables it connects to, is tie any of that data to a client's menstrual cycle. A sleep or heart-rate reading arrives as a raw number, not flagged against where a client is in her cycle, and neither platform pulls in lab results at all. That gap is worth knowing about: research shows vagally-mediated heart rate variability genuinely shifts across the menstrual cycle within the same person,[4] so a dip in a client's recovery numbers around certain points in her cycle may reflect normal hormonal fluctuation rather than overtraining, and a platform with no cycle context has no way to surface that distinction. It isn't a criticism of either product, since check-in and program delivery are what coaches come to these platforms for, and both do that job well. It's simply outside what either was built to cover.

Pricing

As of September 2026, Everfit's published plans start free ("Forever Free") for up to 5 clients on its Starter tier. Pro runs from about $16 to $290 a month scaling with client count from 5 to 300 clients, and Studio runs from about $88 to $358 a month for 50 to 500 clients. Enterprise is custom-priced for 500 or more clients and includes white-label, API access, and a dedicated success manager. Add-ons are priced separately: automation from $24 to $29 a month, on-demand content collections from $21 to $25 a month, payments and packages from $8 to $9 a month, and meal plans and recipe books from $33 to $39 a month. Signup takes about 5 minutes with no credit card required, per everfit.io/pricing.[3]

As of September 2026, Trainerize's published plans start free for a single client, with a Grow plan at $9 a month, Pro plans starting at $23 a month and scaling with client count up to 200 clients, and Studio Plus at $248 a month per location for larger rosters. A 30-day free trial with unlimited clients and no credit card required is available before committing. Some features, like advanced nutrition coaching and video coaching, cost extra as add-ons rather than being bundled into the base price, and annual billing saves 10 percent. Full current pricing is listed on trainerize.com/pricing.[1]

Both companies update pricing periodically, and Everfit's page currently shows a promotional strikethrough price on some tiers, so treat these as a snapshot, not numbers to quote back months from now.

Who Should Pick What

There's no single winner here, since the two platforms are solving slightly different problems.

Everfit tends to fit:

  • Coaches who want AI-assisted programming and deep native nutrition tools, including AI meal-photo logging, without switching apps
  • Sport coaches who want skill drills and performance analytics built into the same platform as general programming
  • Coaches comfortable picking and paying for specific add-ons (automation, on-demand content, meal plans) rather than one bundled price
  • Businesses that expect to grow into an Enterprise account with a dedicated success manager

Trainerize tends to fit:

  • Coaches who want to start free and grow into paid tiers as their client list grows
  • Coaches who want a specific, named list of wearable integrations to confirm before they commit
  • Coaches planning to add group programs, challenges, or community features alongside one-on-one work
  • Gym or studio owners managing multiple trainers and locations under one account

Still not sure? Most coaches can tell within a couple of weeks on the free tier whether the workflow fits how they coach. For more options, Trainerize alternatives for coaches who want more client context covers where other platforms land.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Everfit or Trainerize better for a solo online coach?

Both work for a solo coach starting out. Everfit's Starter plan is free for up to 5 clients, so you can run a small roster without paying anything. Trainerize's free Basic plan covers a single client, then its Grow plan at $9 a month picks up from there. Compare where each plan's next price step lands, not just the free tier.

Does Everfit or Trainerize include nutrition coaching?

Both build nutrition in natively, though the tools differ. Everfit includes meal plan creation, recipe books, and MacroSnap, an AI meal-photo logging feature, with a separate Meal Plans and Recipe Books add-on for more depth. Trainerize includes meal tracking with photo and barcode logging plus a Smart Meal Planner and Flexible Meal Planner, with a more full-featured nutrition tier available as an add-on on some plans. Neither replaces working with a registered dietitian on medical nutrition needs.

Do Everfit or Trainerize show client wearable or health data?

Trainerize is specific about this: it names Apple Watch, Withings, Garmin, Fitbit, and MyFitnessPal on its features page, pulling in steps, sleep, resting heart rate, weight, body fat, and more on most paid tiers. Everfit's features page only mentions integrations in general terms, without naming which wearable platforms sync, so it isn't possible to confirm a specific device list from the company's own materials as of September 2026. Neither platform organizes any of that data around a client's menstrual cycle or pulls in lab results, since that's outside what either was built to do.

Which is cheaper, Everfit or Trainerize?

Both have permanent free tiers, so the real cost comparison is at the paid tiers you'd actually grow into. Trainerize's Pro plan starts at $23 a month and scales with client count up to 200 clients, with Studio Plus at $248 a month per location for larger rosters. Everfit's Pro plan runs from about $16 to $290 a month scaling with client count, with several features, like automation and on-demand collections, priced as separate add-ons rather than bundled in.

Can I switch from Everfit to Trainerize, or the other way around, without losing client data?

Neither platform advertises a one-click migration tool for moving from a competitor. Switching means rebuilding your program templates and re-inviting clients, since workout libraries and client records don't transfer automatically between the two. Each company's support team can tell you what's exportable, like client contact lists, versus what has to be rebuilt from scratch.
