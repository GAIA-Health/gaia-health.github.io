Ovia vs What to Expect vs BabyCenter: 2026 Guide

Pick Ovia for kick counting, contraction timing and food-safety lookups. Pick What to Expect for week-by-week content with 3D videos and community groups, free. Pick BabyCenter for free Birth Clubs by due month plus 3D visuals, a kick counter and contraction timer. Veya suits tracking pregnancy beside sleep, mood and nutrition.

Quick Answer

Ovia is the tracking-tools pick: its App Store listing lists a kick and contraction counter and symptom and food-safety lookups, and employer or health-plan access unlocks premium tools. What to Expect has free week-by-week content, 3D weekly videos and community groups. BabyCenter has free Birth Clubs by due month, 3D fetal videos, a kick counter and a contraction timer. Both of those listings are published by Everyday Health, Inc., a Ziff Davis company. Here's how they compare:

  • If you want kick counting, contraction timing and food-safety lookups: Go with Ovia. Free to download; premium tools come through an employer or health plan.
  • If you want week-by-week content, 3D videos and community groups: Go with What to Expect. Free, per its listing.
  • If you want free Birth Clubs and 3D visuals: Go with BabyCenter. Free, with a kick counter and contraction timer too.
  • If you want pregnancy tracking alongside your whole health picture: Try Veya.

What is the real difference between Ovia, What to Expect and BabyCenter?

Ovia tracks. Its listing describes counting baby kicks and contractions as your due date approaches, plus lookup tools for symptoms and food safety. Ovia and Ovia Pregnancy are now one app, and it's branded Ovia Health by Labcorp.

What to Expect informs. Its listing describes week-by-week updates, 3D videos of baby development, a kick counter and community groups. It's built on the "What to Expect" book-series brand.

BabyCenter connects. Its listing describes Birth Clubs for people with due dates in the same month, 3D fetal development videos, a kick counter, a contraction timer and free childbirth classes, all free.

For tracking tools, Ovia is the closer comparison to a dedicated health app. For content and community, What to Expect and BabyCenter are the closer comparison to each other.

What changed when Ovia's apps merged under Labcorp?

Labcorp agreed to acquire Ovia Health in August 2021. According to the App Store version history, version 7.1.0 (April 29, 2025) merged Ovia and Ovia Pregnancy into one app, and version 7.5.0 (August 19, 2025) introduced the name Ovia Health by Labcorp. Existing Ovia Pregnancy users could log in with their existing email and password.

Pros

  • Kick counter and contraction timer both listed
  • Lookup tools for symptoms and food safety
  • Merged cycle and pregnancy app, so history carries across stages
  • Employer or health-plan members can enter plan information to unlock premium tools like health coaching

Cons

  • The listing doesn't show a standalone price. The listing shows the app as free to download with no in-app purchase prices, and says premium tools unlock through an employer or health plan. Check the current terms in the app.
  • Run by Labcorp. Because access can come through an employer or health plan, read the privacy policy for what is shared with a plan sponsor before you enter plan information.
  • The listing doesn't mention a community feature
  • The listing doesn't mention 3D visuals or wearable integration

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the real difference between Ovia, What to Expect, and BabyCenter?

All three can log a pregnancy and list a kick counter, but they lean differently. Ovia's listing emphasizes kick and contraction counting plus symptom and food-safety lookups, and premium tools through an employer or health plan. What to Expect's listing emphasizes week-by-week updates, 3D videos and community groups. BabyCenter's listing emphasizes Birth Clubs by due month, 3D fetal videos, a contraction timer and free childbirth classes. Sources: Ovia, What to Expect, BabyCenter (App Store, as of October 2026).

Are What to Expect and BabyCenter really owned by the same company?

Their App Store listings are both published by Everyday Health, Inc., and the copyright lines name Ziff Davis as the parent of each (What to Expect, BabyCenter). They operate as separate apps with different branding and features, so read each privacy policy on its own.

Is Ovia free?

It's free to download, and the App Store listing shows no in-app purchase prices. The listing says people with Ovia Health by Labcorp through an employer or health plan can enter plan information to unlock premium tools such as health coaching. The listing doesn't show a standalone price, so check the current terms in the app.

Which pregnancy app has the best privacy: Ovia, What to Expect, or BabyCenter?

We can't rank them, and none of the three is a simple privacy story. What to Expect and BabyCenter share a publisher and parent company, and Ovia access can run through an employer or health plan. Read each app's current privacy policy, and Mozilla's Privacy Not Included reviews of BabyCenter and What to Expect, before you enter health details.

Which is best for community support during pregnancy?

BabyCenter and What to Expect both list community groups; BabyCenter's listing highlights Birth Clubs for people with due dates in the same month, and What to Expect's lists groups for due dates in the same month too. Ovia's listing doesn't mention a community feature.

Can I use more than one of these apps at once?

A lot of people do. A common combination is BabyCenter or What to Expect for content and community, paired with a dedicated tracking app for kick counts, contraction timing, symptoms, and any lifestyle data you want to keep alongside your pregnancy. There's no rule against running two apps, and each does a different job well.
