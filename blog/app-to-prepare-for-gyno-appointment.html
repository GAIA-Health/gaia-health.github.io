Best Apps to Prep for a Gynecologist Appointment (2026)

Most period and health-tracking apps let you log your cycle. Far fewer let you turn that log into something you can actually hand a doctor. This guide compares Clue, Flo, Bearable, Apple Health, and Go Go Gaia on one thing only: what they let you export, summarize, print, or share before an appointment.

By Go Go Gaia Team • Published August 4, 2026 • 10 min read • App Comparison

Quick Answer: Best Export Feature?

There's no single best app for every appointment. It depends on whether you want a polished summary, raw data, or live provider access:

  • Best for a dated cycle report: Clue. Downloadable Cycle Report covering 6 cycles of dates, symptoms, and conditions.
  • Best for a symptom-pattern summary: Flo. Health Report bundles cycle and symptom trends, gated behind Flo Premium.
  • Best for raw, clinician-ready data: Bearable. Free CSV export of anything you've logged, no summary layer.
  • Best for direct provider access: Apple Health. Share with Provider syncs data automatically, but only to participating US health systems.
  • Best all-in-one summary (free): Go Go Gaia. Health summary pulls cycle, symptoms, mood, meds, sleep, and nutrition into one export, plus lab PDF upload and an invite-based sharing flow.

What to Look for in an Export Feature

Not every "report" feature does the same job. Before picking an app for this purpose, it helps to know what you're actually looking for:

A Summary, Not Just a Log

A month of daily entries is useful to you. A doctor with 15 minutes needs the condensed version: date ranges, patterns, and the handful of symptoms that matter. Look for an app that turns entries into a short document, not one that just lets you scroll further.

Format That's Easy to Hand Over

A PDF or printable page is easier to hand across a desk than an unlocked phone. A raw CSV export is useful if your provider specifically wants the underlying data, but it's not something you'd print and read cover to cover.

What's Actually Included

Some reports cover cycle dates only. Others fold in symptoms, medications, sleep, or nutrition. If your appointment is about more than your cycle, check whether the export reflects that or whether you'll need a second app for the rest.

Whether It's Free

A few of these features sit behind a subscription or one-time purchase. It's worth checking the export screen of whatever you already use before assuming you need a new app entirely.

The 5 Apps Compared

For a Dated Cycle Report: Clue

Best if you want: A downloadable PDF built specifically around your cycle history.

Key Features

  • Cycle Report available from the Analysis tab, covering roughly the last 6 cycles
  • Report includes cycle dates, tracked symptoms, and any conditions you've logged
  • Downloadable as a PDF you can print or attach to an email
  • Clue Connect allows sharing cycle data with a partner in real time

Strengths

  • The report is purpose-built for this exact use case: a clean, dated summary rather than raw screens
  • Six cycles of history gives a doctor enough range to see patterns, not just one month
  • Strong privacy track record, which matters if you're deciding what to trust with cycle data at all

Limitations

  • The Cycle Report has historically been offered as a separate purchase rather than a free base feature, so check current pricing in-app
  • Clue Connect is built for sharing with a partner, not for direct provider access
  • Report content is cycle and symptom data only. No nutrition, sleep, or medication tracking to include

Who Should Choose This

Clue is a good fit if you:

  • Want a report built specifically around cycle dates and patterns
  • Are comfortable paying for the report feature if it's not bundled into the free tier
  • Don't need medication, sleep, or nutrition data in the same document

Pricing: Free base app. Cycle Report availability and pricing should be confirmed in-app, as it has been offered as an add-on purchase.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

For a Symptom-Pattern Summary: Flo

Best if you want: A report that bundles cycle and symptom trends into something built to share with a clinician.

Key Features

  • Health Report feature summarizes cycle and symptom patterns over time
  • Report is designed to be shared with a doctor as part of an appointment
  • Flo for Partners allows free, real-time data sharing with a partner
  • Large symptom library to log against, since Flo tracks well beyond bleeding days

Strengths

  • The report format is aimed squarely at appointment prep, not just personal tracking
  • With 420M+ downloads, it's an app most people have already used, so there's no learning curve to get to the export
  • Large content library alongside tracking, if you also want educational context

Limitations

  • The Health Report sits behind Flo Premium, so it's not available on the free tier
  • Flo for Partners shares data with a partner, not a provider, so it doesn't replace the report for appointment prep
  • Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing period and pregnancy data with Facebook and Google. A related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry has preliminary court approval for a combined $59.5M settlement, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026. Flo has since added an Anonymous Mode

Who Should Choose This

Flo is a good fit if you:

  • Already use Flo and are willing to subscribe for the report feature
  • Want a report built specifically with the doctor-appointment use case in mind
  • Value a familiar interface over trying a new app just for the export

Pricing: Free (limited features), Flo Premium required for the Health Report, roughly $59.99/year.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

For Raw, Clinician-Ready Data: Bearable

Best if you want: Every logged data point handed over exactly as you entered it, with no summary layer in between.

Key Features

  • CSV export from My Data, with 3-month, 6-month, or all-time ranges
  • Export covers every custom metric you've set up, not just preset categories
  • Correlation ("Impacts") view can be screenshotted alongside the raw export
  • Fully customizable tracking, so the export reflects whatever you actually log, cycle included

Strengths

  • Free CSV export is a genuine strength here. Many clinician-facing tools charge for this exact feature
  • Because tracking is fully customizable, the export can include things other apps don't track at all, like symptoms specific to a chronic condition
  • Well suited to a provider who wants to review the underlying data rather than a pre-written summary

Limitations

  • No built-in PDF or printable summary. It's a CSV file, which most people won't print and hand over as-is
  • Not every in-app chart has a one-tap share button. Some require a screenshot to bring into a document
  • Not cycle-first, so if cycle dates are the main thing your doctor wants, you're relying on however you've set up that tracking category yourself

Who Should Choose This

Bearable is a good fit if you:

  • Track a wide range of custom symptoms or conditions alongside your cycle
  • Have a provider who's comfortable reviewing raw data rather than a summary
  • Want the export itself to be free, even if it takes more prep work to turn into something printable

Pricing: Free (generous tier, includes CSV export), Premium roughly $34.99/year or $6.99/month for additional features.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

For Direct Provider Access: Apple Health

Best if you want: Your data to sync to your provider automatically, without exporting or printing anything yourself.

Key Features

  • Share with Provider, built into iOS since iOS 15
  • Once connected, data syncs to the provider's system roughly once a day
  • Can include cycle tracking data logged in the Health app, alongside other health metrics
  • Free, with no separate app or subscription required

Strengths

  • It's the only option here with genuinely live, ongoing sharing rather than a one-time export
  • No extra app to set up if your provider already participates and you're already logging in Health
  • Built into the phone, so there's nothing to download or subscribe to

Limitations

  • Only works with US healthcare organizations that participate in Apple's health records sharing program. If your clinic isn't set up for it, this option isn't available to you
  • Depends on your cycle and symptom data already being logged in the Health app itself, whether manually or synced from another app
  • No standalone printable summary if your provider isn't part of the program. You'd fall back to screenshots

Who Should Choose This

Apple Health is a good fit if you:

  • Have an iPhone and a provider confirmed to participate in Apple's health records program
  • Already log cycle and health data in the Health app, whether directly or through a connected app
  • Want ongoing sharing rather than a one-time document for a single appointment

Pricing: Free, built into iOS.

Availability: iPhone only, requires a participating healthcare provider. Check with your clinic before your appointment.

For an All-in-One Summary: Go Go Gaia

Best if you want: One export that reflects your cycle, symptoms, mood, medications, sleep, and nutrition together, not just cycle dates.

Key Features

  • Generates a health summary that draws from cycle, mood, symptoms, medications and supplements, sleep, and nutrition tracked in the app (350+ metrics available to log)
  • Lab PDF and CSV upload, so uploaded lab results sit alongside your tracked data instead of living in a separate folder
  • Invite-based sharing, including a professional-connect option through the for-professionals portal, where a practitioner can view the data you've chosen to share
  • 1-click logging designed to keep daily entries fast enough to actually stick with between appointments

Strengths

  • Lab upload means blood work sits next to the symptoms and dates it relates to, rather than a separate PDF you have to remember to bring
  • The professional-connect option is closer to Apple Health's live sharing than a one-time export, but isn't limited to providers on a specific health records program

Limitations

  • iOS only, no Android version yet
  • Newer app with a smaller user base than Clue or Flo, so fewer people have tested the export against a wide range of clinics
  • Setting up 350+ possible metrics takes more initial setup than a single-purpose cycle app if you only want the basics

Who Should Choose This

Go Go Gaia is a good fit if you:

  • Want one export that covers more than your cycle
  • Have lab results you want tracked alongside your symptoms rather than separately
  • Want a free option for both the summary and the sharing flow

Pricing: Free for the health summary and core tracking.

Step 3: Making Your Decision

A quick way to match your situation to an app:

If you just need cycle dates and a clean PDF: Clue's Cycle Report is built exactly for that, as long as you're okay with it being a separate purchase.
If you already use Flo and don't mind subscribing: The Health Report is designed around this exact use case.
If your provider wants raw data, not a summary: Bearable's free CSV export covers anything you've logged, cycle or otherwise.
If your clinic supports it and you want ongoing sync: Apple Health's Share with Provider skips exporting altogether, when it's available to you.
If your appointment covers more than your cycle: Go Go Gaia's health summary folds in mood, meds, sleep, and nutrition alongside cycle data, with lab uploads included.

Privacy Considerations

Whatever you export is leaving the app, so it's worth a quick check before you share it:

  • Know what's in the file. A CSV export can include more raw detail than a curated summary. If you're emailing it ahead of your visit, skim it first.
  • Check who can see an in-app share. Invite-based sharing, like Go Go Gaia's professional connect or Clue Connect, should let you control exactly what's visible and to whom. Confirm that before sending an invite.
  • Delete what you don't need to keep. If you print a summary for a single appointment, that's one more physical copy of your health data. Shred or securely store it afterward.
  • Read the current privacy policy, not just the marketing page. Data practices change. If privacy is a priority alongside the export feature itself, it's worth checking each app's policy directly.

Getting the Most Out of Your Export

A few habits make any of these exports more useful once you get to the appointment:

  • Generate the export a day or two before, not in the waiting room. Give yourself time to check it's current and readable.
  • Know where the export button lives. Find it once, ahead of time, so you're not hunting through menus during the visit.
  • Bring 2 to 3 cycles of data if you can. A single cycle can be an outlier. Multiple cycles show what's actually consistent. Our appointment prep guide covers what's worth logging in that window.
  • Have a backup. If an export fails to generate or your provider's system doesn't accept it, a short written note with the same core details still works.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which app has the best export feature for a doctor appointment?

It depends on what you need in the record. Clue's Cycle Report covers 6 cycles of dates, symptoms, and conditions as a downloadable PDF. Flo's Health Report bundles cycle and symptom patterns but sits behind Flo Premium. Bearable exports raw CSV data across any custom metric you've tracked, which suits clinicians who want the full data rather than a summary. Go Go Gaia generates a health summary that pulls in cycle, symptoms, mood, meds, sleep, and nutrition together, plus lets you upload lab PDFs so they sit alongside your tracked data.

Can I share my tracking app data directly with my doctor?

A few apps support direct in-app sharing rather than just exporting a file. Go Go Gaia has an invite flow, including a professional-connect option, so a practitioner can view the data you choose to share. Apple Health's Share with Provider feature can sync your data to a provider's system roughly once a day, but only works with healthcare organizations that participate in Apple's health records program. Clue and Flo are built around exporting or printing a report rather than live provider access.

Is Apple Health enough to bring to a gynecologist appointment?

It can work if your cycle data is already logged in the Health app and your provider's system supports Apple's health records sharing. The catch is that Share with Provider only connects to participating US healthcare organizations, so it won't work everywhere. If your clinic isn't set up for it, you're back to screenshots or a written summary, which is where a dedicated tracking app's export tends to be more reliable.

Do I need a paid subscription to export my data?

It varies by app. Flo's Health Report requires Flo Premium. Clue's Cycle Report has historically been offered as a purchase separate from the base app, so check the current price in-app before your visit. Bearable's CSV export is part of its free tier. Go Go Gaia's health summary is available without a subscription. If cost is a factor, it's worth checking the export screen of whatever app you already use before assuming you need to switch.

What's the difference between a raw data export and a health summary?

A raw export, like Bearable's CSV, gives you every logged entry with no interpretation. That's useful if your provider wants to review the full data themselves. A summary, like Clue's Cycle Report or Go Go Gaia's health summary, condenses months of entries into a shorter document built to be read in a few minutes. Neither is strictly better. A summary works well for a short appointment, and a raw export works well if your provider specifically asks for the underlying data.

Can I track more than my period in these apps?

Clue and Flo are built cycle-first, with symptom logging layered on top. Bearable is built to track anything you define, cycle included. Go Go Gaia tracks cycle, mood, symptoms, medications and supplements, sleep, and nutrition together, so a single export reflects more than cycle dates alone. Which setup you want depends on whether your appointment is purely about your cycle or covers a broader set of symptoms.

Should I bring a printed report or just show the app on my phone?

A printed or PDF summary tends to work better than handing over your unlocked phone, mostly because it's faster for your provider to read and easier for them to keep with your chart if needed. If your app doesn't generate a printable summary, a written note covering the same basics, like last period date, cycle range, and dated symptoms, still beats reconstructing it from memory in the room.

Final Thoughts

Most tracking apps assume you'll be the only one who ever reads your log. The ones in this guide are the exceptions, built with an actual handoff in mind, whether that's a printed page, a CSV file, or a live sync to your provider's system.

If you're not sure where to start, check the app you already use first. There's a decent chance the export you need already exists in a menu you haven't opened yet.

A doctor can read a one-page summary in under a minute. A scroll of screens takes longer.

If your current app doesn't generate one, it might be worth trying one that does before your next visit.
