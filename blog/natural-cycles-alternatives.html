Natural Cycles Alternatives for Cycle Tracking in 2026

This guide is for one specific situation: you like Natural Cycles' temperature-based approach, or you're tired of the subscription price, but contraception isn't your goal here. Natural Cycles is FDA-cleared as a birth control method, and that clearance is the whole reason it works the way it does. This guide doesn't cover using it, or anything else, that way. If you just want to track your cycle, whether that's for symptom awareness, planning ahead, or curiosity, this guide covers 8 apps people switch to instead, what each one actually measures, and how they compare on price and features.

Why People Look for Natural Cycles Alternatives

Two groups tend to land here. The first likes that Natural Cycles builds predictions from a measured signal, basal body temperature, and wants that feel minus the contraception framing and price tag. The second just wants a cheaper or free tracker and doesn't need the specific clearance because contraception isn't the goal right now. Both are reasonable. It's a well-studied product built for a specific job, and this guide is for when that job isn't the one you need done.

Natural Cycles Is FDA-Cleared Birth Control

Worth stating plainly first: Natural Cycles is an FDA-cleared Class II medical device, first cleared in 2018, with later clearances covering wearable temperature inputs.[1] The company reports 98% effectiveness with perfect use and 93% with typical use.[1] None of the 8 apps below have that clearance or are presented here as a substitute for it. This guide doesn't weigh contraception options or cover how to use any product as birth control. That conversation belongs with you and a healthcare provider.

When Staying With Natural Cycles Makes Sense

Setting contraception aside, since that isn't something this guide covers, there are tracking reasons to stay put. You may want temperature-confirmed predictions over a calendar estimate, you may be comfortable with the daily thermometer habit and not want to lose your calibrated history, or you may use the NC° Band or a connected wearable for automatic logging. If none of that fits, here are the alternatives.

The 8 Alternatives Compared

Reminder before the list: none of the apps below are FDA-cleared as contraception.

Clue: Deep Symptom Logging, Clean Privacy Record

Broad symptom tracking from a company with a long privacy record, built from your logged history rather than a temperature method. Clue tracks 200+ symptom tags, syncs with Apple Watch and Fitbit, and is Berlin-based, covered by EU GDPR.[2] Predictions come from your calendar and symptoms, not a confirmed physiological signal, and deeper pattern views sit behind Clue Plus.

Pricing: Free, Clue Plus $9.99/month or $39.99/year.[2] iOS / Android. See our Clue vs Natural Cycles comparison on the FDA-clearance confusion around Clue's discontinued birth control product.

Flo: Biggest Community, Free Pregnancy Mode

The most-used tracker here, with the widest content library and a free pregnancy mode, also not built around temperature. Predictions come from logged history with AI assistance, and free users see ads. Flo's history is worth knowing: it shared health data with third parties including Meta from 2016 to 2019, settled with the FTC in 2021, and added Anonymous Mode in 2022. A related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry got preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a $59.5 million settlement, with a final hearing set for October 29, 2026.[3]

Pricing: Free (ads), Premium up to ~$59.99/year, with cheaper shorter plans listed in-app.[4] iOS / Android. More in our Flo vs Natural Cycles and Flo Alternatives guides.

Apple Health: Free, Already on Your iPhone

Basic cycle tracking with no new account, plus a temperature signal if you own a newer Watch. Apple Watch Series 8 or later, Ultra, or SE 3 gather wrist temperature overnight for retrospective ovulation estimates, once Sleep Focus has run 4+ hours a night for about 5 nights to set a baseline and roughly 2 full cycles have passed.[5][6] Data stays on-device, with encrypted iCloud backup. No content library, community, or pregnancy mode, and iPhone only.

Pricing: Free, built into iOS.

Oura: Overnight Temperature Trends From a Ring

Temperature-based cycle insight bundled with sleep and recovery data, if you already wear or plan to wear an Oura Ring. Cycle Insights estimates cycle phase, period, and ovulation from overnight temperature trends plus heart rate and HRV, which Oura reports as more than 96% accurate, trained on over 42 million nights of cycle data.[7] You need the ring itself, a separate purchase, plus an active Membership, and symptom logging is thinner than a dedicated cycle app.

Pricing: Membership $5.99/month or $69.99/year, on top of the ring.[8] More on the mechanics in how wearables detect your cycle.

Tempdrop: A Temperature Sensor Without the Contraception Framing

The same overnight temperature signal Natural Cycles is built on, measured by a wearable armband instead of an oral thermometer, in an app not marketed as birth control. An algorithm filters out sleep disruptions from the reading.[9] The free app tier covers daily readings and trend charts. Tempdrop Premium adds automated ovulation prediction.[9] Of everything here, it's the closest measurement match to Natural Cycles, minus the clearance.

Pricing: Sensor sold separately from the free app, with optional Premium. Check tempdrop.com for current pricing.

Go Go Gaia: Cycle Tracking Next to Mood, Sleep, and Food

Your cycle tracked alongside mood, sleep, fitness, and nutrition, with wearable temperature as one input among several rather than the whole method. This is our app, so factor that in. It pulls wearable data automatically via Apple Health, including Apple Watch, Oura, Garmin, and WHOOP, and surfaces correlation insights from your own data. Core tracking is free, no ads or data selling. Honest limits: iOS only, a much smaller community than Flo or Clue, and not FDA-cleared as contraception.

Pricing: Free for core features, Premium $9.99/month or $49.99/year.[10] iOS App Store

Premom: Free Ovulation Test Strip Reading

A free app that reads ovulation test strips through your phone's camera, alongside basic cycle tracking, without a temperature-first method. Photograph an LH strip for an automated reading, plus optional manual BBT charting and a paid Premium tier.[11] Useful if you already use test strips, though temperature entry is manual.

Pricing: Free, optional Premom Premium. Check premom.com, or see our Premom Alternatives guide.

Stardust: Daily Moon-Phase and Hormone Content

A different kind of daily ritual than a temperature routine: a moon calendar and daily hormone-stage narrative, plus friend syncing.[12] Predictions are calendar and symptom-based rather than measured, symptom tracking is lighter than Clue's or Flo's, and there's no pregnancy mode.

Pricing: Free, Stardust Plus roughly $6.99/month or $24.99 to $39.99/year depending on the offer.[13] More in our Stardust Alternatives guide.

Moving Your Data to a New App

Switch in order so you don't lose history. Write down recent cycle lengths, period start dates, and temperature patterns before you cancel anything. Set up the new app first, entering the last 2 or 3 dates by hand for a baseline, and give it about 2 cycles to calibrate. Then cancel the Natural Cycles subscription separately in the App Store or Google Play, since canceling in-app doesn't always stop billing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is any Natural Cycles alternative also birth control?

No. None of the apps here, including Go Go Gaia, are FDA-cleared to prevent pregnancy. Natural Cycles holds that specific clearance, first granted in 2018, with later clearances covering wearable temperature inputs. Everything in this guide is for tracking, not for contraception decisions. That conversation belongs with a healthcare provider.

Which alternative is most similar to Natural Cycles' temperature method?

Tempdrop is closest: an armband taking your temperature overnight, the same signal Natural Cycles uses, via a sensor instead of a thermometer. Apple Health's wrist temperature feature and Oura's ring do something similar. None of the three are cleared as contraception, so the similarity is in the measurement, not the medical claim.

Can Apple Watch or Oura replace a basal thermometer for cycle tracking?

They measure a related signal differently. Apple Watch Series 8 and later, Ultra, and SE 3 gather wrist temperature overnight for retrospective ovulation estimates after consistent wear. Oura does something similar through the ring, combined with heart rate. Both are wellness features on hardware you may already own, not a diagnostic or contraceptive tool.

Is Go Go Gaia a good fit if I'm leaving Natural Cycles?

Depends why you're leaving. If price was the sticking point and contraception isn't a need, core tracking is free. If you liked seeing your body's data drive predictions, Go Go Gaia pulls wearable temperature alongside your cycle rather than building the method around it. If daily temperature logging is what you want to keep as your primary method, Tempdrop stays closer to that.

Will I lose my cycle history if I switch away from Natural Cycles?

Data won't transfer automatically. Write down recent cycle lengths and period start dates before you switch, enter the last 2 or 3 by hand in the new app, and give it a couple of cycles to calibrate.

Final Thoughts

Natural Cycles is the app in this space that holds the birth control clearance, and this guide isn't the place to weigh that decision. Moving on for a tracking reason, match the app to what drew you there: symptom logging and privacy, Clue. Community and free pregnancy tracking, Flo. A temperature signal without the subscription or framing, Tempdrop, or Apple Health and Oura if you own the hardware. Cycle next to sleep, mood, and food, that's Go Go Gaia. A daily ritual, Stardust or Premom.

Still deciding? Pick one and log for 2 cycles.
