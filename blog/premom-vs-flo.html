Premom vs Flo: OPK Scanning vs Cycle Tracking (2026)

Premom and Flo get compared a lot, and the confusion usually starts the same way: you test with an ovulation predictor kit, Premom tells you one thing, and Flo's calendar tells you another. This guide covers what each app actually does, why their ovulation dates can genuinely disagree, Premom's 2023 FTC order laid out factually, and a third option worth knowing about.

Quick Answer

Premom and Flo solve different problems. Premom is built around ovulation predictor kits (OPKs): you photograph the test strip, and the app quantifies your LH level and tells you if you're at low, high, or peak fertility. Flo is a broader cycle tracker with the largest community in the category, roughly 77 million active users, and Premom is more of a niche tool by comparison. Here's how they compare:

  • If you test with OPK strips regularly and want the results scanned and quantified: Go with Premom. Strip photography, LH quantification, a large TTC community.
  • If you want the deepest general cycle tracking and biggest community: Go with Flo. ~77M active users, an AI health assistant, and a large content library.
  • If you want LH logging folded into mood, sleep, and nutrition tracking: Try Go Go Gaia.

Premom: What You Get

Premom is built around ovulation predictor kits. Instead of asking you to eyeball whether a test line looks darker than the control line, the app's camera reads the strip and quantifies your LH level, giving you a low, high, or peak reading. It's a genuinely useful piece of software for anyone who tests daily, and it's built and maintained by Easy Healthcare Corporation, the same company behind Easy@Home test strips.

Features

  • OPK strip scanning: photograph an ovulation test and get a quantified LH reading (low, high, or peak) instead of reading the line yourself
  • Pregnancy test strip scanning as well, using the same camera-analysis approach
  • Cycle and symptom logging, plus BBT charting
  • Community forums for TTC users, including PCOS-specific discussion
  • Premium tier: advanced cycle reports, personalized content, expert webinars, and an Ask an Expert feature

Strengths

The strip scanning is genuinely useful. If you're testing daily with OPK strips, having the app read and quantify the line for you removes a lot of the guesswork that comes with judging line darkness by eye, especially early in a testing cycle when the line is faint.

It works well with irregular cycles. Because Premom's core signal comes from an actual hormone test each time you use it, rather than a calendar prediction, it doesn't depend on your cycle being predictable the way calendar-based apps do.

The community is TTC-specific. Premom's forums skew toward people actively trying to conceive, including a meaningful PCOS-focused subset, which can be a more targeted fit than a general cycle-tracking community.

Limitations

The 2023 FTC order is the big thing to know about. We cover the details below, but in short: Premom's developer shared health and device data with third parties, including China-based analytics firms, without proper disclosure, and settled with the FTC as a result. Premom has since updated its data practices.

It's narrower than a general cycle tracker. Premom doesn't offer Flo's depth of symptom tracking, its content library, or a broad community that isn't fertility-focused. If you're not using OPK strips, a lot of Premom's core value doesn't apply to you.

No mood, sleep, fitness, or nutrition tracking. Premom stays focused on fertility signals and cycle basics.

Pricing

Premom Premium: ~$39.99/year as of 2026. Adds advanced cycle reports, personalized content, and access to fertility experts through webinars and an Ask an Expert feature. Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

Flo: What You Get

Flo is the most-used period tracker in the category, with around 77 million active users and over 300 million registered accounts. It covers cycle, symptom, and pregnancy tracking broadly, and ovulation is one piece of a much bigger feature set rather than the whole product.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with calendar and algorithm-based predictions
  • Manual ovulation test logging: you enter your own result, with no strip photography
  • AI Health Assistant that checks in on symptoms like cramps, fatigue, and mood
  • Thousands of health articles reviewed by 100+ medical professionals
  • Secret Chats: anonymous community discussions, with about 7 million people chatting monthly
  • Free pregnancy mode with week-by-week tracking

Strengths

The community is the largest in the category. Secret Chats put you in anonymous discussions with millions of other users, useful if you want to compare notes on TTC beyond just fertility signals.

The content library is extensive. Thousands of articles cover cycle, fertility, and general health topics, reviewed by medical professionals.

It's a broader health app than Premom. Symptom tracking, pregnancy mode, and general cycle content all sit inside one app, so you're not switching between tools once you're past the fertility-testing phase.

Limitations

Privacy is the big concern. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 for sharing period and pregnancy data with Facebook and Google without user consent. A related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025 for its role in receiving that data. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode and open-sourced it.

No strip scanning. If you're testing with OPK strips regularly, Flo asks you to read and enter the result yourself rather than analyzing the strip for you.

Ads and paywalls. Free users see ads, and the AI Health Assistant, ad-free experience, and full content library sit behind Flo Premium (~$59.99/year as of 2026).

Pricing

Free: Cycle and symptom tracking, manual ovulation test logging, pregnancy mode, community access. Includes ads.

Flo Premium: ~$12.99/month or ~$59.99/year as of 2026. Adds the AI Health Assistant, an ad-free experience, and full content library access. Check current pricing in-app.

Why Your Ovulation Date Might Not Match Between Apps

This is the question that brings a lot of people to this comparison, so it's worth walking through the mechanics plainly. Premom and Flo can land on different ovulation dates because they're often built from different kinds of information, not because one is simply wrong.

Premom's estimate is built from a direct hormone reading. When you scan an OPK strip, the app is quantifying the actual LH level in that specific urine sample at that specific time. LH surges sharply 24 to 36 hours before ovulation, so a peak reading is a real physiological signal from that day, not a forecast.

Flo's default estimate is built from a calendar and algorithm model. Unless you're manually entering your own OPK results into Flo, its ovulation window comes from your logged cycle length, past cycle history, and symptoms, run through a prediction model. That's an estimate based on patterns, not a same-day hormone measurement.

Both approaches have real limits. A single LH test can miss a surge if you test at the wrong time of day or your surge is unusually short. A calendar prediction can miss the mark entirely if your cycle is irregular, since it's extrapolating from history rather than reading anything from your body that day. Neither method is infallible, and using an app is not the same as clinical fertility testing.

If you're logging OPK results into both apps and still seeing a mismatch, it's worth checking whether one app is using your manually entered test result and the other is defaulting to its own calendar prediction instead. That's the most common source of the disagreement, not a bug in either app.

Premom's 2023 FTC Order: What Actually Happened

Here's the factual record, with dates, so you can decide for yourself what it means for you.

May 2023: The FTC announced that Premom's developer, Illinois-based Easy Healthcare Corporation, had shared users' sensitive health and device data with third parties without proper disclosure or consent. That included sharing data with AppsFlyer and Google, as well as with two China-based analytics firms, Umeng and Jiguang, through SDKs integrated into the app. The shared data reportedly included health information, social media account details, precise geolocation, and device and Wi-Fi network identifiers. The FTC's complaint also cited a violation of the Health Breach Notification Rule for failing to notify consumers about the unauthorized disclosures.

The settlement: Easy Healthcare agreed to pay a $200,000 penalty. Under the proposed order, the company is barred from sharing users' personal health data with third parties for advertising purposes, and it must obtain user consent before sharing health data for any other purpose. It's also required to tell consumers clearly how their data will be used.

Since the settlement: Premom has updated its data-sharing practices and agreed to delete the data that was previously shared with those third parties. The strip-scanning feature itself, which is the core reason people use the app, wasn't the subject of the FTC's complaint. The issue was what happened to data in the background, not how the scanning technology works.

To be clear about what this doesn't mean: it doesn't mean Premom's core scanning feature is unreliable or that the app stopped working the way people expect. It does mean the app had a real, documented lapse in how it handled sensitive health data, and that's worth knowing before you decide how comfortable you are logging fertility data there. Read Premom's current privacy policy yourself if this is a deciding factor for you.

Which Should You Choose?

There's no single "best" app here, and a lot of people genuinely use both for different reasons. Here's the honest breakdown:

Choose Premom if:

  • You test with OPK strips regularly and want the line read and quantified for you
  • Your cycle is irregular and you want a same-day hormone signal rather than a calendar guess
  • You want a TTC-specific community, including PCOS-focused discussion
  • You're comfortable with Premom's post-FTC privacy practices

Choose Flo if:

  • You want the deepest general feature set and the largest community of people tracking cycles
  • Health education and medically reviewed articles matter to you
  • You want a free pregnancy mode once you conceive
  • You're comfortable manually entering your own OPK results instead of scanning them

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Premom and Flo give me different ovulation days?

They're measuring different things. Premom reads an actual LH test strip, so its ovulation estimate is built from a real hormone surge detected in your urine that day. Flo's default ovulation estimate comes from a calendar and algorithm model based on your logged cycle history and symptoms, not a direct hormone reading, unless you manually enter your own OPK results into Flo. When the two disagree, it's usually because one is reading a physical test and the other is predicting from patterns.

Is Premom safe to use after the FTC order?

In May 2023, the FTC announced that Premom's developer, Easy Healthcare Corporation, had shared users' health and location data with third parties, including two China-based analytics firms, AppsFlyer, and Google, without proper disclosure. As part of the settlement, the company paid a $200,000 penalty and is now barred from sharing users' health data with third parties for advertising without consent. Premom has updated its data-sharing practices and agreed to delete the data that was shared. Its core strip-scanning feature is unrelated to the violation, so whether the update changes your comfort level is a personal call worth making with the current privacy policy in front of you.

Does Flo scan OPK test strips like Premom does?

Flo lets you log ovulation test results manually, choosing a result like negative, positive, or peak, but it doesn't photograph and analyze the test strip itself. Premom's strip-scanning and LH-quantification feature is specific to Premom among the major cycle apps.

How much does Premom or Flo Premium cost?

As of 2026, Premom Premium runs about $39.99/year and adds advanced cycle reports, personalized content, and access to fertility experts through webinars and an Ask an Expert feature. Flo Premium runs about $12.99/month or roughly $59.99/year and adds an AI Health Assistant, an ad-free experience, and full content library access. Both apps have usable free tiers, and pricing can shift with promotions, so check the current price inside each app.

Can Go Go Gaia log LH test results like Premom?

Go Go Gaia lets you log LH test results alongside your cycle, mood, sleep, and other health data. It doesn't photograph and analyze the test strip the way Premom does, so you read your own result and enter it. The tradeoff is that your LH readings sit next to your broader health picture, which can surface patterns a fertility-only app doesn't have the data to show.

Which is better for irregular cycles, Premom or Flo?

Both apps support irregular-cycle tracking, and neither can predict an irregular cycle from a calendar alone. Premom's strip-scanning approach has an advantage here because it reads an actual hormone surge each time you test rather than relying on your cycle history to guess, which matters more when your cycle length varies. Flo's PCOS-adjacent content and symptom tracking are broader, but its default predictions are still calendar-based unless you're logging your own OPK results into the app.
