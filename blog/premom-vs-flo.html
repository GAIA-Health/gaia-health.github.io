Premom vs Flo: OPK Scanning vs Cycle Tracking (2026)

Choose Premom if you test with ovulation strips and want the app to read and quantify them. Choose Flo if you want broader cycle, symptom and pregnancy tracking and don't mind typing in test results yourself. Veya suits people who want LH logging next to sleep, mood and nutrition. Their ovulation dates differ because they use different inputs.

Quick Answer

Premom reads your LH test strip through the camera; Flo logs the result you enter and covers more of your health. Premom's App Store listing describes scanning ovulation tests to quantify LH levels, and Flo's listing describes LH-test and basal body temperature logging alongside symptom, pregnancy and perimenopause tracking (as of October 2026). Here's how they compare:

  • If you test with OPK strips regularly and want the results scanned and quantified: Go with Premom. Strip photography, LH quantification, a community for sharing tests and BBT charts.
  • If you want broader cycle tracking and a very large user base: Go with Flo. About 2 million App Store ratings, an AI Health Assistant and Secret Chats in Premium, and symptom, pregnancy and perimenopause tracking.
  • If you want LH logging folded into mood, sleep, and nutrition tracking: Try Veya.

What does Premom do well, and where does it fall short?

Premom is built around ovulation predictor kits. Instead of asking you to eyeball whether a test line looks darker than the control line, the app's camera reads the strip and quantifies your LH level; its App Store listing describes scanning your ovulation tests to quantify LH levels and estimate ovulation timing. It's built by Easy Healthcare Corporation, the company behind Easy@Home test strips.

Features

  • OPK strip scanning: photograph an ovulation test and get a quantified LH reading (low, high, or peak) instead of reading the line yourself
  • Pregnancy tracking features alongside ovulation tracking
  • Cycle and symptom logging, plus BBT charting
  • A community where users ask questions and share fertility tests and BBT charts
  • A Premium subscription; the listing mentions a Cycle Analysis Report

Strengths

The strip scanning is genuinely useful. If you're testing daily with OPK strips, having the app read and quantify the line for you removes a lot of the guesswork that comes with judging line darkness by eye, especially early in a testing cycle when the line is faint.

It doesn't depend on a calendar prediction. Because Premom's core signal comes from an actual hormone test each time you use it, rather than a forecast, it isn't limited by how predictable your cycle is the way calendar-based predictions are.

The community is fertility-focused. Per its listing, Premom's community lets users ask questions and share test results and BBT charts, which can be a more targeted fit than a general cycle-tracking community.

Limitations

The 2023 FTC order is the big thing to know about. We cover the details below, but in short: the FTC alleged Premom's developer shared health data and device information with third parties, including two China-based analytics firms, without telling users, and agreed to a proposed order with penalties and data-sharing limits.

It's narrower than a general cycle tracker. Premom doesn't offer Flo's depth of symptom tracking, its content library, or a broad community that isn't fertility-focused. If you're not using OPK strips, a lot of Premom's core value doesn't apply to you.

Narrower scope. Premom's listing centers on fertility signals and cycle basics, with no mention of mood, sleep, fitness or nutrition tracking.

Pricing

Free: LH scanning, cycle and symptom logging, community access (the app is free to download, per its listing).

Premom Premium: an auto-renewing subscription. The App Store listing shows several Premium price points and doesn't label which is which, so check the current price in the app (as of October 2026).

What does Flo offer, and what are its drawbacks?

Flo is one of the most widely used period trackers; its App Store listing shows about 2 million ratings (as of October 2026). It covers cycle, symptom, pregnancy and perimenopause tracking, and ovulation is one piece of a much bigger feature set rather than the whole product.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with calendar and algorithm-based predictions
  • Manual LH test and basal body temperature logging: you enter your own result, with no strip photography
  • A 24/7 Health Assistant and Symptom Checker in Premium (the Symptom Checker isn't available in the UK, EU and some other regions)
  • Daily insights and expert videos in Premium
  • Secret Chats (anonymous community discussions), listed as a Premium feature
  • A pregnancy mode with weekly development updates, plus Anonymous Mode and a Perimenopause Score

Strengths

Secret Chats give you a community. Per the listing, Secret Chats are part of Premium, useful if you want to compare notes with other users on TTC beyond just fertility signals.

There's a content layer. The listing mentions daily insights and expert videos in Premium.

It's a broader health app than Premom. Symptom tracking (70+ symptoms and moods, per the listing), pregnancy mode and perimenopause tracking all sit inside one app, so you're not switching between tools once you're past the fertility-testing phase.

Limitations

Privacy is the big concern. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021, which alleged it shared health data with marketing and analytics firms including Facebook and Google despite promising to keep it private. A related class action against Flo, Google and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final approval hearing on October 29, 2026. Separately, a federal jury found Meta liable in August 2025 for collecting data from Flo users. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, and Mozilla's July 2026 review scored Flo 7 out of 10.

No strip scanning. If you're testing with OPK strips regularly, Flo asks you to read and enter the result yourself rather than analyzing the strip for you.

Paywalled features. The AI-style Health Assistant, Secret Chats and daily insights sit in Flo Premium, and reviewers on the listing mention frequent prompts to upgrade.

Pricing

Free: cycle and period tracking with core tools; see the listing for which features need Premium.

Flo Premium: a paid subscription. The App Store listing shows several in-app price options without labeling monthly versus annual, so check the current price in the app (as of October 2026).

Why do Premom and Flo give different ovulation dates?

This is the question that brings a lot of people to this comparison, so it's worth walking through the mechanics plainly. Premom and Flo can land on different ovulation dates because they're often built from different kinds of information, not because one is simply wrong.

Premom's estimate is built from a direct hormone reading. When you scan an OPK strip, the app is quantifying the LH level in that specific urine sample at that specific time. LH rises ahead of ovulation, so a peak reading is a real physiological signal from that day, not a forecast.

Flo's default estimate is built from a calendar and algorithm model. Unless you're entering your own OPK results into Flo, its ovulation window comes from your logged cycle length, past cycle history and symptoms, run through a prediction model. That's an estimate based on patterns, not a same-day hormone measurement.

Both approaches have real limits. A single LH test can miss a surge if you test at the wrong time of day or your surge is unusually short. A calendar prediction can miss the mark entirely if your cycle is irregular, since it's extrapolating from history rather than reading anything from your body that day. Neither method is infallible, and using an app is not the same as clinical fertility testing.

If you're logging OPK results into both apps and still seeing a mismatch, it's worth checking whether one app is using your manually entered test result and the other is defaulting to its own calendar prediction instead. That's the most common source of the disagreement, not a bug in either app.

What did the FTC's 2023 order against Premom say?

Here's the factual record, with dates, so you can decide for yourself what it means for you.

May 17, 2023: The FTC announced that Premom's developer, Easy Healthcare Corporation, shared users' sensitive health data with AppsFlyer and Google, and shared social media account information, precise geolocation and device and Wi-Fi identifiers with two China-based analytics firms, Umeng and Jiguang, through SDKs built into the app. The FTC said Premom failed to notify users, violating the Health Breach Notification Rule.

The proposed order: Easy Healthcare would pay a $100,000 civil penalty and a total of $100,000 to Connecticut, the District of Columbia and Oregon. It would be permanently barred from sharing users' personal health data with third parties for advertising, would need consent before sharing health data for other purposes, and must seek deletion of data already shared. The FTC's announcement said the order needed federal court approval to take effect.

What it doesn't cover: The FTC's announcement concerned background data sharing, not the strip-scanning feature, which wasn't the subject of its allegations. The order also required Easy Healthcare to put comprehensive privacy and security programs in place.

To be clear about what this doesn't mean: it isn't a finding about whether Premom's scanning works. It is a documented issue in how the company handled sensitive health data, and it's worth knowing before you decide how comfortable you are logging fertility data there. Read Premom's current privacy policy yourself if this is a deciding factor for you.

Which should you choose, Premom or Flo?

There's no single right app here, and a lot of people use both for different reasons. Here's the honest breakdown:

Choose Premom if:

  • You test with OPK strips regularly and want the line read and quantified for you
  • Your cycle is irregular and you want a same-day hormone signal rather than a calendar guess
  • You want a fertility-focused community where people share tests and BBT charts
  • You're comfortable with Premom's post-FTC privacy practices

Choose Flo if:

  • You want a broader feature set and a very large user base
  • Daily insights and expert videos in Premium appeal to you
  • You want a pregnancy mode once you conceive
  • You're comfortable manually entering your own OPK results instead of scanning them

  • You want LH test results sitting next to your mood, sleep, and nutrition data
  • You want to see how your lifestyle habits connect to your cycle over time
  • You're okay entering your own test result instead of scanning it
  • You're on iOS (or okay using the web app on other platforms)

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Premom and Flo give me different ovulation days?

They're measuring different things. Premom reads an actual LH test strip, so its ovulation estimate is built from a hormone reading taken that day. Flo's default ovulation estimate comes from a calendar and algorithm model based on your logged cycle history and symptoms, unless you enter your own OPK results into Flo. When the two disagree, it's usually because one is reading a physical test and the other is predicting from patterns.

Is Premom safe to use after the FTC order?

In May 2023, the FTC announced that Premom's developer, Easy Healthcare Corporation, had shared users' health data and device information with third parties, including AppsFlyer, Google and two China-based analytics firms, without notifying users. The proposed order included a $100,000 civil penalty, $100,000 to three states, a bar on sharing health data for advertising, and a consent requirement. Whether that history changes your comfort level is personal; read Premom's current privacy policy and decide with that in hand.

Does Flo scan OPK test strips like Premom does?

Flo lets you log LH test results manually, per its App Store listing, but that listing doesn't describe photographing and analyzing the test strip. Premom's listing describes scanning ovulation tests to quantify LH levels.

How much does Premom or Flo Premium cost?

Both apps are free to download with a paid Premium subscription. Their App Store listings (Premom, Flo) show several in-app price options without labeling them, so check the current price inside each app (as of October 2026).

Can Veya log LH test results like Premom?

Veya lets you log LH test results alongside your cycle, mood, sleep, and other health data. It doesn't photograph and analyze the test strip the way Premom does, so you read your own result and enter it. The tradeoff is that your LH readings sit next to your broader health picture, which can surface patterns a fertility-only app doesn't have the data to show.

Which is better for irregular cycles, Premom or Flo?

Both apps support logging for irregular cycles, and a calendar alone can't predict an irregular cycle well. Premom's strip-scanning approach reads a hormone signal each time you test rather than relying on cycle history, which can matter more when your cycle length varies. Flo's symptom tracking is broader. Neither replaces a conversation with a clinician about your cycle.
