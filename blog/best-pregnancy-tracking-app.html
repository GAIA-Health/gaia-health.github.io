What should you look for in a pregnancy app?

A good pregnancy app does four jobs: explains your baby's development week by week, connects you with other parents, logs your own symptoms and health data, and offers practical tools such as a kick counter and contraction timer. BabyCenter, Ovia, Pregnancy+ and Veya list kick and contraction tools, while What to Expect leans on editorial content. Pregnancy apps fall into three broad categories, and knowing what each does helps you pick.

Content and Education

What's happening at week 14? Is this symptom normal? Can I eat sushi? Content-focused apps answer these questions with week-by-week guides, articles reviewed by medical professionals, and baby development updates. If you're a first-time parent, this is often the most valuable feature.

Community and Support

Pregnancy can feel isolating, especially if you're the first in your friend group to go through it. Apps with Birth Clubs or due-date groups connect you with others at the same stage. The quality of the community varies a lot between apps.

Health Tracking

How's your nausea trending? Are you sleeping worse in the third trimester? What's your weight gain pattern? Health tracking apps capture your symptoms, mood, nutrition, and physical changes over time. This data is useful for you AND for your provider at appointments.

Practical Tools

Kick counters, contraction timers, hospital bag checklists, and appointment trackers. These become especially useful in the third trimester and during labor.

How do you get the most out of a pregnancy app?

The habits that make a pregnancy app useful are logging symptoms from the first trimester, tracking food and sleep, and writing down questions as they come up. Together they give you and your provider a record of your own pregnancy rather than an average one. Here are the habits in order:

  1. Start early. First trimester symptoms (nausea, fatigue, mood changes) are worth tracking from the start. The data helps your provider and helps you see what's normal for YOUR pregnancy.
  2. Track what you eat. Pregnancy nutrition affects everything from your energy to your baby's development. If your app doesn't have nutrition tracking, consider using a separate food logging app.
  3. Log your questions. Write down questions as they come up so you don't forget them at appointments. Some apps have built-in note features for this.
  4. Don't compare yourself to others. Community forums are great for support but can also fuel anxiety. Every pregnancy is different. Your symptoms, your timeline, and your experience are your own.
  5. Track sleep changes. Sleep quality often drops during pregnancy, especially in the third trimester. Logging it helps you and your provider address it before it becomes a bigger problem.
  6. Bring your data to appointments. "I've been nauseous" is vague. "My nausea peaks at 7/10 on days 45-60 and is worse when I sleep under 6 hours" is actionable.

What is the best free pregnancy app?

BabyCenter is free with no in-app purchases listed, offering week-by-week tracking, Birth Club communities, and expert-reviewed content. What to Expect is also free (ad-supported) with strong editorial content, and Ovia is free too. Veya offers free pregnancy tracking with a kick counter, contraction timer, nutrition, mood, and wearable integration. Flo and Pregnancy+ have free tiers, with some pregnancy features behind a subscription.

Which pregnancy app has the best baby development visuals?

Pregnancy+ has detailed, interactive 3D baby models. You can rotate and zoom the models, choose baby ethnicity, and see development at each stage. BabyCenter and What to Expect also offer 3D development videos, and Flo uses size comparisons and weekly updates.

Are pregnancy apps safe for my data?

Privacy varies a lot. Veya doesn't sell data or show ads. On their App Store privacy labels (as of October 2026), Pregnancy+ lists identifiers as data that may be used to track you, while What to Expect and BabyCenter (both owned by Everyday Health) list contact info, identifiers, usage data, and sensitive info. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing health data. Always check an app's privacy policy before entering sensitive pregnancy information.

Should I use one pregnancy app or multiple?

Many parents use two: one for content and community (What to Expect or BabyCenter for articles, forums, and 3D visuals) and one for health tracking (Veya for kick counting, contraction timing, symptoms, nutrition, and wearable data). This combination covers both editorial depth and practical tools.

Can I track nutrition during pregnancy with an app?

Most pregnancy apps focus on content rather than meal tracking. Veya has built-in meal and macro logging alongside pregnancy tracking. Ovia includes nutrition in its customizable logging plus a food-safety lookup. If your pregnancy app doesn't track meals, a separate food-logging app fills the gap.

Which pregnancy app is best for first-time parents?

What to Expect is often recommended for first-time parents because of its clear, detailed content based on the bestselling book. BabyCenter's Birth Club communities connect you with other parents due the same month. Pregnancy+ is great if you're a visual learner who wants to see your baby's development in 3D.

Glow vs Ovia vs Clue: which is best for first-time moms?

All three can track a pregnancy, but they come at it differently. Ovia has dedicated pregnancy tracking with week-by-week updates, a food-safety lookup, and community content, and is free on the App Store. Glow's pregnancy app, Nurture, covers week-by-week tracking, symptoms, a contraction timer, and an in-app community, with Glow Premium listed mostly at $59.99 on the App Store (as of October 2026). Clue includes a pregnancy mode alongside its period, trying-to-conceive, and perimenopause modes. For first-time moms who most want community and editorial depth, many also look at What to Expect and BabyCenter.

Do Flo or Ovia have better community support for pregnancy questions?

Both have communities, with slightly different strengths. Flo has a very large user base (570M+ downloads by Flo's own count) and an AI assistant for quick questions. Ovia is pregnancy- and family-focused, and pairs community content with week-by-week tracking and a food-safety lookup. If size and an AI helper matter most, Flo fits. If you want a pregnancy-specific space, Ovia fits. For due-date groups, BabyCenter's Birth Clubs put you with parents due the same month.

What should I consider when choosing a wearable to help track pregnancy?

During pregnancy, a wearable is most useful for tracking sleep, resting heart rate, and activity rather than predicting anything, since cycle prediction no longer applies. Look for comfort over a changing body, good sleep tracking (rest gets harder later in pregnancy), and an app that can import the wearable's data so you see it alongside your symptoms. Devices like the Apple Watch, Oura Ring, and Garmin all capture sleep and heart rate, but the wearable alone won't track kicks or contractions, so pair it with a pregnancy app that logs those. Always follow your provider's guidance over any device reading.

Many parents use two apps, and that's a perfectly good approach. Get the baby development content from one. Track your actual health in another. The goal is to feel informed and organized, not overwhelmed.
