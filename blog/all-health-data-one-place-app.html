Your Oura ring lives in one app. Your CGM lives in another. Your lab PDFs sit in a folder somewhere, and your period app has no idea any of it exists. This guide compares 5 apps that try to pull it together, and looks closely at the one thing most of them miss: showing that data against your cycle phase.

Step 1: Why Your Health Data Is Scattered in the First Place

This isn't really your fault. Every wearable maker wants you living inside their app. Every CGM manufacturer builds its own closed ecosystem. Hospitals and labs each run their own patient portal. None of these companies has much incentive to make their data easy to move somewhere else.

Before picking an app to fix this, it helps to know what you actually need pulled together:

Wearable Data

Sleep, heart rate, HRV, temperature, and activity, from an Apple Watch, Oura Ring, Garmin, Fitbit, or similar. Most of this can flow into a hub automatically once you connect the device.

CGM and Glucose Data

If you use a continuous glucose monitor like Dexcom or FreeStyle Libre, or a metabolic ring like Ultrahuman, that data usually lives in its own app unless something else is built to pull it in.

Lab Results

Bloodwork, hormone panels, thyroid tests. Usually a PDF from a patient portal or a piece of paper from your doctor's office. Very few apps let you actually store and revisit this alongside everything else.

Cycle Phase

The piece almost nobody connects to the rest. Your sleep, HRV, glucose response, and energy don't behave the same way in the follicular phase as they do in the luteal phase. Wearables can already detect a lot of this shift, but most general health dashboards never show it against your actual cycle.

Step 2: The 5 Apps Compared

For a Free Automatic Hub: Apple Health

Best if you want: Everything your iOS apps already collect, sitting in one place, without setting anything up.

Key Features

  • Built into every iPhone, free, no account needed beyond your Apple ID
  • Automatically receives data from nearly any iOS wearable app or fitness app via HealthKit
  • Basic Cycle Tracking module for logging periods and symptoms
  • Health Records can pull in labs and visit summaries, but only from hospital systems that participate
  • One tap to export a PDF summary for a doctor's visit

Strengths

  • It's already there. If you own an iPhone, you're already collecting data in it whether you open the app or not
  • Free, with no premium tier or upsells
  • The closest thing to a universal iOS data pipe, since most other apps are built to write to it
  • Health Records, where available, pulls real clinical data directly from participating hospital systems

Limitations

  • It's a storage shelf, not an analyst. Sleep, HRV, and cycle data each sit on their own card with no correlation between them
  • No overlay of wearable data against cycle phase
  • Health Records only works if your hospital or clinic participates. Most people can't use it at all
  • No manual lab upload. You can't just drop in a PDF from a lab that isn't a participating system
  • No CGM-specific dashboard, even though some glucose apps can write basic readings to it
  • iOS only. There's no Apple Health on Android

Who Should Choose This

Apple Health is ideal if you:

  • Just want your data captured somewhere without extra effort
  • Have a hospital system that supports Health Records
  • Plan to use a second app on top of it for actual analysis
  • Want a free, no-account-required option

Pricing: Free, built into iOS.

Download: Pre-installed on all iPhones.

For Provider-Connected Care: Guava Health

Best if you want: One dashboard that combines your medical records, labs, and wearable data, especially if you're working with a doctor or functional medicine practice.

Key Features

  • EHR integrations that pull in medical records and lab results from participating providers
  • Wearable and device syncing from Garmin, Oura, Fitbit, and Dexcom
  • Daily symptom and lifestyle logs inside the Guava app
  • AI-generated summaries and alerts that flag meaningful changes across your data
  • A companion Provider Dashboard used by practices to monitor patients remotely
  • Free consumer app, available on iOS, Android, and web, in 20+ languages

Strengths

  • The strongest EHR and lab integration of any app in this comparison, when your provider supports it
  • Direct Dexcom CGM syncing alongside multiple wearable brands
  • AI summaries are built for sharing with a provider, not just self-review
  • Cross-platform, including a full web version
  • Rated well on both app stores (4.7 and 4.9 respectively, per its own listings)

Limitations

  • Not built around the menstrual cycle. There's no cycle-phase view, so it won't show you how your labs or wearable data shift across your cycle
  • Its deepest features (EHR pull-in, provider dashboard) depend on your clinic or practice using Guava, which most people's doctors don't
  • General health platform, not built specifically for women's health or reproductive tracking
  • Best suited to people actively managing a condition with a provider, less useful as a general wellness tracker

Who Should Choose This

Guava Health is ideal if you:

  • Work with a provider or practice that already uses Guava
  • Manage a chronic condition and want labs, records, and wearable data together
  • Want AI-generated summaries to bring to appointments
  • Don't need cycle-specific tracking

Pricing: Free for the consumer app. Provider-side dashboard pricing varies by practice.

Download: Available on iOS, Android, and web.

For Custom Symptom Correlation: Bearable

Best if you want: To track anything you can think of and see how it correlates with everything else you're tracking.

Key Features

  • Fully customizable symptom and factor tracking
  • Reads sleep, heart rate, HRV, and steps from Apple Health, Health Connect, Fitbit, Garmin, and Samsung Health
  • An "Impacts" view that surfaces correlations between anything you track
  • Period tracking available as one module among many, not the core focus
  • Medication, supplement, and treatment logging

Strengths

  • The most flexible correlation engine of any app here. If you can name it, you can track it and see what it correlates with
  • Pulls from more third-party wearable brands than most single-purpose apps
  • Popular with people managing multiple chronic conditions at once
  • Generous free tier, GDPR-compliant, doesn't sell data

Limitations

  • Wearable sync is mostly one-directional (pulling in, not writing back), and it doesn't connect that data to cycle phase specifically
  • No CGM ingestion
  • No lab result storage or upload
  • No nutrition or macro tracking
  • The flexibility comes with setup time. It takes a while to configure what you actually want tracked

Who Should Choose This

Bearable is ideal if you:

  • Want to track very specific, custom factors that most apps don't have a preset for
  • Care more about correlation than about cycle-phase context
  • Already have your wearable connected to Apple Health or Health Connect
  • Need an Android option

Pricing: Free (generous tier), Premium ~$34.99/year or ~$6.99/month.

Download: Available on iOS and Android.

For Deep Data on Your Own Ring: Oura

Best if you want: The most detailed possible picture of your own sleep, HRV, temperature, and readiness, plus increasingly accurate cycle and ovulation detection built from that data.

Key Features

  • Overnight temperature, HRV, resting heart rate, and sleep stage tracking from the ring
  • Cycle Insights, which uses your temperature trend to detect ovulation and forecast your period, reporting roughly 96% ovulation detection accuracy in Oura's own published validation study
  • A Fertile Window feature combining heart rate, HRV, temperature, and respiratory rate
  • Oura Advisor, an AI assistant launched in February 2026 that answers reproductive health questions inside the app
  • A partnership with Natural Cycles for FDA-cleared, temperature-based digital birth control

Strengths

  • Unmatched depth on the metrics it does track, since it owns the sensor generating the data
  • Its cycle-phase detection is validated and specifically built around the temperature shift that happens after ovulation, which is the same signal HRV and resting heart rate patterns partly reflect
  • The Natural Cycles integration is a genuinely useful option for hormone-free contraception or conception planning
  • Comfortable, unobtrusive hardware that people actually wear consistently, which matters for data quality

Limitations

  • It's a hub for its own data, not other people's. It doesn't ingest data from a Garmin, Fitbit, Apple Watch, or CGM
  • No lab result storage
  • No nutrition or workout planning
  • Requires buying the ring hardware plus an ongoing membership subscription on top of it
  • Correlation stays within its own metrics. It won't connect your cycle data to something like glucose or a symptom you logged elsewhere

Who Should Choose This

Oura is ideal if you:

  • Want the ring specifically and don't need to combine data from other wearables
  • Care most about validated, temperature-based cycle and ovulation detection
  • Are interested in hormone-free contraception via the Natural Cycles integration
  • Don't need lab results or nutrition tracking in the same app

Pricing: Ring purchase (roughly $300-$550 depending on model) plus a monthly membership subscription required for full features.

Download: App available on iOS and Android, requires an Oura Ring.

Worth Noting: Health Connect and Google Fit (Android)

If you're on Android, there's no direct equivalent to Apple Health's ecosystem, but there's a close one. Health Connect is Google's on-device layer that lets apps share fitness and health data with each other, and Google Fit sits on top of it for basic activity tracking.

Like Apple Health, both are free data pipes rather than apps that analyze or overlay anything for you. If you're on Android and want more than storage, Bearable and Guava Health both connect to Health Connect and are available free on Google Play.

Privacy Considerations

Combining your wearable, CGM, and lab data in one place means that one app now holds a lot about you. Here's how each handles it:

  • Apple Health keeps data on-device by default and is widely regarded as privacy-strong, since Apple's business model isn't built around selling health data.
  • Guava Health handles clinical data through EHR integrations, which fall under stricter healthcare data-handling requirements given its use with medical practices.
  • Bearable is GDPR-compliant and states it doesn't sell user data.
  • Oura collects continuous biometric data from the ring. Review its current privacy policy directly, since a wearable worn 24/7 generates more data than an app you open occasionally.
  • Go Go Gaia keeps data private and doesn't sell it to third parties. Uploading lab results is optional and happens through the web app under your control.

Whichever app you choose, it's worth reading the current privacy policy rather than assuming, especially for anything handling lab results or continuous biometric data.

Getting the Most Out of a Combined Dashboard

Whichever app you land on, a few things make it actually useful instead of just another place your data sits unread:

  1. Connect your wearable first. That's the data that requires the least ongoing effort from you, and it's usually the fastest way to start seeing something.
  2. Add labs when you get them, not months later. Upload a lab PDF the same week you receive it. Waiting means you'll eventually lose track of where it is.
  3. Give it at least one full cycle before judging the patterns. A single week of data won't show you anything about how your metrics shift with your cycle. A full cycle, or two, will.
  4. Don't try to interpret labs yourself. An app that stores your labs is not the same as a clinician reading them. Bring what you've collected to your doctor rather than drawing conclusions on your own.
  5. Check what's actually connecting, not just what's installed. It's easy to assume a wearable is syncing when it stopped weeks ago. Spot-check the data periodically.

Pro Tip

If you're not sure which app fits, start with whichever one already has the least setup friction for the data source you care about most. If that's your wearable, start there. If it's labs, prioritize an app that lets you upload your own PDFs rather than waiting on a provider integration.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best app for seeing wearable, lab, and cycle data in one place?

It depends on what you're trying to do. Apple Health is the free, automatic hub that most iOS apps already write to, but it doesn't analyze anything. Guava Health is built for people working with a provider who want to combine EHR records, labs, and wearable data with AI summaries. Bearable is best if you want to correlate custom symptoms against anything you track. Oura is the best hub for its own ring data specifically. Go Go Gaia pulls wearables, CGM data, and lab uploads together and overlays all of it against your cycle phase, which none of the others do.

Can Apple Health show my sleep and HRV data against my cycle phase?

Not really. Apple Health has a Cycle Tracking module and it stores sleep, HRV, and other wearable metrics, but each lives on its own card. There's no built-in view that overlays your HRV or sleep trend against where you are in your cycle. You'd need a separate app that reads from Apple Health and adds that layer on top.

Does Guava Health work for period or cycle tracking?

Guava Health is built as a general health dashboard for combining EHR records, labs, and wearable data, often for people managing a chronic condition alongside a provider. It's not built around the menstrual cycle specifically, so it won't show you cycle phases or overlay your data against them the way a women's health app does.

Can I upload my own lab results to a health tracking app?

It depends on the app. Guava Health connects to labs through EHR integrations with participating providers. Apple Health's Health Records feature only works if your hospital system participates, and even then it's not a manual upload. Go Go Gaia lets you upload lab PDFs or CSV files yourself through its web app, so you're not dependent on your provider using a specific system. None of these apps interpret or diagnose from your labs. They store and display what you upload so you and your doctor can review it together.

Do I need a wearable to use one of these apps?

Every app in this comparison works with manual logging alone. A wearable just fills in data you'd otherwise have to enter by hand, like overnight HRV, resting heart rate, or temperature. If you don't have a wearable yet, start logging manually and add a device later. The historical data you build up manually stays useful either way.

What CGMs and glucose monitors can sync with a health tracking app?

Dexcom and FreeStyle Libre are the two most widely supported continuous glucose monitors across health apps, and Ultrahuman's ring and CGM combo is increasingly supported too. Go Go Gaia ingests data from all three (Dexcom, Libre, and Ultrahuman) and overlays glucose trends against your cycle phase. Apple Health can receive glucose data if the CGM's own app writes to HealthKit, but it won't connect that data to anything else automatically.

Is there a free option for Android users who want their health data together?

Health Connect is Google's version of Apple Health: a free, on-device layer that lets Android apps share fitness and health data with each other, and Google Fit sits on top of it for basic activity tracking. Like Apple Health, it's a passive data pipe rather than an app that analyzes or overlays your data for you. Guava Health and Bearable are both cross-platform and available free on Android if you want an app that does more with the data than just store it.

Final Thoughts

There's no single app that wins at everything here, because "combine my health data" covers a few genuinely different jobs. If you just want a free place for your data to land automatically, Apple Health already does that without you doing anything. If you're working with a provider on a chronic condition, Guava Health's EHR and lab integration is hard to beat. If correlation across custom symptoms is what you're after, Bearable is the most flexible. If you specifically want the deepest data on your own ring, Oura keeps getting better at validated cycle and ovulation detection.

And if what you actually want is to see your wearable data, your CGM readings, and your own uploaded labs shown against your cycle phase instead of on four separate screens, that's the gap Go Go Gaia is built to close.

Whichever you pick, the value isn't in having the data sitting somewhere. It's in actually looking at it often enough to notice what changes and when.
