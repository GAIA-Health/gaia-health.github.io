What Matters in a Sleep and Cycle App

"Tracks sleep" means very different things depending on the app. Here's what to weigh before you pick one, or two.

Whether It Actually Measures Sleep, or Just Logs It

Wearables like Oura, Whoop, and Apple Watch use a sensor (worn on your finger or wrist) to detect sleep stages, wake-ups, and duration automatically overnight. Apps without their own hardware, like Flo, Clue, Bearable, and Go Go Gaia, either rely on you logging sleep by hand or import the data a wearable already collected. Neither approach is wrong. They're solving different problems.

Whether Sleep Gets Connected to Cycle Phase

This is the gap most sleep trackers have. Oura and Whoop can tell you your deep sleep dropped last night. Neither was built to tell you it dropped because you're three days from your period. A cycle-aware app can show sleep quality plotted against follicular, ovulatory, and luteal phases, which is where the pattern usually lives.

How Much Manual Logging It Asks Of You

An app that requires you to rate your sleep every morning will get skipped on the mornings you're most tired, which are often the ones you'd most want data on. Automatic wearable sync solves this. If you don't own a wearable, look for an app that makes manual logging fast.

Whether Sleep Sits Next to Other Symptoms

Sleep rarely moves alone. Mood, energy, cramps, and stress usually shift with it. Apps that show sleep next to those other symptoms, not in a separate tab, make the connections easier to spot.

Sleep quality does shift across a typical cycle, largely driven by progesterone after ovulation. For the mechanics of why, see our pieces on the luteal phase and on HRV and resting heart rate across your cycle, which cover the same hormone shift from the cardiovascular side. Step 2: The 7 Apps and Wearables Compared

For Sleep-Stage Accuracy: Oura

Best if you want: The most detailed, accurate sleep data available on a consumer device, with cycle awareness as a secondary feature.

Key Features

  • Finger-worn ring that tracks sleep stages (REM, deep, light), time awake, sleep latency, and a nightly Sleep Score
  • Overnight temperature, HRV, heart rate, and blood oxygen alongside sleep data
  • Bedtime Guidance that suggests your optimal bedtime based on recent scores
  • Cycle insights that factor overnight temperature into phase estimates

Strengths

  • Among the most accurate consumer sleep trackers available, with strong agreement to lab-grade sleep studies in independent testing
  • The ring form factor is comfortable for many people to wear overnight, unlike a watch or band
  • Rich data: sleep stages, HRV, and temperature all layered into one readiness picture
  • Cross-platform (iOS and Android)

Limitations

  • Cycle tracking is a secondary feature. It doesn't log symptoms, flow, or mood the way a cycle-first app does
  • Hardware costs $399 to $499 depending on finish, plus a membership
  • Most sleep detail, including sleep stages and HRV trends, sits behind the paid membership after the included trial month
  • No web app

Who Should Choose This

  • You want the most precise sleep data you can get without a clinical sleep study
  • You're comfortable pairing it with a separate app for detailed cycle tracking
  • Wearing a ring appeals to you more than a watch or band

Pricing: Ring hardware is $399 to $499. Membership is $5.99/month or $69.99/year, required for most sleep and readiness detail.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

For Phase-Based Sleep Coaching: Whoop

Best if you want: Sleep and recovery guidance that already adjusts for where you are in your cycle.

Key Features

  • Wrist-worn band tracking four sleep stages (awake, light, REM, slow-wave), continuous heart rate, HRV, and skin temperature
  • Sleep Coach that calculates how much sleep you actually need based on recent strain and recovery
  • Menstrual Cycle Coaching that predicts your current phase from logged dates and biometric trends, then adjusts sleep and strain guidance around it
  • A predicted cycle calendar with symptom-pattern insights on higher tiers

Strengths

  • One of the few wearables that explicitly ties sleep guidance to cycle phase, not just sleep in isolation
  • No separate hardware purchase. The band comes included with membership
  • Detailed, continuous data collection rather than periodic spot checks
  • Cross-platform (iOS and Android)

Limitations

  • No true month-to-month option. Membership bills annually or over 24 months, from $199 to $359 per year depending on tier
  • Deeper sleep coaching and menstrual cycle features require the higher Peak or Life tiers
  • It's a coaching and recovery tool first. Symptom logging beyond cycle phase is limited compared to a dedicated cycle app
  • Wearing a band to sleep every night isn't comfortable for everyone

Who Should Choose This

  • You want sleep and training guidance that already accounts for your cycle phase
  • You'd rather have one device handle sleep, recovery, and strain together
  • You're comfortable with an annual membership rather than owning hardware outright

Pricing: $199 to $359/year depending on tier, device included. Monthly billing ($25 to $40/month) is available to existing members only.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

For Staying in the Apple Ecosystem: Apple Watch / Apple Health

Best if you want: Free sleep stage tracking and basic cycle logging without buying another device or subscription.

Key Features

  • Sleep stages (Awake, Core, Deep, REM) tracked automatically when you wear the watch to bed with Sleep Focus on
  • Sleep consistency and schedule tracking in the Health app on iPhone
  • Built-in Cycle Tracking that logs periods and predicts fertile windows, with retroactive ovulation detection on Series 8 and later via wrist temperature
  • All data lives in Apple Health, where third-party apps can read and write to it

Strengths

  • No subscription required for sleep or cycle tracking. It's included with the watch
  • Recent sleep-staging accuracy is competitive with dedicated sleep wearables in independent testing, particularly for light and REM sleep
  • Everything syncs automatically to Apple Health, where other apps (including cycle-focused ones) can pull it in
  • You're likely already wearing it if you own an iPhone and an Apple Watch

Limitations

  • Cycle Tracking in Health is genuinely basic. No symptom logging, mood, or correlation insights, just period dates and predictions
  • Battery life means some people don't wear it every single night, creating gaps in sleep data
  • Deep sleep detection accuracy trails REM and light sleep detection in independent validation
  • Requires an iPhone. No Android support

Who Should Choose This

  • You already own an Apple Watch and don't want another subscription
  • Free, no-frills sleep stages are enough for you
  • You want your sleep data to flow into other apps rather than staying siloed

Pricing: Free with an Apple Watch (Series 6 or later for sleep stages). Watch hardware starts around $249 to $399 depending on model.

Download: Built into watchOS and the Health app on iOS

Best if you want: A widely used cycle app that lets you log sleep alongside symptoms, without needing a wearable.

Key Features

  • Daily logging of sleep, energy, mood, flow, and dozens of other symptoms

Strengths

  • One of the most widely used cycle apps, with a well-developed prediction engine from a large logged-cycle dataset
  • Sleep sits in the same daily log as your other symptoms, so it's easy to add without extra setup
  • Generous free tier that covers core cycle and symptom tracking
  • Cross-platform (iOS and Android)

Limitations

  • Sleep logging is manual. As of 2026, Flo doesn't automatically sync sleep data from a wearable
  • No sleep stages or objective duration, just what you remember to log
  • Flo's data practices have drawn scrutiny in the past over health data sharing with third parties, which the company has since addressed with an Anonymous Mode and updated policies
  • Deeper correlation and insight features sit behind Flo Premium

Who Should Choose This

  • You want an established, widely used cycle app and don't own a wearable
  • Quick manual logging works fine for you
  • You want a broad content library alongside tracking

Privacy Considerations

Sleep data reveals a lot: when you're home, how you're sleeping, and indirectly, how you're feeling. It's worth checking each company's privacy policy and App Store data label before you connect a wearable to a cycle app. Here's the short version:

  • Clue is widely regarded as the privacy leader among mainstream cycle apps and states it doesn't sell user data.
  • Bearable is GDPR-compliant and states it doesn't sell data.
  • Go Go Gaia keeps your data private and doesn't sell it to third parties.
  • Flo faced scrutiny in past years over health data shared with third-party analytics tools, and has since added an Anonymous Mode and updated its data practices. Read the current policy before you decide.
  • Oura, Whoop, and Apple each publish detailed privacy policies covering biometric data. Since this data includes sleep and heart data, it's worth reading how each handles data if your account is ever compromised or the company changes ownership.

Getting the Most Out of Sleep and Cycle Tracking

Whatever you pick, here's how to actually get something useful out of it:

  1. Give it at least two full cycles. One rough week doesn't tell you much. A pattern across two or three cycles is much more convincing.
  2. Log cycle days even if your wearable handles sleep automatically. The sleep data is only useful in context. Without cycle days logged, you're just looking at a sleep chart.
  3. Look at trends, not single nights. One bad night can come from travel, alcohol, or stress. A consistent dip in the same days each cycle is the signal worth paying attention to.
  4. Pair a wearable with a cycle app if you want both. Plenty of people use Oura or Whoop for the sensor and a cycle-first app for the context, since neither category fully replaces the other yet.
  5. Bring the pattern to your doctor if sleep is a real problem. "I sleep badly the week before my period" is a useful, specific thing to say to a doctor. A tracked pattern gives them something concrete to work from.

Pro Tip

If you already own a wearable, don't buy a second one. Connect what you have to a cycle-aware app and see the pattern first. You can always add a dedicated sleep tracker later if you want more precision than your current device gives you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best period tracker that also tracks sleep?

It depends on whether you want the sensor or the context. Oura and Whoop are the most accurate at measuring sleep itself, since they're built specifically for it. Apple Watch tracks sleep for free if you already own one. Among cycle-first apps, Clue can sync wearable sleep data with Clue Plus, Bearable tracks sleep alongside many other symptoms, and Go Go Gaia shows sleep next to cycle phase, mood, and symptoms with a wearable connected.

Do Oura or Whoop track your menstrual cycle?

Both offer some cycle awareness, but neither is built as a cycle tracker first. Whoop's Menstrual Cycle Coaching predicts your current phase from logged dates and biometric trends and adjusts its sleep and strain guidance accordingly. Oura offers cycle insights that factor temperature data into phase estimates, but it's a secondary feature layered onto a sleep and readiness product. If cycle detail like symptoms, flow, and phase-specific correlations matters to you, a dedicated cycle app paired with your wearable's sleep data gives you more.

Can period tracking apps read sleep data from a wearable?

Several can. Clue syncs sleep and heart rate data from Oura, Apple Watch, Whoop, Fitbit, and other wearables with a Clue Plus subscription. Bearable connects to Apple Health, Google Fit, and select wearables to import sleep automatically. Go Go Gaia syncs with Apple Watch, Oura, and Garmin and shows that sleep data alongside cycle phase, mood, and symptoms. Flo lets you log sleep manually but doesn't sync wearable sleep data automatically as of 2026.

Why does sleep quality change across the menstrual cycle?

Progesterone rises sharply after ovulation and has a mild sedative effect, but it also raises core body temperature, which can make it harder to fall and stay asleep. Many people notice more wakings, lighter sleep, and worse sleep quality in the days right before their period, when both progesterone and estrogen drop quickly. Our companion piece on the luteal phase covers the hormone mechanics in more depth.

Do I need a wearable to track sleep alongside my cycle?

No, but it helps. You can log sleep manually in most cycle apps, rating how rested you felt or noting hours slept. A wearable like Apple Watch, Oura, or Whoop adds sleep stages, wake counts, and objective duration without you having to remember to log it, which is especially useful on the nights you're too tired to open an app. Apps like Go Go Gaia, Clue, and Bearable can pull that wearable data in automatically once it's connected.

Wearable or cycle app: which should I get first?

If you don't own either yet, start with whichever gap bothers you more. If you have no idea how you actually sleep, a wearable gives you the objective numbers. If you already sense your sleep and cycle are connected but can't prove it, a cycle app that lets you log or sync sleep will show you the pattern faster. Many people end up with both, since the wearable measures and the cycle app explains.

Final Thoughts

There's no single best option here, because sleep tracking and cycle tracking are still, for the most part, two different products solving two different problems. If pure sleep-stage precision is what you're after, Oura and Whoop are hard to beat, and Apple Watch gets you most of the way there for free if you're already in that ecosystem. If cycle detail and privacy matter more, Clue and Flo are well established, and Bearable is strong if sleep is one symptom among many you're tracking.

The gap all of them share to some degree is showing sleep and cycle phase in the same view without extra work. That's the specific thing Go Go Gaia is built to close: connect a wearable, and your sleep data shows up next to your cycle phase, mood, and symptoms automatically, recapped each quarter in a Health Wrapped alongside the rest of your health.

Whichever you pick, the useful move is the same: track for two cycles and see what actually shows up, rather than guessing.
