BabyCenter Alternatives in 2026: Seven Pregnancy Apps and Who Each One Fits

The closest alternatives to BabyCenter are What to Expect and The Bump for articles and community, Pregnancy+ and Sprout for 3D baby models, Ovia and Flo for continuity from cycle tracking, and Veya for health tracking with wearable data. BabyCenter is a good free app, so switching only makes sense if one thing about it doesn't fit you.

This guide covers why people look around, what BabyCenter does well, and seven alternatives matched to different needs, with pricing and App Store links. One of them is our own app, and we say so where it appears.

By Veya Team • Published November 3, 2026 • 11 min read • App Comparison

Quick Answer: Which App Should You Try Instead of BabyCenter?

  • Similar style, different publisher: The Bump. Daily articles, tools and community

  • 3D baby models: Pregnancy+ or Sprout
  • One app from cycle to postpartum: Ovia, Flo or Veya

Why Do People Look for BabyCenter Alternatives?

Most of the reasons are about fit, not quality. The ones we hear most:

  • The feed is community-heavy. Birth Clubs are a draw for many people, and a lot for others. If you mostly want your own numbers and notes, a busy feed can get in the way.
  • Ads. BabyCenter is free and ad-supported. Its privacy labels list third-party advertising among the purposes for data linked to you, so some people prefer an app with a different model.
  • Continuity. BabyCenter is built around pregnancy and parenting. If you tracked your cycle or fertility before, you may want that history in the same app as your pregnancy, and then your postpartum recovery too.
  • Wearable and Apple Health data. If your watch or ring already records sleep and heart rate, you may want that next to your symptoms.
  • Curiosity. Some people just like to see how a different app handles the same weeks.

What Does BabyCenter Do Well?

Quite a lot, and it costs nothing to download. Per its App Store listing, BabyCenter offers week-by-week tracking with 3D fetal development videos, a pregnancy calendar for appointments and symptoms, a kick counter, a contraction timer, free baby safety and childbirth classes, a baby registry tool, and a free loyalty program that earns points toward discounts. Its Birth Clubs put you in a group of people due the same month, which is hard to replicate. After the birth it adds a baby growth tracker and sleep and feeding guides.

If you want free content and a due-date community, BabyCenter already does that well. Our What to Expect vs BabyCenter and Ovia vs BabyCenter guides go deeper on how it compares head to head.

Seven BabyCenter Alternatives Compared

No app is a drop-in replacement, so each one below is matched to what you might want to change. For a wider pregnancy-only overview, see our pregnancy app comparison.

What to Expect: For Expert-Reviewed Articles and a Parent Community

Who it fits: You like BabyCenter's week-by-week reading but want a different editorial voice, or you want community groups without the Birth Club structure.

What it does well

  • Week-by-week tracking with 3D development videos, per its listing
  • Expert-reviewed articles alongside community groups and a feed of trending conversations
  • Logs for symptoms, weight, kick counts and medications, plus a newborn and baby log

Trade-offs

  • Published by Everyday Health, Inc., the same publisher as BabyCenter, so the business model is similar (see our What to Expect vs BabyCenter comparison)
  • Free with advertising; its privacy labels list third-party advertising among the purposes for data linked to you
  • Focused on pregnancy content, with less broad mood, sleep or nutrition tracking

Price model: Free to download; ad-supported.

Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker: For Structured Tracking From Cycle to Postpartum

Who it fits: You want a tracking-first app that follows you from cycle tracking and trying to conceive through pregnancy and postpartum, or you get Ovia through an employer or health plan.

What it does well

  • One consolidated app that combines Ovia and Ovia Pregnancy, per its App Store release notes
  • Carries cycle and TTC history straight into pregnancy tracking
  • Employer and health plan programs through Labcorp, which owns Ovia Health

Trade-offs

  • Smaller community than BabyCenter, and no 3D baby visuals
  • Consumer pricing is not published up front, so check the app and your own plan
  • Ovia's own privacy policy allows sharing or selling de-identified data for research, so it is worth a read

We cover the Ovia side in more depth in Ovia vs BabyCenter and Ovia alternatives.

Price model: Free to download; premium access is often offered through an employer or health plan. Check the app for current pricing.

Download: Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker on the App Store

Flo: For Pregnancy Mode Inside a Cycle Tracker

Who it fits: You already track your cycle in Flo and want to stay in one familiar app when you get pregnant, or you want an AI chatbot for quick questions.

What it does well

  • Pregnancy mode with week-by-week tracking inside the regular cycle app
  • Large user base and a familiar interface
  • Cycle and pregnancy under one roof, so you are not changing apps between life stages

Trade-offs

  • Much of the pregnancy content sits behind a paid plan, and free users see upgrade prompts
  • Flo has a privacy history worth reading up on, including a 2021 FTC settlement; its listing now describes an optional Anonymous Mode
  • Not a community-first app the way BabyCenter is

Price model: Free with ads, plus paid plans. Its listing showed a Pregnancy Tracker plan at $49.99 in October 2026; check the listing for current prices.

Download: Flo Cycle & Period Tracker on the App Store

Pregnancy+: For 3D Baby Models and a Broad Toolkit

Who it fits: You enjoy BabyCenter's 3D videos and want interactive models you can rotate and zoom, plus planning tools in one place.

What it does well

  • Interactive 3D baby development models, week-by-week guides and daily articles, per its listing
  • Tools including a due date calculator, kick counter, weight log, contraction timer, hospital bag list and birth plan
  • A bump photo diary for a visual pregnancy record

Trade-offs

  • Some tools sit behind the premium plan, so check which ones before you commit
  • Pregnancy-only scope, so you would change apps for cycle tracking or postpartum
  • Community features are newer than BabyCenter's Birth Clubs

Price model: Free with in-app purchases. Premium was listed at $5.99 a month or $34.99 a year in October 2026.

Download: Pregnancy + | Tracker App on the App Store

Sprout Pregnancy Tracker: For Health Logging With a Journal

Who it fits: You want 3D baby models plus a place to log symptoms, medications and vitals, and a journal you can turn into a keepsake.

What it does well

  • Interactive 3D baby models and a week-by-week timeline, per its listing
  • Symptom, medication and vitals logging, a kick counter, a contraction timer and a weight tracker
  • Pregnancy journal with prompts and a PDF keepsake option

Trade-offs

  • Pregnancy-focused, so no cycle tracking or longer-term continuity
  • A smaller community presence than BabyCenter or What to Expect
  • We have not verified how its free and paid tiers split, so check the listing

Price model: Free to download; see the listing for in-app purchase details.

Download: Pregnancy Tracker App - Sprout on the App Store

The Bump: For a Familiar Content-and-Tools App

Who it fits: You like the BabyCenter format of daily articles, community and tools, and want to compare it with a similar app from a different publisher.

What it does well

  • Daily articles matched to your week, a baby-size-as-produce comparison and a 3D growth visualization, per its listing
  • Free tools including a kick counter, a contraction counter, a weight gain tracker, a journal and a food safety tool
  • Baby name lists and a due date calculator

Trade-offs

  • Published by The Knot Inc. and, like BabyCenter, built around content and community more than personal health data
  • Pregnancy and baby focus, so no cycle history
  • Check its privacy label before logging sensitive details

Price model: Free to download; see the listing for current in-app purchases.

How Do You Choose?

Start with the one thing that made you look. Then try an app for a week before you decide.

  • If it's the community: stay with BabyCenter, or try What to Expect or The Bump for a different crowd.
  • If it's the ads or privacy: read each app's privacy label and current policy before logging anything sensitive. Apple notes that the labels are developer-provided and not verified by Apple.
  • If it's continuity: pick an app that also does cycle tracking (Ovia, Flo or Veya), so your history doesn't start over.
  • If it's the visuals: Pregnancy+ and Sprout lean hardest on 3D models.
  • If it's your wearable data: check that the app reads it before you switch.
  • If it's the third trimester: confirm the kick counter and contraction timer are free, since some apps put them in a paid plan.

Switching is easy because every app builds your week from your due date. Enter the exact date your provider gave you and you'll land on the right week. If you need to work it out, our due date calculator and pregnancy week calculator give you an estimate. Your provider's date is the one that counts. You can also run two apps side by side, one for reading and one for logging.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good alternative to BabyCenter?

It depends on what you want to change. What to Expect and The Bump are the closest in style, with week-by-week content and communities. Pregnancy+ and Sprout focus on 3D baby models and pregnancy tools. Ovia and Flo carry cycle tracking into pregnancy. Veya is for people who want health tracking, wearable data and continuity from cycle to postpartum. BabyCenter itself remains a solid free option.

Is BabyCenter free?

Yes. BabyCenter's App Store listing shows the app as free to download, and it is ad-supported. Its privacy labels list third-party advertising among the purposes for data linked to you, so read the current privacy policy before logging sensitive details.

Is there a pregnancy app without ads?

Some apps are lighter on ads than others, but the answer changes between versions, so check each listing and privacy label. In this guide, Veya runs no ads and does not sell data. Ovia says it has not shown ads to consumer users since June 2025. Flo and Pregnancy+ offer paid plans, and the free versions of the others are supported by advertising or partners.

Can I use BabyCenter and another pregnancy app together?

Yes, and many people do. A common pairing is one app for articles and community, like BabyCenter or What to Expect, and a second for private daily logging of symptoms, kick counts and wearable data. Just enter the same due date in both so the weeks line up.

Will I lose my data if I switch from BabyCenter?

Logs don't transfer automatically between pregnancy apps. Your week-by-week view rebuilds as soon as you enter your due date, so what you would re-enter is mostly things you want to keep referencing, like a weight log or recent symptom notes. Screenshot those first, or check BabyCenter's privacy policy for how to request a copy of your data.

Final Thoughts

BabyCenter is a good free app, and plenty of people never need to leave. If you do want something different, match the app to the reason: What to Expect or The Bump for a similar style, Pregnancy+ or Sprout for 3D models, Ovia or Flo for cycle continuity, and Veya if logging and wearable data matter more than community. Many people end up using BabyCenter for the reading and a second app for the logging, and that works too.
