TrueCoach vs Trainerize: Which Fits Your Coaching Business?

Both platforms handle program delivery well. This guide compares who each is built for, how programming and check-ins actually work day to day, current pricing, and which type of coaching business tends to land where.

By Go Go Gaia Team • Published August 25, 2026 • 9 min read • For Coaches

Educational content for fitness and coaching professionals, not medical or financial advice. Pricing and features are current as of August 2026 and change periodically, so confirm current details on each company's own site before buying.

Quick Answer

TrueCoach and Trainerize both give you a workout builder, video exercise library, client app, and check-in tools, and either can run a coaching business well. TrueCoach leans toward independent strength and conditioning coaches who want a focused, one-on-one tool. Trainerize leans toward coaches and studios that want room to add group programs, community features, and a lower-cost entry point as they grow. Neither shows you a client's cycle phase or lab work, since that's outside what either was built to track.

If you've spent any time comparing coaching software, TrueCoach and Trainerize are probably the two names that keep coming up. Both are established, both are used by real coaching businesses at scale, and both do the core job, building and delivering programs, well. The differences show up in who each one is really built for, how deep the check-in and nutrition tools go, and what it costs as your roster grows. This guide walks through all of that using each company's own published information, plus a look at the one thing both platforms leave out: any view into a client's cycle, sleep, or lab data alongside her training.

Who Each Platform Is Built For

TrueCoach describes itself as built for personal trainers first. Its marketing and feature set are aimed at independent coaches: strength and conditioning specialists, bodybuilding and nutrition coaches, and healthcare-adjacent practitioners like physical therapists and chiropractors who also program training. It also serves larger operations, including CrossFit gyms, university athletic programs, and recreation centers, but the core product decisions read as built around one coach managing a roster of individual clients closely. TrueCoach cites more than 16,000 coaches on the platform, over 250,000 total clients, and more than 50 million workouts delivered.

Trainerize (now branded ABC Trainerize) positions itself more broadly, as a coaching and client-engagement platform for independent coaches, personal training studios, gyms, and large multi-location businesses like health clubs, recreation centers, and corporate wellness programs. It names Gold's Gym, Life Time, YMCA, and Burn Boot Camp among the businesses using it. Trainerize cites more than 400,000 trainers and coaches, over 45,000 fitness businesses, and more than 1.6 million clients on the platform, a scale that reflects its pull with gyms and studios running multiple trainers, not just solo coaches.

Neither positioning is a hard rule. Plenty of solo coaches run their whole business on Trainerize, and plenty of gyms use TrueCoach for their training staff, so if you're choosing based on where each company has clearly invested, TrueCoach reads as the tighter, coach-and-client tool, and Trainerize reads as the one built to also handle a business with several trainers and a front desk.

Programming and Delivery Compared

Both platforms give you a drag-and-drop workout builder, a video exercise library, in-app messaging, a branded or semi-branded client mobile app, and integrated payments through Stripe. Where they diverge is in a few specific areas: how nutrition is handled, whether wearable data is included at the entry tier, and how much either platform leans into group and community features.

The wearable gap is worth flagging on its own. Trainerize includes Apple Health, Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin, and Withings syncing (steps, activity, sleep, heart rate, and weight) on most paid tiers. TrueCoach's equivalent, syncing with Apple, Garmin, and WHOOP, is held back for its Standard and Pro plans, so a coach on the entry Starter tier won't have it. If wearable data matters to how you coach, that's a real factor in which tier you'd actually need, not just which platform.

Check-Ins and the Client-Data Gap Both Share

Both platforms are genuinely strong at the fitness side of check-ins. TrueCoach offers custom check-in and intake forms with progress photos, and it tracks body metrics like weight, BMI, body fat percentage, and circumference measurements, auto-charted into trend lines so a coach can see change over time. Trainerize covers similar ground, with progress photos, scheduling, and check-in workflows built around client compliance and streaks. For a broader look at what coaches actually use to fill the gap between formal check-ins, how to monitor client progress between sessions covers the options beyond what either platform ships natively.

What neither platform does is connect that data to anything happening in a client's cycle. Trainerize can pull in sleep data through its wearable sync, but it arrives as a raw number, not tied to where a client is in her cycle or what else might be shifting that week. Neither platform pulls in lab results at all. That's not a criticism of either product. Check-in software and program delivery are what coaches search these platforms out to do, and both do that job well. It's just a scope neither one was built to cover, and it's worth knowing going in if a client's off week keeps showing up in her training data without an obvious training-related cause.

Pricing

As of August 2026, TrueCoach's published plans start at $26.34 a month billed annually (or $29.98 a month billed month-to-month) for up to 5 active clients, stepping up to $57.99 a month for up to 20 clients and $136.99 a month for up to 50, with custom pricing above that. There's no free tier, but there's a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, and a 90-day money-back guarantee for first-time annual subscribers, all listed on truecoach.co/pricing.

As of August 2026, Trainerize's published plans start free for a single client, with a Grow plan at $9 a month, Pro plans starting at $23 a month and scaling with client count up to 200 clients, and Studio Plus at $248 a month for larger rosters, per location. A 30-day free trial with unlimited clients and no credit card required is available before committing. Some features, like advanced nutrition coaching and video coaching, cost extra as add-ons rather than being bundled into the base price. Annual billing saves 10 percent. Full current pricing is listed on trainerize.com/pricing.

Both companies update pricing periodically, so treat the numbers here as a snapshot rather than something to quote back verbatim months from now.

Who Should Pick What

There's no single winner here, since the two platforms are solving slightly different problems.

TrueCoach tends to fit:

  • Solo strength, conditioning, or online coaches running mostly one-on-one relationships
  • Coaches who want detailed check-in metrics and progress-photo tracking without a lot of extra features to configure
  • Practitioners like physical therapists or chiropractors who program training alongside other care

Trainerize tends to fit:

  • Coaches who want to start free and grow into paid tiers as their client list grows
  • Coaches planning to add group programs, challenges, or community features alongside one-on-one work
  • Gym or studio owners managing multiple trainers under one account
  • Coaches who want wearable sync included without needing to upgrade to a higher tier

If you're still not sure after weighing those, most coaches find the free trial (TrueCoach) or free single-client tier and 30-day trial (Trainerize) is enough to tell within a couple of weeks whether the workflow fits how you actually coach.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is TrueCoach or Trainerize better for a solo online coach?

Both work well for solo coaches, and the better fit comes down to how you want to grow. TrueCoach was built with independent strength and conditioning coaches in mind and keeps its feature set focused on program delivery and progress tracking for one-on-one relationships. Trainerize also supports solo coaches on its free single-client tier and low-cost Grow plan, and it adds group challenges and community features if you ever want to run cohort-based programs alongside individual coaching.

Can I switch from TrueCoach to Trainerize, or the other way around, without losing client data?

Neither platform advertises a one-click migration tool for moving from a competitor. In practice, switching means rebuilding your program templates and re-inviting clients to the new app, since workout libraries and client records don't transfer automatically between the two. Each company's support team can usually walk you through what can be exported, like client contact lists, versus what has to be recreated.

Does TrueCoach or Trainerize include nutrition coaching?

Both offer a nutrition layer, built differently. TrueCoach handles macro and calorie tracking through a MyFitnessPal integration, so clients log food there and the data flows back to your dashboard. Trainerize builds meal plans, recipes, and calorie tracking natively into the app, with a more full-featured version available as an add-on. Neither is a replacement for working with a registered dietitian on medical nutrition needs.

Do TrueCoach or Trainerize show client wearable or health data?

Both sync some wearable data, with different scope. Trainerize connects to Apple Health, Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin, and Withings, pulling in steps, activity, sleep, heart rate, and weight on most paid tiers. TrueCoach syncs with Apple, Garmin, and WHOOP, but that integration is reserved for its Standard and Pro plans rather than the entry tier. Neither platform organizes that data around a client's menstrual cycle or pulls in lab results, since that's outside what either was built to do.

Which is cheaper, TrueCoach or Trainerize?

Trainerize has the lower entry cost, with a free single-client tier and paid plans starting at $9 a month, while TrueCoach's plans start around $26 a month billed annually with no free tier. At higher client counts the math shifts, and the better test is which plan's included features, like nutrition, check-ins, wearable sync, and group tools, match what you'll actually use, since both list current pricing on their own sites and change it periodically.
