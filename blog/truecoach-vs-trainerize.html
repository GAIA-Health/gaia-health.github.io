TrueCoach vs Trainerize: Which Fits Your Coaching Business?

Both platforms handle program delivery well. This guide compares who each is built for, how programming and check-ins actually work day to day, current pricing, and which type of coaching business tends to land where.

By Veya Team • Published August 25, 2026 • Last reviewed October 9, 2026 • 9 min read • For Coaches

Educational content for fitness and coaching professionals, not medical or financial advice. Pricing and features are current as of September 2026 and change periodically, so confirm current details on each company's own site before buying.

Quick Answer

Choose TrueCoach if you coach clients one-on-one and want a focused tool. Choose Trainerize if you want a free entry plan, named wearable integrations, or room to add group messaging and multi-location studio plans. Both include a workout builder, video library, client app, and messaging. Neither's official pages mention cycle or lab data.

If you've spent any time comparing coaching software, TrueCoach and Trainerize are probably the two names that keep coming up. Both are established, both are used by real coaching businesses at scale, and both do the core job, building and delivering programs. The differences show up in who each one is built for, how nutrition and wearable data are handled, and what it costs as your roster grows. This guide walks through all of that using each company's own published information, plus the one thing neither's official pages mention: any view into a client's cycle or lab data alongside her training.

Who is each platform built for?

TrueCoach calls itself the "#1 software for personal trainers" on its own site (its claim, not ours), as of September 2026.[1] Its feature set reads as built around one coach managing a roster of individual clients closely. TrueCoach cites more than 16,000 coaches on the platform, over 250,000 total clients, and more than 50 million workouts delivered, as of September 2026.[1]

Trainerize (now branded ABC Trainerize) positions itself more broadly, citing more than 400,000 trainers and coaches, over 45,000 health and fitness businesses, and more than 1.6 million clients, as of September 2026.[2] It names Gold's Gym, Life Time, YMCA, and Burn Boot Camp among the businesses using it, and it offers a Studio Plus tier for larger rosters, which reflects its pull with gyms and studios running multiple trainers, not just solo coaches.[2]

Neither positioning is a hard rule. Plenty of solo coaches run their whole business on Trainerize, and plenty of gyms use TrueCoach for their training staff, so if you're choosing based on where each company has clearly invested, TrueCoach reads as the tighter, coach-and-client tool, and Trainerize reads as the one built to also handle a business with several trainers and a front desk.

Both platforms give you a drag-and-drop workout builder, a video exercise library, in-app messaging, a client mobile app, and Stripe-based payments, as of September 2026.[1][2] Where they diverge is in a few specific areas: how nutrition is handled, how wearable data is listed, and how much either platform leans into group features.

Do TrueCoach or Trainerize show cycle or lab data alongside check-ins?

Both platforms cover the fitness side of check-ins. TrueCoach's features page lists client metrics including body metrics, progress photos, and custom data points, and Trainerize's lists metrics such as weight, body fat, and lean mass.[1][2] For a broader look at what coaches actually use to fill the gap between formal check-ins, how to monitor client progress between sessions covers the options beyond what either platform ships natively.

Neither company's official pages mention connecting that data to a client's cycle: as of September 2026 we found no menstrual cycle, hormone, lab, or symptom data on either site.[1][2] That's not a criticism of either product. Check-in software and program delivery are what coaches come to these platforms for. It's worth knowing going in if a client's off week keeps showing up in her training data without an obvious training-related cause. How much do TrueCoach and Trainerize cost?

As of September 2026, TrueCoach's published plans start at $26.34 a month billed annually for up to 5 active clients, stepping up to $57.99 a month for up to 20 clients and $136.99 a month for up to 50, with larger rosters beyond that, so check TrueCoach's pricing page.[1] There's no free tier, but there's a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, and a 90-day money-back guarantee for first-time annual subscribers, all listed on truecoach.co/pricing, which is also the best place to check the current month-to-month rate before annual billing.

As of September 2026, Trainerize's published plans start free for a single client, with a Grow plan at $9 a month, Pro plans starting at $23 a month and scaling with client count up to 200 clients, and Studio Plus at $248 a month for larger rosters, per location.[2] A 30-day free trial with unlimited clients and no credit card required is available before committing. Some features may cost extra as add-ons on some plans, so check the pricing page. Annual billing saves 10 percent. Full current pricing is listed on trainerize.com/pricing.

Both companies update pricing periodically, so treat the numbers here as a snapshot rather than something to quote back verbatim months from now.

Should you pick TrueCoach or Trainerize?

There's no single winner here, since the two platforms are solving slightly different problems.

TrueCoach tends to fit:

  • Solo or online coaches running mostly one-on-one relationships
  • Coaches who want detailed check-in metrics and progress-photo tracking without a lot of extra features to configure
  • Coaches who want a tool described by its maker as built for personal trainers

Trainerize tends to fit:

  • Coaches who want to start free and grow into paid tiers as their client list grows
  • Coaches planning to add group programs and group messaging alongside one-on-one work
  • Gym or studio owners managing multiple trainers under one account
  • Coaches who want a specific, named list of wearable integrations to confirm before they commit

If you're still not sure after weighing those, most coaches find the free trial (TrueCoach) or free single-client tier and 30-day trial (Trainerize) is enough to tell within a couple of weeks whether the workflow fits how you actually coach. Frequently Asked Questions

Is TrueCoach or Trainerize better for a solo online coach?

Both can work for solo coaches, and the better fit comes down to how you want to grow. TrueCoach positions itself as software for personal trainers, with a feature set centered on program delivery and client tracking. Trainerize also supports solo coaches on its free single-client tier and low-cost Grow plan, and it offers group messaging if you ever want to run group programs alongside individual coaching.

Can I switch from TrueCoach to Trainerize, or the other way around, without losing client data?

We did not find a migration tool for moving between the two on their official pricing or features pages. Ask each company's support team what can be exported and imported before you switch, so you know what you would need to recreate.

Does TrueCoach or Trainerize include nutrition coaching?

Both offer a nutrition layer, built differently. TrueCoach handles macro and calorie tracking through a MyFitnessPal integration, and delivers meal plans through its Documents feature. Trainerize builds meal tracking, a Smart Meal Planner, and a Flexible Meal Planner natively into the app. Check each pricing page for which nutrition features come with each plan. Neither is a replacement for working with a registered dietitian on medical nutrition needs.

Do TrueCoach or Trainerize show client wearable or health data?

Both list wearable integrations, with different devices. As of September 2026, Trainerize names Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin, Withings, and MyFitnessPal, and lists steps, sleep, resting heart rate, and weight among the client metrics coaches can view. TrueCoach names Apple Health, Garmin, WHOOP, Oura, and MyFitnessPal. We could not confirm which plans include wearable sync. Neither company's official pages mention organizing that data around a client's menstrual cycle or pulling in lab results.

Which is cheaper, TrueCoach or Trainerize?

Trainerize has the lower entry cost, with a free single-client tier and paid plans starting at $9 a month, while TrueCoach's plans start around $26 a month billed annually with no free tier. At higher client counts the math shifts, and the better test is which plan's included features, like nutrition, check-ins, wearable sync, and group tools, match what you'll actually use, since both list current pricing on their own sites and change it periodically.
