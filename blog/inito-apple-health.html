Does Inito Sync With Apple Health? Here's What Transfers and What Doesn't

Inito's App Store listing, its FAQ page and its website don't mention Apple Health, and Apple Health has no place to store Inito's estrogen, LH, PdG or FSH numbers anyway. This guide covers what transfers, what doesn't, and how to line your Inito readings up with Apple Watch or other wearable data.

By Veya Team October 14, 2026 7 min read

Quick Answer: Does Inito Sync With Apple Health?

As of October 14, 2026, Inito doesn't list an Apple Health integration. Neither its App Store listing nor its FAQ page mentions Apple Health.[2][3]

Even if one existed, Apple Health could only hold test results, such as a surge or no-surge result in its "Ovulation Test Result" category, not your hormone numbers.[4][5] To see whether your version writes anything, check the Apps list in the Health app on your iPhone.[6]

Inito reads four of these hormone signals. Apple Health has no fields for most of them.

What does Inito record, and what does it say about Apple Health?

Inito says its monitor measures LH, estrogen, PdG (a urine metabolite of progesterone) and FSH, and its App Store listing names the same four.[1][2] That's a wider set than a standard LH strip, and we explain how E3G, PdG and the LH surge differ in what each at-home hormone test measures.

We read the App Store listing, the FAQ page and the Inito website for any Apple Health or HealthKit wording and found none.[1][2][3] We also didn't find a documented export feature in those pages. That doesn't prove Inito never writes anything to Apple Health. It means Inito doesn't say it does, so we won't claim it does.

What can Apple Health hold from a hormone monitor?

Apple Health stores data in fixed types. For ovulation tests, HealthKit has one category called Ovulation Test Result, with values for negative, LH surge, estrogen surge and indeterminate.[4][5] Apple also has a separate Progesterone Test Result category, with values for positive, negative and indeterminate.[7] Both are result categories. Neither holds a concentration.

Inito's four-hormone view is a real strength, and it's the reason a simple sync wouldn't be enough. The detail that makes Inito useful is the same detail Apple Health can't carry.

How do you combine Inito with your Apple Watch or other wearable data?

None of these is a perfect sync. Each gets you part of the way, and you can mix them.

1. Keep the detail in the Inito app

Inito's own app is where your four-hormone readings live, so treat it as the source of truth for the numbers. Take a dated screenshot of each cycle's chart so you have your own record.

2. Check for an export or share option in your version

We couldn't find an export or report feature described on Inito's listing or FAQ.[2][3] Look in the app's settings, since features change between versions, and contact Inito support if you can't find one. You don't need an export for Veya: a screenshot of the Inito table view is what it reads.

3. Log the surge result in an app that writes to Apple Health

Since Apple Health can hold a surge or no-surge result, you can log the result of each test day in an app that reads and writes the Ovulation Test Result category.[4] You keep the numbers in Inito and the yes/no in Apple Health, which puts the timing of your LH surge on the same timeline as your Apple Watch data. For what the watch itself estimates, see how Apple Watch ovulation estimates work.

4. Use an app that reads Apple Health and lets you log LH

This is where Veya fits. It reads sleep, HRV, wrist temperature and cycle data from Apple Health, along with the Ovulation Test Result category, so your watch data and any results other apps write there land on one timeline. You can log a strip result or a urine LH number in mIU/mL yourself, and upload lab results as a PDF or photo. You can also bring the numbers in. In the Veya web app (app.myveya.com, Import), choose "Inito chart screenshot" and upload a screenshot of your chart, one cycle at a time. How can you check whether your version of Inito writes to Apple Health?

Open the Health app on your iPhone, tap your profile picture, and look under Privacy for Apps. Apple says that list shows every app that has requested access to your Health data, and you can see what each one reads or writes.[6] If Inito appears there, tap it to see which categories it's allowed to use. If it doesn't appear, it isn't connected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources and the listings cited below, checked October 14, 2026. Not medical advice.

Does Inito sync with Apple Health?

As of October 14, 2026, Inito doesn't list an Apple Health integration. Neither its App Store listing nor its FAQ page mentions Apple Health. You can check your own version in the Health app under your profile picture, then Privacy, then Apps. If Inito isn't listed there, it isn't connected.

Does Inito work with HealthKit?

Inito doesn't mention HealthKit in its App Store listing or FAQ page. HealthKit is the framework apps use to read and write Apple Health data, so the Apps list in the Health app is the quickest way to see whether your version has requested access.

Can Apple Health store Inito's estrogen, LH, PdG or FSH numbers?

Apple Health has no data types for those values. It has an Ovulation Test Result category that holds a negative, LH surge, estrogen surge or indeterminate result, so at most a surge flag could be stored. A separate Progesterone Test Result category holds only a positive, negative or indeterminate result.

Can you export your Inito data?

We couldn't find an export feature described on Inito's App Store listing or FAQ page. Check the settings in your version of the app, and keep a dated screenshot of each cycle's chart as a backup. You don't need an export for Veya: a screenshot of the Inito table view is what it reads.

How do you combine Inito with Apple Watch data?

There's no automatic route that Inito lists. A practical approach is to keep the numbers in Inito and log each surge result in an app that reads Apple Health, so it sits next to your watch's temperature and sleep data. Veya, which we make, does this for wearable data and can import your Inito readings from a chart screenshot, so they sit next to your sleep and cycle data. It isn't an Apple Health sync, because Apple Health has no place to store those hormone values.
