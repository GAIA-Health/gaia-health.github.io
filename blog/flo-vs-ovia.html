Flo vs Ovia: Cycle & Pregnancy Compared (2026)

Flo and Ovia both track your cycle and your pregnancy, but they're built around different things. Flo is the community and content app: the largest overall user base of any tracker, with a content library reviewed by medical professionals and a pregnancy mode included free. Ovia is the continuity app: it carries you from fertility tracking into pregnancy in one place, with a kick counter, contraction timer, and food-safety lookup built in, and it consolidated three separate apps into one in March 2026. Here's how they actually compare.

What Changed at Ovia in March 2026

Ovia did not shut down. In March 2026, Ovia Health consolidated its three separate apps, Ovia Fertility, Ovia Pregnancy, and Ovia Parenting, into a single app called the Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker. The standalone pregnancy app was sunset, and users were directed to the consolidated app.

Some background helps here. Ovia Health has been owned by Labcorp since 2021, and Ovia continues to offer employer and health plan versions of its programs through Labcorp. If you got Ovia through work or your insurer, that path still exists inside the consolidated app.

If you're comparing Flo and Ovia specifically because you used to use a standalone Ovia app and got redirected, it's worth knowing this wasn't a shutdown or a data loss event. It's a merge. The tracking depth and pregnancy content carried over into the single app. Pricing for the consolidated app has also shifted across versions, so check current pricing inside the app rather than relying on older articles, including older ones of ours. For the fuller story on the consolidation and other apps to consider, see our Ovia alternatives guide.

Flo: What You Get

Flo is the most-used period and pregnancy tracker in the category, with around 77 million active users. It's the app your friends are probably already using, and its pregnancy mode is included in the free tier rather than gated behind a subscription.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with AI-powered predictions
  • Pregnancy mode with week-by-week tracking, included free
  • Visual tracking of baby growth and body changes
  • Thousands of health articles reviewed by 100+ medical professionals
  • AI chatbot for pregnancy questions (Flo Premium)
  • Secret Chats for anonymous community discussions
  • Partner sharing for joint tracking
  • Weekly checklists and food safety information

Like any calendar-based tracker, Flo's AI-powered predictions are still built on cycle averages under the hood — our breakdown of why period trackers get predictions wrong covers why that math can miss regardless of which app you're using.

Strengths

The community is the largest in the category. With around 77 million active users, Flo's Secret Chats give you access to anonymous discussions with millions of other people. If you have a question about a symptom at 2am, someone in the Flo community has probably experienced it too.

The content library is extensive. Thousands of articles cover cycle, fertility, and pregnancy topics, all reviewed by medical professionals. If you want to learn while you track, Flo offers more educational content than most trackers.

Free pregnancy mode. Unlike Ovia, Flo includes pregnancy tracking in its free tier. You get week-by-week fetal development updates and pregnancy-specific content without paying, though ads are part of the free experience.

Limitations

Privacy is the big concern. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 for sharing period and pregnancy data with Facebook and Google without user consent, and a related class action against Flo and Google got preliminary court approval for a combined $59.5 million settlement in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode and says it has changed its practices.

Most pregnancy-specific tools sit behind Flo Premium. The AI chatbot and deeper insights require a subscription, and free users see regular upgrade prompts.

Limited wearable support and no nutrition tracking. Flo has an Apple Watch companion app, but no Oura, Garmin, or Fitbit integration — it's focused on cycle and pregnancy tracking rather than broader health data.

Pricing

Free: Cycle and symptom tracking, pregnancy mode, community access. Includes ads.

Flo Premium: ~$59.99/year. Adds the AI assistant, an ad-free experience, and full content library access.

Ovia: What You Get

Ovia is built around one continuous record from fertility tracking through pregnancy and into parenting. Since March 2026, that's a single consolidated app called the Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker, replacing what used to be three separate apps.

Features

  • Consolidated cycle + pregnancy tracker in one app
  • Realistic baby development illustrations
  • Bump tracker for recording your growing belly
  • Food safety lookup tool (search any food to check if it's safe)
  • Kick counter and contraction timer, included
  • Symptom tracking
  • Post-pregnancy parenting features

Strengths

Smooth transition from fertility tracking to pregnancy. If you used Ovia for cycle or fertility tracking, your data carries over into pregnancy in the same app, and the app continues into postpartum parenting features afterward.

Kick counter and contraction timer are included, not premium add-ons. These practical third-trimester tools ship as part of the core app rather than sitting behind a separate paywall.

The food safety lookup is genuinely useful. Search a specific food during pregnancy and check whether it's considered safe, a tool most competitors don't offer at all.

Limitations

No free consumer tier. The consolidated app costs roughly $150/year or $22/month, making it one of the more expensive options in the category. It's often provided at no direct cost as an employer or health-plan benefit through Labcorp, but there's no free tier if you're paying for it yourself.

Community is smaller. Ovia's community is pregnancy-focused but doesn't approach Flo's scale.

No wearable integration and no nutrition tracking beyond the food safety lookup. Ovia doesn't log meals or connect to devices like Apple Watch or Oura.

Owned by Labcorp. Employer-sponsored versions can share de-identified health data with insurers and employers.

Pricing

Free: No free consumer tier. Sometimes available at no direct cost through an employer or health plan.

Ovia Cycle & Pregnancy Tracker: ~$150/year or ~$22/month. Pricing has shifted across versions, so check current pricing in the app.

Community Support for Pregnancy Questions

Both apps have communities, with different strengths. Flo has the largest overall user base, around 77 million active users, with active in-app discussions (Secret Chats) plus an AI assistant for quick questions. Ovia's community is smaller but pregnancy-focused, and it pairs well with its week-by-week tracking and food-safety lookup.

If raw size and an AI helper matter most, Flo has the edge. If you want a pregnancy-specific community attached to a continuous cycle-to-pregnancy record, Ovia fits well. For the busiest due-date-grouped communities specifically, BabyCenter's Birth Clubs tend to be the most active of any pregnancy app, which is worth knowing if community size is your main deciding factor. Neither Flo nor Ovia groups discussions by due date the way BabyCenter's Birth Clubs do.

Is Flo Premium Worth It vs Ovia's Free Features?

This question is worth reframing before answering it, because Ovia doesn't actually have a meaningful free consumer tier the way Flo does.

Flo's free tier already includes cycle tracking, symptom logging, and a free pregnancy mode with week-by-week fetal development updates, all with ads. Flo Premium, at about $59.99/year, adds the AI chatbot, an ad-free experience, and full content library access on top of that already-usable free base.

Ovia's consolidated app costs roughly $150/year or $22/month, with no free tier for consumers who pay for it directly. It's often provided at no direct cost as an employer or health-plan benefit through Labcorp, in which case the "free" version is really a workplace benefit rather than a free consumer tier. Pricing has shifted across versions, so check the current price inside the app.

So the honest comparison isn't Flo Premium against a free Ovia tier. It's Flo's optional paid upgrade (on top of a genuinely usable free app) against Ovia's baseline subscription price (unless your employer covers it). If you're weighing Flo Premium specifically, it's worth asking whether the AI chatbot, ad-free experience, and full content library matter enough to you to pay for something Flo's free tier already covers in a more limited form. If Ovia's kick counter, contraction timer, and food-safety lookup are what you want, those come standard with the subscription rather than as a further upsell.

Which Should You Choose?

There's no single "best" app. It depends on what you care about most. Here's the honest breakdown:

Choose Flo if:

  • You want access to the largest community of people tracking cycles and pregnancies
  • You want a free pregnancy mode without paying anything upfront
  • Health education and articles are important to you
  • You need Android support
  • You're comfortable with Flo's privacy history (or willing to use Anonymous Mode)

Choose Ovia if:

  • You used Ovia for fertility tracking and want your data to carry over into pregnancy
  • A built-in kick counter and contraction timer matter to you
  • The food safety lookup tool is useful to you
  • You have employer or health-plan coverage for it, or are comfortable with the subscription cost
  • You want continuity into postpartum parenting features

Choose Go Go Gaia if:

  • You want pregnancy tracking (kick counter, contraction timer) alongside nutrition, mood, and sleep in one app
  • You track with a wearable (Apple Watch, Oura, or Garmin) and want that data connected to your pregnancy
  • You want a generous free tier with no ads
  • You're on iOS (or okay using the web app on other platforms)
  • Community and editorial content aren't what you're looking for from a tracking app
