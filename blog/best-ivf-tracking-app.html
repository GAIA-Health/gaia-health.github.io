What should an IVF tracking app actually do?

IVF tracking is fundamentally different from natural fertility tracking. You're not trying to detect ovulation. You're managing a medical treatment with precise timing requirements. Here's what matters.

Medication Management

Many IVF protocols involve several medications, often with overlapping schedules: injections at set times, another medication added mid-cycle, a trigger shot at the hour your clinic specifies, and progesterone support after transfer. Your clinic sets the details, and an app can remind you of them and keep a record of what you took.

A useful IVF app sends reminders and tracks what you've taken. Some also let you note injection sites.

Appointment and Lab Tracking

IVF involves frequent monitoring: blood tests that can include estradiol, LH, and progesterone, plus ultrasounds to count follicles and measure lining thickness. Keeping those results together makes it easier to see how your treatment is progressing and to have informed conversations with your doctor.

Embryo and Cycle Milestones

From stimulation start to egg retrieval to fertilization reports to transfer day, there are specific milestones in every IVF cycle. Tracking them helps you keep organized, especially if you're going through multiple cycles.

Emotional Wellbeing

IVF is emotionally intense. The hormones, the uncertainty, the waiting. Tracking your mood, stress levels, and sleep alongside your treatment can help you recognize patterns and communicate with your partner or therapist about how you're doing. Some people find that having an emotional record helps them feel more in control during a process that often feels out of their hands.

  • Medication scanning. Berry's site lists scanning medication labels as a premium feature, and the App Store notes use of the phone camera for medication scanning

  • Care paths for IVF, IUI, FET, and egg freezing, with treatment-stage support
  • Hormone charts, symptom logging, and lab result tracking

Limitations

Which IVF apps are safest for your data?

IVF data is extremely sensitive: treatment details, embryo information, pregnancy test results, financial data. Here's how each app handles it:

  • Berry Fertility says in its privacy policy that it may act as a HIPAA business associate when it serves healthcare providers, and that it has not sold personal information to third parties in the past twelve months. Health data handled for a partner clinic is governed by HIPAA, while your own use of the consumer app falls under Berry's policy, so read it.
  • Alife Health also builds clinic-side software. We did not find privacy details in its App Store listing, so read its privacy policy before entering treatment data.
  • Bonzun IVF is made by a Swedish company, Bonzun Health Information AB. We did not confirm a regulatory classification for the app, so check its privacy policy rather than assuming one.
  • Veya states in its App Store listing: "No ads. No selling your data. Export or delete it anytime."
  • Glow is a general fertility app. We did not confirm its current privacy practices, so read its policy before entering sensitive treatment data.

How should you track during IVF?

  1. Set up medication reminders before your cycle starts. Don't wait until day one when you're already stressed. Enter all your medications and times ahead of time so reminders are ready to go.
  2. Ask your clinic how to handle injection sites. If your app doesn't log them, a simple note (left side Monday, right side Tuesday) works. Follow your clinic's instructions on technique.
  3. Write down lab results right away. Your clinic may call with estradiol and progesterone numbers. Log them right away so you don't forget. A history across monitoring appointments is useful to bring back to your care team.
  4. Track how you're feeling, not just what you're taking. IVF medications affect your mood, energy, and sleep. Logging these alongside your treatment gives you (and your partner or therapist) a clearer picture of the emotional toll.
  5. Don't compare your numbers to others. IVF forums can be a rabbit hole of comparing follicle counts and estradiol levels. Your numbers are your numbers. Focus on your own trends, not someone else's.
  6. Keep tracking between cycles. If you're doing multiple cycles, the time between them matters too. Track your recovery, how your body returns to baseline, and any changes you make to nutrition or supplements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an app specifically for IVF?

Yes. Berry Fertility is built for IVF, IUI, FET, and egg freezing, with medication label scanning, injection videos, and clinic integration at partner clinics. Alife Health is a free app for logging IVF results, medications, and appointments. Bonzun IVF is a free app with medically reviewed IVF guidance. These differ from general fertility apps like Natural Cycles or Flo, which focus on natural conception.

What should I track during IVF?

Common things to track are medication schedules (timed as your clinic instructs), appointment dates and results, hormone levels from blood work, follicle counts from ultrasounds, embryo development updates, side effects, and your emotional wellbeing. A good IVF app helps you keep these together so you can share them with your care team.

Can I use a regular period tracker for IVF?

You can, but it's not ideal. Regular period trackers are designed for natural cycles. IVF involves controlled ovarian stimulation, egg retrieval, and embryo transfer, none of which follow natural cycle patterns. A dedicated IVF app or an all-in-one tracker with medication and lab tracking will be more helpful.

Are IVF tracking apps safe for my data?

It varies. Berry Fertility's privacy policy says it may act as a HIPAA business associate for healthcare providers and that it has not sold personal information in the past twelve months. For the other apps, we did not confirm privacy details in their listings. Always read an app's privacy policy before entering sensitive treatment data.

How many IVF cycles does the average person do?

There isn't one typical number, and it depends on age and diagnosis. A study of 156,947 UK women treated between 2003 and 2010 estimated that 65.3% had a live birth by the sixth cycle (prognosis-adjusted), and 68.4% among women under 40 using their own eggs. Rates fell with age (Smith et al., JAMA 2015). Your clinic can talk through what applies to you.

Do fertility clinics recommend specific apps?

Some do. Berry Fertility has announced partnerships with clinic networks such as Pinnacle Fertility, so some clinics offer it to patients. Most clinics don't require a specific app, though. You can choose whichever one works for your needs, and bringing organized tracking data to appointments is helpful regardless of which app you use.

Final Thoughts

IVF is a complex medical process, and keeping everything organized shouldn't add to the stress. The right app depends on what kind of support you need most.

If you want an app built for IVF, Berry Fertility is a good place to start, particularly if your clinic is a Berry partner. If you want a free place to log results and medications, Alife Health is worth trying. If you want free, medically reviewed guidance with emotional-support content, look at Bonzun IVF. If community matters most, Glow has a large community of people trying to conceive. And if you want to track the broader picture, nutrition, sleep, mood, supplements, and lab results alongside your treatment, Veya covers the health tracking side next to its IVF stage tracking.

Some people use two apps: one for IVF logistics and one for overall health. That's a perfectly good approach. The important thing is keeping yourself organized and giving your care team clear data to work with.
