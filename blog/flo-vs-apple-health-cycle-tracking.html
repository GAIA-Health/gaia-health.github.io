Apple Health's Cycle Tracking suits you if you want free, built-in period predictions and, with a compatible Apple Watch, retrospective ovulation estimates. Flo suits you if you want a large community, an article library and a Premium assistant. Veya, our app, suits you if you want cycle data next to mood, sleep and nutrition. None is birth control.

What changed in Apple Health Cycle Tracking and Flo in 2026?

Apple's Cycle Tracking support page (published September 14, 2026) now covers menopause stage tracking, cycle factors for pregnancy, lactation and contraception, and a PDF export of the last 12 months. Flo's listing (version 10.8.0, October 2026) lists a Premium Symptom Checker and TTC fertility insights.

  • Apple: Cycle deviation notifications review the previous six months of logs, and users 40 and older may be notified if patterns suggest perimenopause, per Apple. Menopause stages are set up in Health under Cycle Tracking, and symptoms can be logged on iPhone or Apple Watch. Heart rate data from Apple Watch can personalize the fertile window and is on by default (Apple Support).
  • Apple ovulation estimates: they need about two menstrual cycles of wearing a compatible Apple Watch to sleep, with Sleep Focus on for at least four hours a night on five nights (Apple Support).
  • Flo: Premium is listed as HSA/FSA eligible and includes daily insights, expert videos, a 24/7 Health Assistant, Secret Chats and TTC fertility insights; the Symptom Checker is not available in the UK, EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland (App Store listing).

For how wrist-temperature estimates work, see Apple Watch cycle estimates explained; for perimenopause, see the perimenopause tracking app comparison.

What does Apple Health's Cycle Tracking do?

Cycle Tracking lives inside the built-in Health app on iPhone, with a matching app on Apple Watch. It logs periods, symptoms and test results, predicts your next period and a six-day fertile window, tracks menopause stages, and needs no download or account beyond turning it on, per Apple Support.

Features

  • Period logging with flow level (light, medium, heavy) and spotting
  • A built-in list of symptoms to log, plus spotting and basal body temperature (Apple's page doesn't give an exact count)
  • Cycle-length and next-period predictions based on your logged history, plus a fertile window estimated with the calendar method (six days) that a positive ovulation test or heart rate data can adjust
  • Retrospective ovulation estimates from nightly wrist temperature on Apple Watch Series 8 or later, or any Apple Watch Ultra model (available only in certain countries or regions)
  • Logging for ovulation test results and basal body temperature
  • Optional cycle deviation notifications that review your previous six months of logs for irregular cycles, infrequent or prolonged periods, and persistent spotting; users 40 and older may also be notified if patterns suggest perimenopause
  • Menopause stage tracking you set up under Cycle Tracking in Health, with symptom logging on iPhone or Apple Watch
  • Cycle factors for pregnancy, lactation and contraception, and a PDF export of the last 12 months of cycle history for sharing with a healthcare provider
  • Health data is encrypted when your iPhone is locked; with two-factor authentication, Cycle Tracking data synced to iCloud is end-to-end encrypted (per Apple Support)

Strengths

It's already there. No account to create, no app to download, no ads to click past. If you have an iPhone, you have this.

Privacy protections are built in. Apple's support page says Health data is encrypted when your iPhone is locked with a passcode, Touch ID or Face ID, and that with two-factor authentication, Cycle Tracking data synced to iCloud is end-to-end encrypted, so Apple does not have the key to decrypt it (Apple Support). There's no separate account or ad model to manage on Apple's side of this comparison.

The ovulation estimate is a useful feature if you already wear an Apple Watch to sleep. Series 8 and later, plus all Ultra models, use nightly wrist temperature to estimate ovulation retrospectively, in countries and regions where the feature is available (Apple Support). Apple notes that Cycle Tracking should not be used as birth control.

Limitations

Check the symptom list before you commit. Apple's page doesn't spell out every option, so if you track something specific, open the Health app and see whether it's there.

No cross-category insights described. Apple's Cycle Tracking page describes logging, predictions and notifications rather than analysis that links your cycle to sleep, mood or workouts, so you may have to notice those patterns yourself.

The ovulation estimate is retrospective, not predictive. It estimates ovulation after the fact, and it depends on the Apple Watch models above and on wearing one to sleep.

No community. It's a logging and prediction tool, not a place to ask other people questions.

What do you get with Flo?

Flo is one of the most widely downloaded period and pregnancy trackers, with over 570 million downloads worldwide according to its App Store listing, which also shows a 4.7 rating from about 2 million ratings (as of October 2026). Where Apple Health is a quiet, built-in utility, Flo is a full app built around logging, learning, and community.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with AI-powered predictions
  • Symptom and cycle logging in a dedicated app
  • A health article library that Flo says is reviewed by medical experts
  • Secret Chats for anonymous community discussions (a Premium feature on the App Store)
  • A virtual Health Assistant (Premium)
  • Pregnancy mode with week-by-week updates (in-depth pregnancy features are Premium)
  • Partner sharing for joint tracking
  • Apple Watch companion app

Strengths

It's a dedicated tracking app. If you want to track something specific to you, compare Flo's symptom options with Apple's before choosing.

The community is a big draw. Flo's Secret Chats connect you to other people who've likely dealt with whatever you're logging.

The content library is extensive. Flo's articles cover cycle, fertility, and general health topics, which Apple's Cycle Tracking page doesn't describe offering.

Limitations

Privacy is the tradeoff. The FTC finalized an order against Flo in June 2021 after alleging Flo shared sensitive health data from millions of users with firms including Facebook and Google despite promising privacy; the order did not include a fine. A separate class action over the same 2016–2019 data sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (Flo's share: $8M; the defendants deny wrongdoing). It received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 29, 2026 (court-approved notice). Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its App Store listing describes as letting you track without personal identifiers linked to your health data. It's still a different starting posture than Apple Health's built-in encryption.

Some features sit behind Flo Premium. The virtual Health Assistant, Secret Chats and expert-led videos are listed as Premium on the App Store.

Wrist-temperature estimates are Apple's feature. Apple's retrospective ovulation estimate is part of its own Cycle Tracking; this comparison doesn't cover whether Flo offers an equivalent. Flo has an Apple Watch companion app.

Pricing

Free: Free to download, with core cycle and symptom tracking; some features sit behind Premium.

Flo Premium: listed at $47.99 and $59.99 (without billing-period labels) in the US App Store (as of October 2026). Adds the virtual Health Assistant, expert-led videos and more, per its App Store listing.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

When is Apple Health's Cycle Tracking enough?

Apple Health is enough when you want free period predictions, a six-day fertile window estimate, encrypted storage and optional Apple Watch ovulation estimates without adding an app. It is not a lesser option you are settling for, and it is the right tool for a real slice of people. Four cases:

If your main goal is knowing roughly when your next period is coming, and your cycle is fairly regular, logging a handful of periods into Apple Health will get you a reasonable prediction without installing anything.

If privacy is your top priority and you'd rather your cycle data stay under Apple's encryption rather than in a third-party app, Apple's built-in option is worth a close look. Apple's support page says Health data is encrypted when your iPhone is locked with a passcode, Touch ID or Face ID, and that with two-factor authentication, Cycle Tracking data synced to iCloud is end-to-end encrypted, so Apple does not have the key to decrypt it. There's no extra company in the loop beyond Apple itself.

If you already wear an Apple Watch to sleep, the retrospective ovulation estimate is a real feature you'd otherwise have to track manually with a basal body thermometer. You're getting something dedicated apps without wrist-temperature access can't replicate on their own.

If you don't want community features, articles, or an AI assistant, and would honestly rather your period tracker not try to be anything more than a logging tool, the simplicity is a feature, not a gap.

Where Apple Health tends to fall short is when you want to understand your cycle in the context of everything else going on with your body: sleep, mood, workouts, nutrition. It logs what you tell it and predicts your cycle from that, but it doesn't connect the dots across categories. That's the gap a dedicated app, whether that's Flo's larger feature set or an all-in-one tracker, is built to fill.

Frequently asked questions about Apple Health Cycle Tracking and Flo

Do I need a period tracking app, or is Apple Health enough?

It depends on what you want from tracking. Apple Health's Cycle Tracking is free, includes period logging, symptom logging and cycle-length predictions, plus retrospective ovulation estimates if you wear a compatible Apple Watch to sleep (Apple Support). If that's all you need, you may not need a separate app. A dedicated app like Flo adds community features, an article library and a Premium assistant. If you want to see patterns across symptoms, moods, and other health metrics, a dedicated app can add value on top of what's built in. Neither is birth control.

How accurate are Apple Health's ovulation estimates?

Apple's support page says retrospective ovulation estimates use wrist temperature from Apple Watch Series 8 or later or any Apple Watch Ultra model, and that the feature is available only in certain countries or regions (Apple Support). Apple doesn't publish an accuracy figure on that page, and the estimate looks back at ovulation rather than predicting it. Apple also says Cycle Tracking should not be used as birth control. Treat it as a helpful data point, not a guarantee.

What symptoms can you log in Apple Health's Cycle Tracking?

Apple's Cycle Tracking lets you log periods, symptoms, spotting, basal body temperature and ovulation test results (Apple Support). The page doesn't list every symptom, so check the Health app for the ones you care about. Dedicated period apps may offer different symptom options, and some, including Veya, show patterns between symptoms and other tracked data over time.

Is Flo or Apple Health better for privacy?

Apple's support page describes strong built-in protections: Health data is encrypted when your iPhone is locked, and with two-factor authentication, Cycle Tracking data synced to iCloud is end-to-end encrypted (Apple Support). The FTC finalized an order against Flo in June 2021 after alleging Flo shared sensitive health data from millions of users with firms including Facebook and Google despite promising privacy; the order did not include a fine. A separate class action over the same 2016–2019 data sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (Flo's share: $8M; the defendants deny wrongdoing). It received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 29, 2026 (court-approved notice). Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its App Store listing describes as letting you track without personal identifiers linked to your health data. Check each app's current privacy policy before you decide.

Can Apple Health Cycle Tracking sync with a period tracker app?

Many period tracker apps can read from and write to Apple Health, so your cycle data doesn't have to live in only one place. Check an app's Health integration settings before assuming data flows both directions. Apple's wrist-temperature and ovulation estimate features are part of Apple's own Cycle Tracking.

Does Apple Health Cycle Tracking support perimenopause and menopause?

It does now. Apple's Cycle Tracking support page (published September 14, 2026) says you can set up menopause stages in Health under Cycle Tracking and log symptoms on iPhone or Apple Watch. Cycle deviation notifications may also notify users 40 and older if patterns suggest perimenopause. Apple says the data should not be used to diagnose a health condition.

Does Apple Health Cycle Tracking work without an Apple Watch?

It does. You can log periods, symptoms and spotting directly in the Health app on iPhone, and you'll still get period predictions based on your logged data. What you lose without a compatible Apple Watch (Series 8 or later, or an Ultra model worn to sleep) is the retrospective ovulation estimate, since that feature depends on wrist temperature (Apple Support).
