Flo, Clue, and Stardust show up together in a lot of "which period app should I download" threads right now, and the honest answer is that they're not solving the same problem. Flo is the feature-complete option, with over 570 million downloads according to its App Store listing. Clue is the science-first option, built in Berlin under EU data law. Stardust is the newer, design-forward option, leaning into moon phases and daily hormone narratives in a way that's made it one of the apps Gen Z talks about most on TikTok.

All three have a documented privacy record worth reading before you hand over cycle data, and the differences between them are bigger than a features list alone would suggest. We'll walk through what you get with each, then spend real time on the privacy architecture, because that's the part most comparisons skip.

Which is better: Flo, Clue, or Stardust?

None wins on every measure. As of October 2026 all three are rated 4.7 to 4.8 on the US App Store (Flo about 2 million ratings, Clue 406,000, Stardust 119,000), so the split is community and depth for Flo, privacy and irregular-cycle logging for Clue, and a daily-ritual feel for Stardust. What do you get with Clue?

Clue is made by BioWink GmbH in Berlin and has built its identity around a science-first approach to cycle prediction. A 2016 Obstetrics & Gynecology study that scored 20 accurate, free cycle apps gave Clue the top score (13 of 15). Its App Store listing shows a 4.8 out of 5 rating from about 406K ratings (as of October 2026).

Features

  • Cycle and symptom tracking with ovulation and fertile-window predictions and a "clinically validated algorithm" claim (per its App Store listing)
  • Modes for period tracking, trying to conceive, pregnancy, and perimenopause
  • Connects to Apple Watch, Oura, WHOOP, Withings, Fitbit and more (per its App Store listing)
  • Clue Plus adds deeper analysis, advanced predictions, and personalized insights
  • Tracking for 200+ symptoms and experiences, per its App Store listing

Pros

  • A strong privacy reputation: Berlin-based and GDPR-governed, with a privacy policy stating sensitive health data is never sold to advertisers
  • A science-first build, with a published study ranking it first among 20 accurate free apps
  • Detailed symptom logging that suits cycles that aren't textbook

Cons

  • Deeper analysis and advanced predictions sit behind Clue Plus ($9.99/month or $39.99/year as of October 2026), and free users may see prompts to upgrade
  • A smaller community than Flo's, with fewer discussion features
  • No astrology or lifestyle framing if that's part of what keeps you engaged with a tracker

Pricing

Flo: FTC Order and Class Action Settlement

The FTC finalized an order against Flo in June 2021 after alleging Flo shared sensitive health data from millions of users with firms including Facebook and Google despite promising privacy; the order did not include a fine. A separate class action over the same 2016–2019 data sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (Flo's share: $8M; the defendants deny wrongdoing). It received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 29, 2026 (court-approved notice).

Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its App Store listing describes as letting you track without personal identifiers linked to your health data. These are real changes, made after years of public scrutiny. Whether that history changes your decision is a personal call, and it's worth reading Flo's current privacy policy directly.

Clue: Berlin, GDPR, and Its Public Statements

Clue is based in Berlin, Germany (BioWink GmbH), so EU GDPR applies to its handling of your data. Clue said in June 2022 that it would not respond to any disclosure request or subpoena of its users' health data by US authorities and had never received such a request (Clue statement). Its privacy policy says tracked health data is never shared with or sold to advertisers, while disclosing that ad networks receive technical data such as app-install and subscription events, and notes that non-EU processors could be subject to access by public authorities. Clue's App Store listing describes the app as a regulated medical device, but its privacy label still lists identifiers used to track you. We found no FTC action or class action against Clue in our research.

Stardust: 2022 Criticism, Then a 2026 Mozilla Review

In June 2022, TechCrunch reported that Stardust periodically sent users' phone numbers to the analytics firm Mixpanel (TechCrunch saw no health data shared with it), and that Stardust's promised end-to-end encryption was unclear: the founder described standard SSL traffic and AES-256 storage on AWS. Its privacy policy at the time allowed disclosure to law enforcement "whether or not legally required," which drew attention after Roe v. Wade was overturned. After TechCrunch got in touch, Stardust revised the policy to remove its end-to-end encryption mentions.

On July 16, 2026, Mozilla published a hands-on review of six period trackers. TechCrunch reported that Stardust was the only one of the six to send sensitive health data (birthdate, contraceptive method, family-planning intentions and symptoms) to another company, the analytics firm RudderStack, tied to an identifier rather than a name. A Stardust spokesperson said RudderStack is "contractually prohibited from selling or using it for its own purposes." Stardust told TechCrunch it "has never received any requests, demands, or legal process for user data from authorities or third parties." Read the current version of the policy yourself if this is a deciding factor for you.

Taken together, the picture on paper: Flo's record includes an FTC order and a class action that Flo has settled while denying wrongdoing, alongside real changes since. None of that means any one app is "unsafe," but it's worth knowing before you decide where years of period, mood, and symptom data lives.

Should you choose Flo, Clue, or Stardust?

There is no single best app here; the right one is the tracker you will open consistently and are comfortable with on privacy. Flo suits community and depth, Clue suits privacy-minded and irregular-cycle logging, Stardust suits people who want a daily ritual, and Veya suits people who want cycle data next to other health data. Frequently asked questions about Flo, Clue, and Stardust

How do Flo, Clue, and Stardust compare?

All three track your cycle, but they're built around different priorities. Flo has a very large community and a deep content library (Flo Premium is listed at $47.99–$59.99). Clue is a Berlin-based, science-first app (Clue Plus is $39.99/year). Stardust wraps cycle tracking in moon-phase and astrology content (annual plans are listed at $24.99–$39.99). Prices are from the US App Store as of October 2026. Standard tracking in all three is not birth control. Which one fits depends on whether you want community and depth, science and privacy, or a tracker that feels like a daily ritual.

Is Clue or Flo better for irregular cycles?

Clue's App Store listing says you can track symptoms even without regular bleeding, and it offers separate modes for periods, trying to conceive, pregnancy and perimenopause. Flo's listing covers core cycle tracking free and puts the Symptom Checker and TTC insights in Premium. Neither app diagnoses anything. Pricing as of October 2026: Clue Plus $39.99/year, Flo Premium listed at $47.99 and $59.99.

Which is best for privacy: Flo, Clue, or Stardust?

Clue is based in Berlin and subject to EU GDPR, and said in 2022 it would not respond to US disclosure requests for user health data (Clue statement). The FTC finalized an order against Flo in June 2021 after alleging Flo shared sensitive health data from millions of users with firms including Facebook and Google despite promising privacy; the order did not include a fine. A separate class action over the same 2016–2019 data sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (Flo's share: $8M; the defendants deny wrongdoing). It received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 29, 2026 (court-approved notice). Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its App Store listing describes as letting you track without personal identifiers linked to your health data. For Stardust, in 2022, TechCrunch reported that Stardust periodically sent users' phone numbers to the analytics firm Mixpanel (TechCrunch saw no health data shared with it), and that Stardust's promised end-to-end encryption was unclear: the founder described standard SSL traffic and AES-256 storage on AWS. Its privacy policy at the time allowed disclosure to law enforcement "whether or not legally required," which drew attention after Roe v. Wade was overturned. After TechCrunch got in touch, Stardust revised the policy to remove its end-to-end encryption mentions. Check each app's current privacy policy yourself.

Which app has the best free version: Flo, Clue, or Stardust?

All three are free to download, but they differ in what's included. Flo lists some features, such as its virtual Health Assistant, as Premium. Clue's free version covers cycle and symptom tracking, with Clue Plus adding deeper analysis, advanced predictions, and personalized insights. Stardust is free with in-app purchases. Compare what's free in each app before subscribing.

Is Clue a medical device?

Clue's App Store listing describes the app as a regulated medical device. Its standard period and cycle tracking is not birth control. Clue also received a 2021 FDA clearance for a separate Clue Birth Control feature, but its current listing doesn't list a birth-control mode. A regulated-device status for prediction features is not a promise that predictions are accurate for you.

What happened with Stardust's privacy policy in 2026?

Mozilla published a review of six period trackers on July 16, 2026, and TechCrunch reported that Stardust was the only one of the six to send sensitive health data to another company (RudderStack), tied to an identifier rather than a name. This came after the 2022 criticism over phone numbers shared with an analytics firm and unclear encryption claims (TechCrunch, 2022).

Does Stardust have a postpartum mode?

It does now. Stardust's App Store listing says version 4.0.0 (October 2026) added Postpartum Mode. It already had Pregnancy Mode, and its listing mentions AR baby-size views and partner tracking. The listing does not say which features are free and which are paid.

What's a good alternative to Flo, Clue, and Stardust?

Veya, our app, is worth a look if you want cycle tracking folded into a broader health picture, tracking 350+ metrics across mood, sleep, fitness, and nutrition with 1-click logging and correlation insights showing how your lifestyle connects to your cycle. Core features are free with no ads. The tradeoffs are that it's iOS only (a web app covers other platforms), doesn't have Flo's community or Stardust's daily content, and is a newer, smaller app than any of the three.

Can you use Flo, Clue, or Stardust as birth control?

Standard period and cycle tracking in Flo, Clue, and Stardust is not birth control, and none of them should be used as contraception. Natural Cycles is an app the FDA has authorized for contraception (FDA De Novo grant). Clue also received a 2021 FDA clearance for a separate Clue Birth Control feature, but its current App Store listing doesn't list a birth-control mode. If app-based birth control is what you're after, that's a conversation to have with a healthcare provider.
