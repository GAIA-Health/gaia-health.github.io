Flo vs Clue vs Stardust: 2026 Period App Showdown

These three keep showing up in the same TikTok comment sections, but they're not really competing for the same reason you'd open a tracker. Flo is the polished, community-heavy option. Clue is the science-first, Berlin-based, CE-marked app that privacy people recommend. Stardust is the moon-phase, astrology-flavored option that's made cycle tracking feel like a daily ritual for a lot of Gen Z users. This guide covers what you get with each, a full privacy-architecture comparison, and a fourth option built around a different premise entirely.

Flo: What You Get

Flo is the most-used period tracker in the category, with around 77 million active users and over 300 million registered accounts. It's the app most of your friends probably already have, and that scale shows up in the feature list.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with AI-powered predictions
  • AI Health Assistant that checks in on symptoms like cramps, fatigue, and mood
  • Thousands of health articles reviewed by 100+ medical professionals
  • Secret Chats: anonymous community discussions, with about 7 million people chatting monthly
  • Pregnancy mode with week-by-week tracking, included free
  • Apple Health integration and an Apple Watch companion app

Pros

  • The largest community in the category, with anonymous discussions on nearly any symptom or question
  • An extensive, medically reviewed content library
  • Free pregnancy mode with week-by-week updates

Cons

  • Free users see ads, and the AI Health Assistant sits behind Flo Premium (~$59.99/year as of 2026)
  • No nutrition, habit, or sleep tracking built in
  • The privacy history (below) is the biggest thing to weigh before committing your data

Pricing

Free: Cycle and symptom tracking, pregnancy mode, community access. Includes ads.

Flo Premium: ~$12.99/month or ~$59.99/year as of 2026. Adds the AI Health Assistant, an ad-free experience, and full content library access. Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

Clue: What You Get

Clue was founded in Berlin in 2012 and has built its identity around a science-first approach to cycle prediction. It's the tracker that scientists, researchers, and privacy advocates tend to recommend, and its algorithm has been studied in published research on menstrual cycle variability.

Features

  • Cycle and symptom tracking with prediction algorithms informed by published research
  • CE mark in the EU for its cycle-tracking and ovulation-prediction features, reflecting regulatory-grade validation there
  • Wearable sync (Oura, Fitbit, WHOOP) with Clue Plus
  • Pregnancy mode and deeper cycle insights with Clue Plus
  • A clean, minimal interface with no ads

Pros

  • The strongest privacy reputation of the three, with a decade-long clean track record
  • A science-first build, with a CE mark reflecting regulatory validation in the EU
  • Flexible, detailed symptom logging that works well for cycles that aren't textbook

Cons

  • The free tier is thinner than it used to be. Pregnancy mode, wearable sync, and deeper insights are locked behind Clue Plus (~$39.99/year), and free users see frequent prompts to upgrade
  • Smaller community than Flo, with less content and fewer discussion features
  • No astrology or lifestyle framing if that's part of what keeps you engaged with a tracker

Pricing

Free: Basic cycle and symptom tracking.

Clue Plus: ~$39.99/year as of 2026. Adds pregnancy mode, wearable sync, and deeper cycle insights. Check current pricing in-app.

Download: Available on iOS and Android

Stardust: What You Get

Stardust pairs standard period tracking with moon phases, hormone-stage explanations, and a distinct visual style that's made it one of the more-discussed period apps on TikTok. It has more than 5 million downloads and a 4.76 out of 5 rating across roughly 110,000 App Store ratings.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with a daily readout of where you are hormonally and what that can feel like
  • Cycle syncing content that aligns your cycle with lunar phases, plus a moon calendar
  • "Cycle Horoscope" and zodiac-matched content
  • Cycle Recap, added in 2026, summarizing your last cycle's sleep and activity patterns by phase
  • Friend syncing so you can compare cycles with people you share the app with

Pros

  • Genuinely fun to open, which is a real reason people stick with a tracker instead of abandoning it after a week
  • Core cycle tracking, symptom logging, and daily content are all free
  • Has made real, credit-worthy changes since 2022, including dropping an overstated encryption claim and law-enforcement language

Cons

  • Symptom tracking is lighter than Flo's or Clue's, and there's no pregnancy mode
  • Requires an account, with no local-only option for data that stays entirely on-device
  • Its privacy policy has changed twice in the last seven months as of mid-2026, each revision disclosing more ad-partner data sharing (see below)

Pricing

Free: Core cycle and symptom tracking, moon-phase and hormone content.

Stardust Plus: ~$2.99/week, ~$3.99/month, or ~$24.99/year as of 2026. Adds deeper insights, unlimited friend syncs, and additional content. Check current pricing in-app.

Flo: FTC Settlement, Then a 2025 Verdict

In 2021, the FTC settled with Flo over sharing users' health data with companies including Facebook and Google without proper consent. A related class action against Flo and Google got preliminary court approval for a combined $59.5 million settlement in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025 for its role in receiving that data.

Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which separates your health data from your identity, and open-sourced it. These are real changes, made after years of public scrutiny. Whether that history changes your decision is a personal call, and it's worth reading Flo's current privacy policy directly.

Clue: Berlin, GDPR, and a CE Mark

Clue is based in Berlin, Germany, which means it's subject to strict EU GDPR privacy laws. It has publicly committed to not sharing user data with US authorities and states it does not sell personal data. Its cycle and ovulation-prediction features also carry a CE mark in the EU, a regulatory designation tied to its science-first approach to how it builds and validates predictions. Clue has no major public enforcement actions or class actions on record.

Stardust: 2022 Criticism, Revised Since, With New Scrutiny in 2026

In 2022, reporting found that Stardust had shared users' phone numbers with a third-party analytics service, and that the app had marketed itself as "encrypted" using language that implied end-to-end encryption when its actual encryption was standard, not end-to-end. Its privacy policy at the time also stated it would comply with law enforcement requests for user data whether or not legally required to, a detail that drew attention after Roe v. Wade was overturned that year. Stardust has since made real changes: it removed the overstated encryption claim and dropped the "whether or not legally required" language, and the company says it has never actually received a law enforcement request for user data.

More recently, a Mozilla Foundation review published in July 2026 found that Stardust's privacy policy had been revised twice in roughly seven months, with each revision disclosing more device and advertising data sharing, including naming Meta's and Google's ad-measurement tools as data partners as of May 2026. Its separate Health Data Privacy Policy, unchanged since October 2025, still states that health data itself sits under a randomized ID, separated from your name or contact information. Read the current version of both policies yourself if this is a deciding factor for you.

Taken together, the three apps sit in a fairly clear order on paper: Clue has the cleanest and longest public record, Flo has the most public enforcement history balanced against real remediation, and Stardust is the newest and least tested of the three, with privacy terms that are still actively changing. None of that means any one app is "unsafe," but it's the kind of thing worth knowing before you decide where three years of period, mood, and symptom data lives.

Choose Flo if:

  • You want the deepest feature set and the largest community of people tracking cycles
  • Health education and medically reviewed articles matter to you
  • You want a free pregnancy mode
  • You're comfortable with Flo's privacy history, or willing to use Anonymous Mode

Choose Clue if:

  • Privacy is your top priority in a tracker

Frequently Asked Questions

How do Flo, Clue, and Stardust compare?

All three track your cycle, but they're built around different priorities. Flo has the largest community and the deepest content library (Flo Premium about $59.99/year). Clue is a Berlin-based, CE-marked app built on a science-first approach with a strong privacy reputation (Clue Plus about $39.99/year). Stardust wraps cycle tracking in moon-phase and astrology content aimed at making tracking feel like a daily ritual (Stardust Plus about $24.99/year). None of the three are birth control. Which one fits depends on whether you want community and depth, science and privacy, or a tracker that feels fun to open.

Which is best for privacy: Flo, Clue, or Stardust?

Clue has the strongest privacy reputation of the three. It's based in Berlin, Germany, subject to strict EU GDPR rules, and CE-marked as a medical device, with a clean public record. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing health data with Facebook and Google. A related class action against Flo and Google got preliminary court approval for a combined $59.5 million settlement in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode. Stardust drew criticism in 2022 for sharing phone numbers with a third-party analytics service and overstating its encryption. It has since revised its practices, but a Mozilla Foundation review in July 2026 found its privacy policy had been revised twice in about seven months, disclosing more ad-partner data sharing, including with Meta's and Google's ad-measurement tools as of May 2026. Read each company's current privacy policy before you decide.

Which app has the best free version: Flo, Clue, or Stardust?

All three have usable free tiers, but they differ in what's included. Flo's free tier includes pregnancy mode but shows ads. Clue's free tier covers basic cycle and symptom logging, but pregnancy mode, wearable sync, and deeper insights sit behind Clue Plus. Stardust's core cycle tracking and daily content are free, with deeper insights and unlimited friend syncing behind Stardust Plus. If a fully free experience with pregnancy tracking matters most, Flo has the edge. If ad-free basic tracking matters more, Clue or Stardust may fit better.

Is Clue a medical device?

Clue carries a CE mark in the EU for its cycle-tracking and ovulation-prediction features, which means it meets certain regulatory standards for a software medical device there. That's different from being FDA-cleared as birth control in the US, which Clue is not, and its own app guidance says not to use it as contraception. The CE mark reflects Clue's science-first approach to how it builds and validates its predictions, not a claim that it prevents pregnancy.

What happened with Stardust's privacy policy in 2026?

A Mozilla Foundation review published in July 2026 found that Stardust's privacy policy had been revised twice in roughly seven months, with each revision disclosing more device and advertising data sharing, including naming Meta's and Google's ad-measurement tools as data partners as of May 2026. This came after Stardust's 2022 criticism over sharing phone numbers with a third-party analytics service and overstating its encryption, both of which the company addressed at the time. Stardust's separate Health Data Privacy Policy, unchanged since October 2025, still states health data sits under a randomized ID separated from your name or contact information. Read the current version of both policies yourself before logging sensitive symptoms.

What's a good alternative to Flo, Clue, and Stardust?

Go Go Gaia is worth a look if you want cycle tracking folded into a broader health picture, tracking 350+ metrics across mood, sleep, fitness, and nutrition with 1-click logging and correlation insights showing how your lifestyle connects to your cycle. Core features are free with no ads. The tradeoffs are that it's iOS only (a web app covers other platforms), doesn't have Flo's community or Stardust's daily content, and is a newer, smaller app than any of the three.

Can you use Flo, Clue, or Stardust as birth control?

Flo, Clue, and Stardust are all period and cycle trackers, and none of them are cleared by the FDA to prevent pregnancy. Clue's own app guidance states it should not be used as a contraceptive, and neither Flo nor Stardust markets itself as one. If app-based birth control is what you're after, that's a distinct category of app (Natural Cycles is the FDA-cleared example) and a conversation to have with a healthcare provider.
