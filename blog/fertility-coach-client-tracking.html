Client Tracking for Fertility Coaches

A look at what a shared client view actually shows a fertility coach, cycle phase, predicted fertile window, BBT trend, and OPK results, and where that view ends and her clinic's care begins.

By Go Go Gaia Team • Published August 26, 2026 • 8 min read • For Coaches

Educational content for fertility coaches and wellness practitioners, not medical advice. Predictions shown in a client's app are estimates built from her own logged data, not medical determinations, and clinical decisions belong with her doctor or fertility clinic.

Quick Answer

A fertility coach working with a client who shares her Go Go Gaia data can see her current cycle phase, her fertile-window status alongside a predicted ovulation date and window with a confidence indicator, a BBT trend chart, her LH or ovulation test results, cervical mucus observations, and a confirmed ovulation date once one's been logged. It's data her app computed from what she tracked, shared on her terms and revocable any time, not a diagnosis or a conception guarantee. Clinical questions still go to her clinic.

Fertility coaching runs on detail. You're trying to understand a client's cycle, her symptoms, her stress load, and how consistent her tracking has been, usually across several months, sometimes longer. And most of that detail lives in a screenshot she sent you last Tuesday, a different screenshot from three weeks ago, and whatever she remembers to mention on your call.

This guide covers the screenshot-relay problem most fertility coaches run into, exactly what a shared client view shows once she connects you in Go Go Gaia, how consent and revocation actually work, and where a coach's role stops and her clinic's begins.

The Screenshot Relay Problem in Fertility Coaching

A client tracking her cycle for fertility reasons is usually logging more than most people ever do: basal body temperature every morning, ovulation test strips, cervical mucus, maybe hormone labs her clinic ran. That's valuable information for a coach helping her stay organized and consistent. It's also information that, without a shared view, has to travel through text messages and photos of a phone screen.

That relay breaks down in predictable ways. A screenshot gets sent without the date visible. A temperature reading looks off and neither of you can tell if it's a bad thermometer read or a real signal, because you're looking at one number instead of the trend around it. By the time you're piecing together a pattern across a full cycle, you're reconstructing it from a dozen separate messages instead of seeing it laid out.

None of that is a client being disorganized. It's what happens when the data lives in her app and the conversation happens somewhere else entirely.

What a Shared View Actually Shows

When a client invites you into Go Go Gaia's coach portal and shares her fertility data, here's what you're looking at. This is a factual list of what's in the view, not a description of what it does for her outcomes.

Cycle phase and fertile window: her current phase in the app's five-phase model, plus whether she's in her fertile window right now, alongside a predicted ovulation date and window with a confidence indicator based on her own logged history.

BBT trend: her basal body temperature charted over time, so a single unusual reading reads as one point on a trend instead of an isolated number you have no context for.

LH and ovulation test results: whatever ovulation predictor kit results she's logged, in sequence, instead of a photo of a single test line.

Cervical mucus observations: what she's recorded day to day, alongside the rest of the cycle picture rather than as a separate note.

Confirmed ovulation date: once her data supports one, distinct from the earlier prediction.

Context she's chosen to share: sleep, symptoms, habits, and hormone labs as logged values, if she's included those in what she shares with you.

That's the panel. It's data her app computed and organized from what she logged, handed to you in one place instead of scattered across messages. It doesn't come with a diagnosis attached, and it isn't built to tell either of you whether a given cycle will end in a pregnancy. What it does is let you spend your time reading a pattern instead of requesting and reassembling one.

Consent, and Taking It Back Any Time

None of this is visible to you by default. A client has to actively invite you in, and she decides what she's sharing when she does it. Nothing about working with a fertility coach requires her to hand over her whole health record, and she should never feel like sharing is a condition of the relationship.

She can adjust what's shared or revoke your access entirely at any point, for any reason, without needing to explain why. If her circumstances change, whether that's pausing tracking, switching clinics, or just wanting more privacy for a while, the access she granted changes with her, not on a schedule you control.

This is the same consent model the portal uses across every professional relationship, not something specific to fertility data. If you want the client-facing version of how this works, in case a client asks you directly, our piece on what a coach can and can't see walks through the invite and revoke flow from her side.

Working Alongside Her Clinic, Not Instead of It

A fertility coach's role and a fertility clinic's role aren't the same job, and the portal is built around that difference. What you're offering is organization, encouragement, and support around the parts of her life that affect how consistently she tracks and how she's doing day to day. That's genuinely useful, and it's a different thing from diagnosing, interpreting labs, or advising on timing.

The predicted ovulation window in her app is an estimate built from her own logged data, shown with a confidence indicator so neither of you mistakes it for certainty. It's there to help her (and you) understand her pattern, not to replace what her clinic tells her or to guide decisions about timing or treatment. Any hormone labs she's logged are exactly that: values she typed in after her clinic ran them, not a live feed from the lab and not something for you to interpret medically.

When something in her data raises a real question, the honest move is the same one you'd already make: point her back to her clinic. Your value is in being the person who's actually looking at her full picture between appointments, not in replacing the appointments themselves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly can I see about a client's fertility data?

Once a client connects you and shares her fertility data, you can see her current cycle phase, her fertile-window status alongside the app's predicted ovulation date and window with a confidence indicator, a BBT trend chart, her logged LH or ovulation test results, cervical mucus observations, and a confirmed ovulation date once she's logged one. Depending on what else she's shared, you may also see sleep, symptoms, habits, and hormone labs as logged values. You never see more than what's in her app and what she's chosen to share with you.

Can a client share her BBT chart but not everything else?

Sharing isn't an all-or-nothing switch on her account. A client chooses what to share when she connects with you, and she can adjust or fully revoke that access at any time from her end. Whatever she's granted shows up as connected on your side, so you're always working from what she's actually agreed to share rather than guessing.

What happens if she gets pregnant?

There's no separate pregnancy view built into the coach portal right now. What stays constant is that she's always in control of what's shared with you, and she can narrow, pause, or revoke access whenever her needs or her tracking change. If she becomes pregnant, that's a conversation for her and her clinic, and she decides what, if anything, she keeps sharing with you.

Do her clinic's lab results come through automatically?

Her clinic's lab results don't come through automatically. There's no live feed from any clinic, lab, or fertility center. What you see is limited to whatever she's manually logged in her own app, including values she typed in herself after getting results from her clinic. Treat those numbers as her personal record-keeping, not a clinical result for you to read into.

Is this the same as timed-intercourse or conception advice?

This isn't timed-intercourse or conception advice, and it isn't meant to be used that way. The portal shows you her logged data so you can be a more organized, informed coach around her week, her energy, and how she's doing, not so you can advise on timing or conception. Any question about timing, treatment, or what a result means belongs with her fertility clinic or doctor.

How accurate is the predicted ovulation date?

It's an estimate built from her own logged data, shown with a confidence indicator, not a medical determination. Some clients have very predictable cycles and some don't, which is exactly why the app also shows a confirmed ovulation date once her BBT shift or test results support one. Lean on the confirmed date over the prediction when the two don't line up, and leave the interpretation of anything uncertain to her clinic.
