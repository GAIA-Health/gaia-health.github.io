Clue vs Flo vs Natural Cycles: 2026 Comparison

Natural Cycles is the only one of the three with an FDA De Novo grant for contraception, so it suits people who want app-based birth control. Clue suits people who want a science-led, GDPR-based tracker; Flo suits people who want a large content library and community features. Veya is a tracker, not birth control.

Quick Answer

Here's how the three split by use case, with sources linked where we cite facts (App Store details are as of October 2026):

  • If you want birth control you can rely on: Go with Natural Cycles. It holds an FDA De Novo grant for preventing or planning pregnancy and estimates ovulation retrospectively from your temperature.
  • If privacy matters most in a tracker: Go with Clue. It's headquartered in Berlin, and its App Store listing says your data is protected by strict European privacy law (GDPR).
  • If you want a large community and content library: Go with Flo. Its listing cites over 570 million downloads (Flo's own figure) and daily articles, though it isn't birth control.
  • If you want all-in-one tracking (cycle + mood + fitness + nutrition + habits) with a free tier: Try Veya.

Clue, Flo, and Natural Cycles show up in the same search results and the same "which app should I use" conversations, so it's easy to assume they're three competitors doing the same job. They aren't. Clue and Flo are both period and cycle trackers that predict your cycle from what you log. Natural Cycles is a birth control method that happens to live in an app, and it's the only one of the three with an FDA De Novo grant for preventing pregnancy.

That structural difference is the thing to understand before anything else here. Once it's clear, the rest of the comparison, free tiers, privacy, and how each handles an irregular cycle, is easier to reason about.

What is the real difference between Clue, Flo, and Natural Cycles?

Clue predicts. It looks at the cycle and symptom data you log and estimates when your period and fertile window will probably happen. Clue has a science-led reputation (a 2016 Obstetrics & Gynecology study gave it the top score, 13 of 15, among 20 accurate, free cycle apps), but those estimates aren't measuring what your body is doing on a given day, and we found no FDA authorization for Clue as contraception.

Flo predicts too. It makes predictions from the cycle data you log, plus a content library and community features. Flo does not market itself as birth control, and its estimates carry the same limitation as Clue's: they're calendar-based, not a measurement of ovulation.

Natural Cycles measures. It uses your basal body temperature to confirm whether you've ovulated, then tells you whether today is a "green day" (not fertile) or a "red day" (use protection or abstain). That measurement approach is what the FDA reviewed when it granted Natural Cycles a De Novo request in 2018. In the FDA's decision summary, real-world data from 15,570 women gave a typical-use Pearl Index of 6.5 (about 6.5 pregnancies per 100 women per year) and a perfect-use Pearl Index of 1. On red days, it tells you to abstain or use a barrier method; the app itself doesn't physically prevent pregnancy, it tells you which days carry risk so you can act on it.

For general cycle and symptom tracking, Clue and Flo are the closer comparison to each other. If a hormone-free, app-based contraception method is what you want, Natural Cycles is the distinct option.

Which free version should you start with?

Clue and Flo are both free to download with in-app purchases, while Natural Cycles is subscription-based ($21.99/month or $149.99/year as of October 2026; we didn't find a free tier on its listing).

Clue is free to download, and its listing prices Clue Plus at $39.99/year or $9.99/month, describing Plus as adding deeper analysis, advanced predictions, and personalized insights.

Flo is free to download with core tools included, per its listing. Annual Premium plans are listed at $47.99–$59.99 (as of October 2026), and Premium is listed as adding the virtual health assistant, Secret Chats, and in-depth pregnancy features.

Natural Cycles is priced as a subscription, with a free NC° Band offered on the annual plan per its listing. It's a regulated medical device you're paying to use as a contraceptive method, not a tracker with optional upgrades.

If a free option matters most, Clue or Flo is the place to start, and it's worth comparing pregnancy features in both apps before paying. Natural Cycles makes sense if you specifically want its contraception feature.

Which of the three is better for privacy?

Cycle and fertility data is some of the most sensitive information you can put in an app, so this is worth reading closely rather than skimming.

Clue leans on GDPR. It's headquartered in Berlin, and its App Store listing says your data is protected by strict European privacy law (GDPR). Apple's privacy label on the listing shows identifiers used for tracking, so read its privacy policy.

Flo has a documented enforcement history. In June 2021 the FTC finalized an order against Flo over allegations that it shared sensitive health data with companies including Facebook and Google despite promising privacy; the order requires consent before sharing health information and carried no fine. A separate class action over the same 2016–2019 data sharing reached a proposed $59.5M settlement with Google, Flo and Flurry (Flo’s share: $8M; the defendants deny wrongdoing). It received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 29, 2026. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which its listing describes as using the app without personal identifiers linked to your health data. Whether that's enough is a personal call, but the history is worth knowing before you hand over your data.

Natural Cycles is a regulated medical device (see its FDA De Novo grant), and its App Store seller is NaturalCycles Nordic AB. We didn't verify its data practices, so as with any health app, read the current privacy policy to see exactly what's collected and shared.

Read each company's current privacy policy and decide what you're comfortable with, especially if app-based contraception data is part of your concern.

Which of the three handles irregular cycles?

This is where the tracker-vs-method distinction matters most, because "handles irregular cycles well" means something different for a tracker than it does for a contraceptive method.

As trackers, Clue and Flo both predict from calendar and symptom history. Calendar-based predictions generally get less reliable when cycles vary a lot, and neither app can fix that. Clue's detailed symptom logging may suit people whose cycles aren't textbook, since you can record whatever pattern shows up, but we haven't seen a head-to-head test.

Natural Cycles works differently, and that changes the calculus. Instead of guessing fertile days from a calendar, it estimates ovulation retrospectively from your daily temperature. That's an advantage in principle, but temperature-based methods generally work best with fairly regular cycles and consistent measurement timing, so it may be a weaker fit if your cycles vary a lot. Disrupted sleep, travel, and inconsistent measurement timing can degrade the temperature signal it depends on.

If your cycles are unpredictable and you mainly want to understand them, a flexible tracker like Clue is the more forgiving choice day to day. If you're evaluating Natural Cycles specifically as a method and your cycles are irregular, that's a conversation to have with a healthcare provider about whether temperature-based tracking will work reliably for you.

What About Stardust?

Stardust comes up in the same searches because it's a well-rated period tracker: its App Store listing shows 4.8 stars from about 119K ratings (as of October 2026). Like Clue and Flo, it's a tracker, not a contraceptive, so it sits closer to that pair than to Natural Cycles.

Per the listing, Stardust pairs cycle tracking with daily insights, moon and zodiac features, and partner sharing. It's free to download with in-app subscriptions, includes a week-by-week Pregnancy Mode, and says "We never sell or share your personal health information" (Apple's privacy label on the same listing still shows identifiers used for tracking). We didn't verify its Android availability.

In short: if Clue and Flo feel clinical or like a chore, Stardust is built around daily insights and a more playful design. It's not a substitute for Natural Cycles if what you actually want is app-based contraception.
