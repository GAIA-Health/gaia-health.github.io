Clue vs Flo vs Natural Cycles: 2026 Comparison

These three come up together constantly, but they aren't really three versions of the same app. Clue is privacy-first and science-led. Flo has the largest community and content library. Natural Cycles is the one of the three that's FDA-cleared as birth control, confirming ovulation from your temperature instead of predicting it from a calendar. Here's what that means for free versions, privacy, and irregular cycles.

The Real Difference: Two Trackers and a Method

Clue predicts. It looks at the cycle and symptom data you log and estimates when your period and fertile window will probably happen. Clue is built on a science-first approach and has a strong reputation for it, but those estimates aren't measuring what your body is doing on a given day, and Clue's own guidance says not to use it as contraception.

Flo predicts too. It uses AI-powered predictions on the cycle data you log, plus a large content library and community. Flo does not market itself as birth control, and its estimates carry the same limitation as Clue's: they're calendar-based, not a measurement of ovulation.

Natural Cycles measures. It uses your basal body temperature to confirm whether you've ovulated, then tells you whether today is a "green day" (not fertile) or a "red day" (use protection or abstain). That measurement is why it could be cleared by the FDA as contraception in 2018 and a calendar tracker could not. As a method, Natural Cycles reports 93% effectiveness with typical use and 98% with perfect use. On red days, it tells you to abstain or use a barrier method; the app itself doesn't physically prevent pregnancy, it tells you which days carry risk so you can act on it.

For general cycle and symptom tracking, Clue and Flo are the closer comparison to each other. If a hormone-free, app-based contraception method is what you want, Natural Cycles is the distinct option.

Free Version Comparison

Clue and Flo both have genuinely usable free tiers for logging periods and symptoms, while Natural Cycles has no meaningful free version because its FDA-cleared method is subscription-based (about $99.99 per year, including a thermometer).

Clue's free tier covers basic cycle and symptom tracking, but it's thinner than it used to be. Pregnancy mode, wearable sync (Oura, Fitbit, WHOOP), and deeper insights are all locked behind Clue Plus (~$39.99/year), and free users see frequent full-screen prompts to upgrade.

Flo's free tier is more generous in one specific way: it includes pregnancy mode with week-by-week fetal development tracking, along with basic cycle and symptom tracking and community access. The trade-off is ads, and Flo Premium (~$59.99/year) is needed for the AI assistant and an ad-free experience.

Natural Cycles only offers a trial, not a permanent free tier. That's a function of what it is: a regulated medical device you're paying to use as a contraceptive method, not a tracker with optional upgrades.

If a free option matters most, Clue or Flo is the place to start, and Flo has the edge specifically if free pregnancy tracking is what you need. Natural Cycles is only worth it if you specifically want its contraception feature.

Privacy Comparison

Cycle and fertility data is some of the most sensitive information you can put in an app, so this is worth reading closely rather than skimming.

Clue has the strongest privacy reputation of the three. It's based in Berlin, Germany, subject to strict EU GDPR privacy laws, and has publicly committed to not sharing user data with US authorities. It states it does not sell your personal data, and it has built privacy into its brand for over a decade with a clean track record.

Flo's record is weaker. The FTC settled with Flo in 2021 over sharing period and pregnancy data with Facebook and Google without user consent. A related class action against Flo and Google got preliminary court approval for a combined $59.5 million settlement in April 2026, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025 for its role in receiving that data. Flo has since added an Anonymous Mode that separates health data from your identity, and it has open-sourced that work. Whether that's enough is a personal call, but the history is worth knowing before you hand over your data.

Natural Cycles handles sensitive fertility data as an FDA-regulated medical device based in Sweden under EU privacy law, which carries its own data obligations distinct from a general tracker. It doesn't carry the kind of public enforcement history Flo does, but as with any health app, it's worth reading the current privacy policy so you know exactly what's collected and shared.

Read each company's current privacy policy and decide what you're comfortable with, especially if app-based contraception data is part of your concern.

Which Handles Irregular Cycles Best

This is where the tracker-vs-method distinction matters most, because "handles irregular cycles well" means something different for a tracker than it does for a contraceptive method.

As trackers, Clue and Flo both predict from calendar and symptom history. Those calendar-based predictions can drop to roughly 18% accuracy when cycles vary significantly. Neither app is great at this, but Clue's flexible, wide-ranging symptom logging makes it a common pick for people whose cycles aren't textbook, since it logs and learns from whatever pattern shows up rather than assuming a standard length.

Natural Cycles works differently, and that changes the calculus. Instead of guessing fertile days from a calendar, it estimates ovulation retrospectively from your daily temperature. That's a real advantage for accuracy in principle, but the company notes the method works best with reasonably regular cycles and a consistent sleep routine, so it's a weaker fit for very irregular cycles or conditions like PCOS. Disrupted sleep, travel, and inconsistent measurement timing can degrade the temperature signal it depends on.

If your cycles are unpredictable and you mainly want to understand them, a flexible tracker like Clue is the more forgiving choice day to day. If you're evaluating Natural Cycles specifically as a method and your cycles are irregular, that's a conversation to have with a healthcare provider about whether temperature-based tracking will work reliably for you.

What About Stardust?

Stardust comes up in the same searches because it's a period tracker with real traction, particularly with younger users. Like Clue and Flo, it's a tracker, not a contraceptive, so it sits closer to that pair than to Natural Cycles.

Stardust pairs cycle tracking with a daily hormone narrative and a distinctive, playful design that treats the cycle as something to engage with rather than just log. Its core experience is free, with an optional paid tier, and it's available on both iOS and Android. It drew privacy criticism in 2022, when reporting showed it shared user phone numbers with a third-party analytics service; the company updated its privacy policy and practices afterward, and its posture has improved since. Its symptom tracking is lighter than Clue's or Flo's, and it doesn't have a pregnancy mode.

In short: if Clue and Flo feel clinical or like a chore, Stardust is the option built around daily engagement. It's not a substitute for Natural Cycles if what you actually want is app-based contraception.

Which Should You Choose?

There's no single "best" app. It depends on what you need most:

Choose Clue if:

  • Privacy is your top priority in a tracker
  • You want a clean, science-backed tracker without extra noise
  • Your cycles are irregular and you want flexible logging rather than rigid predictions
  • You don't need the app to double as birth control

Choose Flo if:

  • You want the largest community and content library
  • You want free pregnancy tracking included in the free tier
  • You're comfortable with Flo's privacy history, or you'll use Anonymous Mode

Choose Natural Cycles if:

  • You want app-based birth control that's actually FDA-cleared for it
  • You prefer a hormone-free method and have talked it through with a provider
  • Your cycles are reasonably regular and your sleep routine is consistent
  • You're willing to pay and take a daily temperature reading (or already own a compatible wearable)

Choose Go Go Gaia if:

  • You want cycle, mood, sleep, fitness, nutrition, and habit tracking in one app
  • You track with a wearable (Apple Watch, Oura, or Garmin) and want that data connected to your cycle
  • You want a generous free tier with no ads
  • You're on iOS (or okay using the web app on other platforms)
