Does Mira Sync With Apple Health? Here's How to Get Your Hormone Readings Next to Your Wearable Data

Mira's App Store listing mentions an Oura Ring integration but doesn't mention Apple Health, and Apple Health has no place to store Mira's LH, E3G or PdG numbers anyway. This guide covers what Mira lists today, what Apple Health can actually hold, and four practical ways to keep your Mira readings next to your Apple Health and Oura data.

By Veya Team October 14, 2026 8 min read

Quick Answer: Does Mira Sync With Apple Health?

As of October 14, 2026, Mira doesn't list an Apple Health integration. Its App Store listing mentions an Oura Ring integration (added in version 3.5.15.2) but says nothing about Apple Health or HealthKit.[1]

Even if a sync existed, Apple Health has no data types for LH, E3G or PdG values. The closest thing is an "Ovulation Test Result" category that holds a negative, LH surge, estrogen surge or indeterminate result, not the number.[4][5] To see whether your version of Mira writes anything, check the Apps list in the Health app on your iPhone.[6]

Wearable signals like temperature and HRV already live in Apple Health. Hormone readings from a monitor usually don't.

What does Mira actually list for Apple Health and Oura?

We checked Mira's App Store listing, its shop page and its help center. The listing's release notes for version 3.5.15.2 introduce an Oura Ring integration and say you can track your temperature trends, Sleep Score and Readiness Score in the Mira app.[1] That reads like Oura data flowing into Mira, so Oura is the one wearable Mira names.

We didn't find any official Apple Health or HealthKit wording on the listing or on miracare.com.[1][2] Mira's older Zendesk help center is closed, and the help articles we read on its current help center don't mention Apple Health either.[3] That's not proof Mira never writes to Apple Health. It means neither the listing nor the site says it does, so we won't claim it does.

Mira's wands cover different hormones depending on the kit. Ultra4 wands test LH, E3G, PdG and FSH. Max wands test LH, E3G and PdG. Plus wands test LH and E3G. There are also FSH-only (Ovum) and PdG-only (Confirm) wands.[2] It's a genuinely useful setup if you want numbers instead of lines, and we cover how the hormones differ in what each at-home hormone test measures.

What can Apple Health hold from a hormone monitor?

Apple Health stores data in fixed types. For ovulation tests, HealthKit has one category called Ovulation Test Result, with values for negative, LH surge, estrogen surge and indeterminate.[4][5] Apple also has a separate Progesterone Test Result category, with values for positive, negative and indeterminate.[7] Both are result categories. There's no field for a concentration.

Even a perfect Mira to Apple Health sync could carry a surge flag, not your E3G or PdG curve. If you want the curve next to your other data, you'll need another route.

How do you keep your Mira readings next to your Apple Health and Oura data?

None of these is a perfect sync. Each one gets you part of the way, and you can mix them.

1. Use Mira's Oura integration

If you wear an Oura ring, the listing says you can see temperature trends, Sleep Score and Readiness Score inside the Mira app.[1] That puts your hormone chart and your Oura trends on one screen, which is the closest Mira gets to a built-in combined view. For how Oura's own cycle features work, see Oura cycle insights explained.

2. Export a report or save screenshots

Mira's help center describes a Doctor's Report: tap your name, open "Reports and connections" (or scroll to the bottom of your chart) and tap "Generate Report" to get a PDF you can download or share. Mira says the report needs at least three months of testing with the monitor.[3] A dated screenshot of each cycle's chart also works as a simple record.

3. Log the surge result in an app that writes to Apple Health

Since Apple Health can hold a surge or no-surge result, you can log the result of each test day in an app that reads and writes the Ovulation Test Result category.[4] You keep the numbers in Mira and the yes/no in Apple Health. It's a manual step, but it puts the timing of your LH surge on the same timeline as your wearable data.

4. Use an app that reads Apple Health and lets you log LH

This is where Veya fits. It reads sleep, HRV, wrist temperature and cycle data from Apple Health, along with the Ovulation Test Result category, so whatever your wearable and other apps write there shows up on one timeline. You can log a strip result or a urine LH number in mIU/mL yourself, and upload lab results as a PDF or photo. Our health patterns page shows how those signals line up across a cycle, and apps that pull wearable, lab and cycle data together compares other options.

Veya can't import Mira's hormone charts today. Its E3G and PdG fields take the creatinine-adjusted urine unit (µg/g Cr) that lab urine panels report, which isn't how Mira shows its numbers, so there's no place to log Mira's E3G or PdG readings. Reading monitor screenshots is something we're working on. If you've also tracked periods in another cycle app, Veya can read period dates from a screenshot of that app's calendar, and Mira is one of the sources you can pick.

How can you check whether your version of Mira writes to Apple Health?

Open the Health app on your iPhone, tap your profile picture, and look under Privacy for Apps. Apple says that list shows every app that has requested access to your Health data, and you can see what each one reads or writes.[6] If Mira appears there, tap it to see which categories it's allowed to use. If it doesn't appear, it isn't connected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational information based on published sources and the listings cited below, checked October 14, 2026. Not medical advice.

Does Mira sync with Apple Health?

As of October 14, 2026, Mira doesn't list an Apple Health integration in its App Store listing or on miracare.com. You can check your own version in the Health app under your profile picture, then Privacy, then Apps. If Mira isn't listed there, it isn't connected.

Does Mira work with Oura?

Mira's App Store listing says version 3.5.15.2 added an Oura Ring integration, so you can track temperature trends, Sleep Score and Readiness Score in the Mira app.

Can you export your Mira data?

Mira's help center describes a Doctor's Report, a PDF you can generate from "Reports and connections" or the bottom of your chart and then download or share. Mira says it needs at least three months of testing with the monitor. Screenshots of each cycle's chart are a simple backup.

Can Apple Health store LH, E3G or PdG numbers from Mira?

Apple Health has no data types for LH, E3G or PdG values. It has an Ovulation Test Result category that holds a negative, LH surge, estrogen surge or indeterminate result, so at most a surge flag could be stored, not the number. A separate Progesterone Test Result category holds only a positive, negative or indeterminate result.

How do you see Mira readings next to Apple Watch or Oura data?

There's no automatic route that either company lists. Options include the Mira Oura integration, saving Mira reports or screenshots by cycle, and logging each surge result in an app that reads Apple Health so it lines up with your wearable data. Veya, which we make, reads Apple Health wearable data and lets you log LH results, but it doesn't import Mira charts.

Is it a problem if Mira doesn't sync with Apple Health?

Not for how the monitor works. Mira's readings stay in the Mira app either way. The gap only matters if you want those readings on the same timeline as your sleep or temperature data from other devices.
