Flo vs Glow: Period Tracking or TTC Focus? (2026)

Flo and Glow both track cycles, but they're built around different questions. Flo is a general cycle health app people use for period tracking, symptoms, and pregnancy once it happens. Glow was built around trying to conceive, with fertility tools and a conception-focused community from the start. This guide covers what each app actually does, both apps' privacy records laid out plainly with dates, and a third option built around a different premise.

  • If trying to conceive is specifically why you're tracking: Go with Glow. Fertility-window tools, TTC community, companion pregnancy and baby apps.

Flo: What You Get

Flo is the most-used period tracker in the category, with around 77 million active users and over 300 million registered accounts. It covers the full range of cycle tracking, from period basics to pregnancy, in one app.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with AI-powered predictions
  • AI Health Assistant that checks in on symptoms like cramps, fatigue, and mood
  • Ovulation and fertile-window predictions
  • Thousands of health articles reviewed by 100+ medical professionals, including conception topics
  • Secret Chats: anonymous community discussions across a wide range of topics
  • Pregnancy mode with week-by-week tracking, included free
  • Partner sharing for joint tracking

Strengths

It covers the whole arc, not just TTC. Flo tracks general cycle health, fertility windows, and pregnancy in the same app, so you're not switching apps as your reason for tracking changes.

The community and content library are the largest in the category. Secret Chats put you in anonymous discussions with millions of other users on topics from TTC to PCOS to perimenopause, and thousands of medically reviewed articles back that up.

Free pregnancy mode. Flo includes pregnancy tracking in its free tier, with week-by-week updates and educational content, no subscription required.

Limitations

Privacy is the big concern. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 for sharing period and pregnancy data with Facebook and Google without user consent. A related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025 for its role in receiving that data. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode and open-sourced it.

TTC isn't the primary design. Fertility prediction and conception content exist, but Flo isn't organized around TTC the way Glow is. Some users trying to conceive want an app built around that specific goal.

No nutrition, habit, or sleep tracking. Flo stays focused on cycle, fertility, and pregnancy. You won't find meal logging or habit tracking inside the app.

Pricing

Free: Cycle and symptom tracking, pregnancy mode, community access. Includes ads.

Flo Premium: ~$12.99/month or ~$59.99/year as of 2026. Adds the AI Health Assistant, an ad-free experience, and full content library access. Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

Glow: What You Get

Glow was built from the start as a fertility and TTC app, before expanding into a family of apps covering pregnancy (Glow Nurture), baby tracking (Glow Baby), and a more casual cycle-tracking sibling (Eve by Glow). The core Glow app leans data-forward, encouraging users to log more inputs, like temperature and cervical mucus, than a typical period tracker.

Features

  • Cycle and fertility-window tracking built around conception
  • Detailed logging inputs: basal body temperature, cervical mucus, ovulation test results, and more
  • Fertility insights and a "conception odds" style breakdown from your logged data
  • Community forums organized around TTC, pregnancy loss, and related topics
  • Partner Connect, for sharing tracking data with a partner
  • Companion apps for pregnancy (Glow Nurture) and baby tracking (Glow Baby)

Strengths

It's built for TTC specifically. The logging fields, insights, and community are all organized around trying to conceive, not retrofitted onto a general period tracker. If that's exactly your situation, the focus shows.

The community is TTC-specific. Forums are organized around conception timelines, pregnancy loss, and related topics, which can feel more relevant than a general cycle-tracking community if TTC is why you're there.

Continuity across life stages. Glow's companion apps mean you can move from TTC tracking (Glow) to pregnancy (Glow Nurture) to newborn tracking (Glow Baby) within the same company's app family.

Limitations

Two documented privacy incidents. In 2020, California's Attorney General settled with Glow over a security flaw in its Partner Connect feature that automatically granted a partner's linking request without the other user's confirmation, exposing sensitive reproductive health data without consent. Glow paid a $250,000 penalty. In February 2024, a security researcher found a bug in Glow's developer API that exposed profile data, names, age ranges, self-reported location, and photos, for roughly 25 million users. Glow fixed the bug about a week after being notified.

Detailed logging can feel like a lot. Glow's data-forward approach (temperature, cervical mucus, multiple daily inputs) is a strength for some and a chore for others, especially if you're not deep into a TTC protocol.

Less useful once TTC isn't the goal. If you conceive or stop trying, Glow's TTC-first design means some users migrate to a different app or to Glow's own pregnancy/baby siblings rather than staying in the core app.

Pricing

Free: Core cycle and fertility tracking, community access.

Glow Premium: ~$69.99/year, with a quarterly option around ~$39.99/3 months and a lifetime option around ~$99.99 covering Glow, Glow Baby, Glow Nurture, and Eve, as of 2026. Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

Privacy: What Each App's Record Actually Shows

Cycle and fertility data is sensitive, and both apps have a documented history worth reading before you commit. Here's the factual record for each, with dates.

Flo: FTC Settlement, Then a 2025 Verdict

In 2021, the FTC settled with Flo over sharing users' health data with companies including Facebook and Google without proper consent. A related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final approval hearing set for October 2026, and a jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025 for its role in receiving that data.

Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which separates your health data from your identity, and open-sourced it. These are real changes, made after years of public scrutiny. Whether that history changes your decision is a personal call.

Glow: A 2020 Consent Flaw, Then a 2024 Data Exposure

In 2020, California's Attorney General settled with Glow, Inc. over a security flaw in its Partner Connect feature. When one user sent a linking request to another, the app automatically granted it without asking the second user to confirm, exposing sensitive reproductive health data, including fertility test results and pregnancy history, to a partner without consent. Glow paid a $250,000 civil penalty and agreed to require affirmative consent before sharing user data going forward, along with ongoing privacy and security assessments.

In February 2024, a security researcher reported a bug in Glow's developer API that exposed profile data, names, self-reported age ranges, self-described location, and uploaded photos, for roughly 25 million users. Health-tracking entries themselves weren't part of what was exposed in this incident. Glow fixed the bug about a week after being notified, ahead of the public disclosure.

To Glow's credit, both incidents were addressed: the 2020 settlement's consent requirement is now built into how Partner Connect works, and the 2024 API bug was patched quickly once reported. Whether two separate incidents four years apart changes your decision is a personal call, and it's worth reading Glow's current privacy policy yourself.

Go Go Gaia: Privacy-Focused, Smaller Scale

Go Go Gaia doesn't run ads and doesn't sell user data. That said, it's a newer, smaller company without the years of public scrutiny that Flo or Glow have been through, and there's no GDPR certification since it's a US-based company. The no-ads, no-data-selling approach is a meaningful commitment, but it hasn't been tested by the same level of outside review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Flo or Glow better for trying to conceive?

Glow was built specifically around TTC (trying to conceive), with fertility-window tracking, a community built around conception and pregnancy, and companion apps for pregnancy and baby tracking. Flo covers ovulation and fertility prediction too, and its content library includes conception topics, but the app itself is built as a general cycle tracker first. If TTC-specific community and a conception-first design matter most to you, Glow's focus is the closer fit. If you want the same app to also handle general cycle and symptom tracking well, Flo covers that more broadly.

Is Glow safe to use? What happened with Glow's data?

Glow has two documented privacy incidents worth knowing. In 2020, California's Attorney General settled with Glow over a security flaw in its Partner Connect feature, which automatically granted a linking request without the other user's confirmation, exposing sensitive reproductive health data to a partner without consent. Glow paid a $250,000 penalty and agreed to require affirmative consent before sharing data. In February 2024, a security researcher found a bug in Glow's developer API that exposed data for roughly 25 million users, including names, age ranges, self-reported location, and profile photos, though not health-tracking entries themselves. Glow fixed the bug about a week after being notified. Whether that history is a dealbreaker is a personal call, and it's worth reading Glow's current privacy policy yourself.

Is Flo or Glow more private?

Both have documented privacy histories. Flo settled with the FTC in 2021 over sharing health data with Facebook and Google, and a related class action against Flo, Google, and Flurry received preliminary court approval in April 2026 for a combined $59.5 million settlement, with a final hearing set for October 2026. A jury separately found Meta liable in August 2025. Flo has since added Anonymous Mode. Glow was fined $250,000 by California's AG in 2020 over a consent flaw in its partner-sharing feature, and a 2024 API bug exposed profile data (not health logs) for around 25 million users. Neither company has a clean record, so read each one's current privacy policy before logging sensitive data.

How much does Glow Premium cost?

As of 2026, Glow Premium runs roughly $69.99/year, with a quarterly option around $39.99 and a lifetime option (covering Glow, Glow Baby, Glow Nurture, and Eve) around $99.99. Pricing can shift with promotions and varies by plan, so check the current price inside the app before subscribing.

What's the difference between Glow and Eve by Glow?

Glow and Eve by Glow are both made by the same company, Glow Inc., but aimed at different audiences. Glow is positioned around fertility and TTC tracking with a more clinical, data-forward feel. Eve is a separate app from the same company with a more casual, lifestyle-oriented take on cycle tracking, including sexual wellness content. If Glow's fertility focus doesn't fit what you're after, Eve is worth a look from the same company before switching elsewhere.

What's a good alternative if Flo or Glow isn't the right fit?

Go Go Gaia is worth a look if you want cycle tracking alongside mood, sleep, fitness, and nutrition in one app, plus LH test logging for ovulation confirmation. Core features are free with no ads. The main tradeoffs are that it's iOS only (a web app covers other platforms) and doesn't have the TTC community or content library that Glow or Flo have built up.
