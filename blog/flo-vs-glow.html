Flo vs Glow: Period Tracking or TTC Focus? (2026)

Flo suits you if you want general cycle tracking with community and pregnancy tools; Glow suits you if trying to conceive is why you track. Both have documented privacy histories, laid out below with dates and sources as of October 2026. Veya, our app, suits people who want mood, sleep and nutrition tracked alongside their cycle.

Quick Answer

Flo and Glow answer different reasons for opening a cycle app. Flo is the broader option for general cycle and symptom tracking; its App Store listing claims over 570 million downloads worldwide (as of July 2026). Glow is the narrower option aimed at trying to conceive, with separate Pregnancy and Baby trackers. If you want cycle tracking folded into a broader health picture, Veya is worth a look. Here's how they compare:

  • If you want general cycle tracking with a broad toolset: Go with Flo. Per its listing: a 24/7 Health Assistant, Secret Chats and in-depth pregnancy features, all Premium.
  • If trying to conceive is specifically why you're tracking: Go with Glow. Its site describes an Ovulation Tracker for people trying to conceive, plus Pregnancy and Baby trackers.
  • If you want cycle, mood, sleep, and nutrition tracking in one free app: Try Veya.

Flo: What You Get

Flo is a general cycle-health app. Its App Store listing claims over 570 million downloads worldwide (as of July 2026) and describes period tracking, pregnancy tools, Secret Chats and a 24/7 Health Assistant.

Features

  • Cycle tracking with AI-powered predictions
  • AI Health Assistant that checks in on symptoms like cramps, fatigue, and mood
  • Ovulation and fertile-window predictions
  • An article library with expert-reviewed content, per Flo
  • Secret Chats: anonymous community discussions across a wide range of topics
  • Pregnancy mode for after conception; in-depth pregnancy features are Premium
  • Partner sharing for joint tracking

Strengths

It covers the whole arc, not just TTC. Flo tracks general cycle health, fertility windows, and pregnancy in the same app, so you're not switching apps as your reason for tracking changes.

Community and content. Secret Chats put you in anonymous discussions, and the App Store listing lists the 24/7 Health Assistant and Secret Chats under Premium.

Pregnancy tools. The listing describes a pregnancy mode with checklists and guidance after conception; in-depth pregnancy features are listed under Premium.

Limitations

Privacy is the big concern. The FTC announced a proposed settlement with Flo Health on January 13, 2021, alleging it shared health data from millions of users with third parties including Facebook's and Google's analytics divisions, AppsFlyer and Flurry despite privacy promises; the final order came June 22, 2021. A related $59.5 million class settlement (Google $48 million, Flo $8 million, Flurry $3.5 million) received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing on October 29, 2026 (HIPAA Journal). Meta did not settle: a federal jury found on August 4, 2025 that Meta violated the California Invasion of Privacy Act, with damages to be decided separately (PPC Land). Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, rolled out to all users in 2022, and open-sourced it in 2023 (Flo Health press page).

TTC isn't the primary design. Fertility prediction and conception content exist, but Flo isn't organized around TTC the way Glow is. Some users trying to conceive want an app built around that specific goal.

Narrower scope. The App Store listing describes cycle, pregnancy and wellbeing tools and does not mention meal, habit or sleep logging.

Pricing

Free: Core cycle-tracking tools. Premium adds the Health Assistant, Secret Chats and in-depth pregnancy features.

Flo Premium: Flo's App Store listing lists Premium purchases up to $59.99 (as of October 2026), with lower-priced tiers also listed. Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

Glow: What You Get

Glow's site describes four apps: an Ovulation Tracker, a Pregnancy Tracker, a Baby Tracker, and a Period Tracker (Eve). The Ovulation Tracker is aimed at people trying to conceive or wanting to understand their cycle.

Features

  • Ovulation Tracker for cycle and ovulation tracking, including for trying to conceive
  • Pregnancy Tracker with due-date calculation and a baby registry
  • Baby Tracker for sleep, diapers, and feeding
  • Period Tracker (Eve), which also supports sexual wellness
  • GlowGPT health assistant, advanced analytics and ad-free use (Premium)
  • Separate apps for each stage, so tracking moves with you

Strengths

Built around trying to conceive. Glow's site describes its Ovulation Tracker for people trying to conceive, with separate Pregnancy and Baby trackers for later stages.

Premium adds AI and analytics. Glow Premium includes the GlowGPT assistant, advanced analytics with comparisons to similar users, premium articles and a daily health plan.

Continuity across life stages. Because Pregnancy and Baby trackers are separate apps from the same company, you can move between them as your stage changes.

Limitations

Two documented privacy incidents. California's Attorney General settled with Glow, Inc. in September 2020 with a $250,000 civil penalty. The WilmerHale summary says Partner Connect granted account-linking requests without authorization or confirmation from the user whose data would be shared, and that password-change requests were not adequately verified. Glow agreed to get affirmative consent before disclosing user data to third parties and to run annual privacy and security risk assessments. A TechCrunch report of February 13, 2024 said a bug in Glow's API exposed names, self-reported age group, location, user IDs and profile images for around 25 million users, and that the researcher said it was fixed about a week after reporting.

More apps to manage. Glow splits its tools across four separate apps, which suits some people and feels fragmented to others.

Aimed at one stage. If you stop trying to conceive, you may move to Glow's pregnancy or period apps or to a different tracker.

Pricing

Free: Glow's site does not spell out which features are free, so check each app.

Glow Premium: $59.99/year after a 7-day free trial, per Glow's site (as of October 2026). Check current pricing in-app, as it can shift with promotions.

What Does Each App's Privacy Record Actually Show?

Cycle and fertility data is sensitive, and both apps have a documented history worth reading before you commit. Here's the factual record for each, with dates.

Flo: FTC Settlement, Then a 2025 Verdict

The FTC announced a proposed settlement with Flo Health on January 13, 2021, alleging it shared health data from millions of users with third parties including Facebook's and Google's analytics divisions, AppsFlyer and Flurry despite privacy promises; the final order came June 22, 2021. A related $59.5 million class settlement (Google $48 million, Flo $8 million, Flurry $3.5 million) received preliminary approval in April 2026, with a final approval hearing on October 29, 2026 (HIPAA Journal). Meta did not settle: a federal jury found on August 4, 2025 that Meta violated the California Invasion of Privacy Act, with damages to be decided separately (PPC Land).

Flo has since added Anonymous Mode, which lets users use the app without a name, email or technical identifiers tied to their health data, rolled out in 2022 and open-sourced in 2023 (Flo Health press page). These are real changes, made after years of public scrutiny. Whether that history changes your decision is a personal call.

Glow: A 2020 Consent Flaw, Then a 2024 Data Exposure

California's Attorney General settled with Glow, Inc. in September 2020 with a $250,000 civil penalty. The WilmerHale summary says Partner Connect granted account-linking requests without authorization or confirmation from the user whose data would be shared, and that password-change requests were not adequately verified. Glow agreed to get affirmative consent before disclosing user data to third parties and to run annual privacy and security risk assessments.

A TechCrunch report of February 13, 2024 said a bug in Glow's API exposed names, self-reported age group, location, user IDs and profile images for around 25 million users, and that the researcher said it was fixed about a week after reporting. The coverage does not list health-tracking entries among the exposed data.

To Glow's credit, the settlement required affirmative consent before sharing data, and the 2024 bug was reportedly fixed about a week after it was reported. Whether two separate incidents four years apart changes your decision is a personal call, and it's worth reading Glow's current privacy policy yourself.

Veya: Privacy-Focused, Smaller Scale

Veya doesn't run ads and doesn't sell user data. That said, it's a newer, smaller company without the years of public scrutiny that Flo or Glow have been through, and there's no GDPR certification since it's a US-based company. The no-ads, no-data-selling approach is a meaningful commitment, but it hasn't been tested by the same level of outside review.

If a conception-first design matters most to you, Glow is the closer fit; if you want one app for general cycle and symptom tracking too, Flo covers that more broadly.
