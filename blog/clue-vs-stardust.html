Clue vs Stardust: Which Period Tracker Fits You? (2026)

Choose Clue if privacy and a research-backed, no-frills design matter most; it is CE-marked as a Class 1 medical device in the EU. Choose Stardust if moon-phase and hormone-stage content is what keeps you tracking, and read its privacy record first. Veya, our app, suits people who want cycle, mood and lab tracking in one record.

Quick Answer

Clue and Stardust hold close to opposite ideas of what opening a period tracker should feel like. Clue leans on documentation: a CE mark, published research partnerships and a detailed privacy policy. Stardust leans on personality, with moon-phase content and a visual style you either love or don't. Veya sits outside that split, folding cycle tracking into a broader health record. Here's how the three compare:

  • If science and privacy matter most: Go with Clue. Berlin-based, CE-marked as a Class 1 medical device in the EU, with published research partnerships.
  • If the tracker itself needs personality to hold your attention: Go with Stardust. Moon-phase and hormone-stage content, cycle sharing with friends.
  • If a postpartum mode matters: Stardust added one in version 4.0.0 (October 2026), while Clue's listing describes modes for periods, trying to conceive, pregnancy and perimenopause.[3][4]
  • If you want cycle, symptom, mood, and lab tracking in one free app: Try Veya.

What do you get with Clue?

Clue is a Berlin-built tracker rated 4.8 from about 406,000 App Store ratings as of October 2026, with Clue Plus at $39.99 a year.[3] Founded in 2012 by Ida Tin, Hans Raffauf, Moritz von Buttlar and Mike LaVigne, it became CE-marked as a Class 1 medical device in 2023 and lists research with Oxford University (2018) and the Kinsey Institute (2019).[1]

Features

  • Cycle tracking with predictions Clue describes as clinically validated
  • 200+ symptoms and experiences, including cramps, mood, energy, skin, and sleep
  • Ovulation and fertile-window predictions
  • An in-app article library
  • Wearable sync, including Apple Watch, Oura, WHOOP, Withings, and Fitbit (some syncing may require Clue Plus, check the app for your tier)
  • Clean, gender-neutral, clinical interface

Strengths

Privacy is Clue's clearest advantage. It's based in Berlin, and its privacy policy says it meets German and EU legal requirements and that sensitive health data you track is never shared with or sold to advertisers.[2]

The science-first framing has documentation behind it. Clue lists research collaborations on its About page and the app is CE-marked as a medical device in the EU.[1] If you want a tracker with that kind of paper trail, Clue offers it.

The design stays out of the way. No zodiac signs, no lunar framing. Just cycle and symptom data in a minimal interface, which a lot of people specifically want after trying trackers with more personality baked in.

Limitations

Deeper features sit behind Clue Plus. The App Store listing describes Clue Plus as adding deeper analysis, advanced predictions, and personalized insights ($39.99/year or $9.99/month, as of October 2026).[3] Some wearable syncing may also require Plus, so check the app for your tier.

No nutrition, fitness, or mood-focused tracking beyond symptoms. Clue stays centered on cycle and reproductive health. If you want meal logging, workout tracking, or a dedicated mood log alongside your cycle, that's a separate app.

No astrology or lifestyle framing, which is either a strength or a limitation depending on what keeps you opening a tracker consistently.

What do you get with Stardust?

Stardust is developed by Stardust App LLC, based in New York, and its App Store description says it never sells or shares personal health information.[4] It pairs standard period tracking with moon-phase visuals, zodiac-flavored "witch type" content, and a distinct look that's made it one of the more design-forward period apps around, with a 4.8 out of 5 rating from roughly 119,000 App Store ratings as of October 2026.[4]

Features

  • Cycle, ovulation, and hormone-stage tracking with a daily readout of what a given phase can feel like
  • A moon-phase wheel that lines up lunar phases with your cycle, plus zodiac and "witch type" content
  • Pregnancy tracking, including a 3D and AR baby visualization feature[4]
  • Symptom logging covering flow, cervical mucus, feelings, cravings, and cycle disruptors
  • Wearable data from Apple Watch and Oura Ring[4]
  • Friend and partner sharing for comparing cycles

Strengths

It's genuinely fun to open. Stardust is one of the few trackers people describe that way. The daily hormone narrative and moon-phase framing make cycle tracking feel less like a chore, and that's a real reason people keep using a tracker instead of abandoning it after a week.

The core experience is free. Cycle tracking, symptom logging, and the daily content are available without paying, with Stardust Plus as an optional add-on.

The feature set has grown. Pregnancy tracking and wearable sync with Apple Watch and Oura Ring are both listed as current features, which is more than some lighter astrology-flavored trackers offer.[4]

Limitations

Its privacy record is worth weighing. TechCrunch reported on July 16, 2026, drawing on Mozilla's Nothing Personal research, that Stardust was the only one of six period apps tested that sent sensitive health data to another company: birth control type, reproductive goals, birthdate and logged symptoms reached the analytics firm RudderStack.[5] Stardust said RudderStack is "contractually prohibited from selling or using it for its own purposes," and its App Store description says "We never sell or share your personal health information," so read the current policy yourself.[4][5]

How do Clue and Stardust handle your privacy differently?

Clue has the stronger public record: it is Berlin-based under EU GDPR while Stardust was reported to have sent sensitive details to an analytics firm. Both state they do not sell health data. The factual record for each, with dates:

Clue: Berlin, GDPR, and a CE Mark

Clue is based in Berlin, Germany, and its privacy policy says its data handling meets German and EU legal requirements and that sensitive health data is never shared with or sold to advertisers.[2] (The same policy says some non-health app events go to advertising networks.) Its About page says the app became a CE-marked Class 1 medical device in the EU in 2023, a regulatory status tied to how its cycle and ovulation-prediction features are documented, not a claim that it functions as birth control. Clue's App Store listing says it should not be used as a contraceptive.[1][3]

Stardust: A Low Score From an Independent Review

Stardust's App Store description says it never sells or shares personal health information.[4] TechCrunch reported in July 2026, drawing on research from the Mozilla Foundation's Nothing Personal series, a different picture: Stardust was the only one of six period apps tested that sent sensitive health data to another company, with birthdate, birth control type, reproductive goals and symptoms reaching the analytics firm RudderStack tied to an identifier. A Stardust spokesperson said RudderStack is "contractually prohibited from selling or using it for its own purposes."[5]

Read Stardust's current privacy policy directly if this is a deciding factor for you.

Veya: Privacy-Focused, Smaller Scale

Veya doesn't run ads and doesn't sell user data. That said, it's a newer, smaller company without the years of independent privacy review that Clue or Stardust have been through, and it doesn't carry Clue's CE mark. The no-ads, no-data-selling approach is a meaningful commitment, but it hasn't faced the same level of outside scrutiny yet.

Frequently asked questions about Clue and Stardust

Is Clue or Stardust more accurate for period predictions?

Both apps use algorithm-based prediction that improves with more logged history, and neither publishes independent accuracy figures against outside data. Clue describes its predictions as built on published cycle research and clinically validated modeling. Stardust layers moon-phase and hormone-stage content on top of its own cycle predictions, but the core method is the same calendar and symptom-based approach most trackers use. For irregular cycles, expect either app's forecast to be less reliable, and lean on the patterns you log rather than the prediction itself.

Which is better for privacy, Clue or Stardust?

Clue has the stronger public privacy record. It's based in Berlin under EU GDPR law, and its privacy policy says sensitive health data is never shared with or sold to advertisers. Stardust, based in New York, states it never sells or shares personal health information, but TechCrunch reported in July 2026, on Mozilla Foundation research, that it was the only one of six period apps tested that sent sensitive details (including birth control type and specific symptoms) to another company, an analytics firm. Read each company's current privacy policy yourself before logging sensitive symptoms.

Is Clue a medical device?

Clue's About page says it became a CE-marked Class 1 medical device in the EU in 2023, and its US App Store listing lists an FDA Owner/Operator Number. That regulatory status covers how its cycle-tracking and prediction features are built and documented, not a claim that the general tracking app functions as contraception. Clue's App Store listing says it should not be used as a contraceptive.

What happened with Stardust's privacy policy in 2026?

TechCrunch reported in July 2026, on research from the Mozilla Foundation's Nothing Personal series on period trackers, that Stardust was the only one of the six apps tested that sent sensitive data to another company. The data included birthdate, birth control type, reproductive goals and symptoms, sent to the analytics firm RudderStack tied to an identifier. Stardust's public marketing states it never sells or shares personal health information, so read its current policy directly if this affects your decision.

Does Stardust or Clue have a postpartum mode?

Stardust does: its App Store listing says version 4.0.0 (October 2026) added Postpartum Mode, on top of an existing Pregnancy Mode. Clue's listing describes modes for period tracking, trying to conceive, pregnancy and perimenopause but does not list a postpartum mode. These are listing descriptions as of October 2026, not feature tests.

What's a good alternative to Clue and Stardust?

Core features are free with no ads. It's iOS only (a web app covers other platforms), it carries neither Clue's research partnerships nor Stardust's daily content, and it's newer and smaller than both.
