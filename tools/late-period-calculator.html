How is a late period worked out?

Your period is late when today is past the date it was expected. The expected date is the first day of your last period plus your cycle length, and the days between that date and today are how late you are. If your last period started on March 1 and your cycle is 28 days, the expected date is March 29, so on April 2 you're 4 days late.

A single date can look more exact than it is, so the calculator also gives a range. With one date it allows a few days either side, and with several period dates it uses how much your own cycles have moved. That's how it tells you whether you're late but still inside your range, or past it.

Why do cycles shift?

Your period follows ovulation, so when ovulation moves, the period moves with it. Stress, illness, travel, changes in sleep or exercise, and coming off hormonal birth control can all nudge that timing. Read why a period can be late for the full picture.

When is a pregnancy test designed to be used?

Most home pregnancy tests are designed to be used from the day your period is due, according to the NHS. The calculator shows that date from your dates. If you're worried about anything, talk to a clinician.
