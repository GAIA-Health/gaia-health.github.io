How much protein women actually need

Sports-nutrition research generally expresses protein needs as grams per kilogram of bodyweight rather than one flat daily number, because a 55 kg woman and an 85 kg woman doing the same workout do not need the same gram total. Published ranges run from about 1.2 g/kg for a mostly sedentary day up to 1.8–2.0 g/kg during resistance training or a fat-loss phase, when protein helps protect muscle. This calculator applies those bands to your own weight instead of quoting one number for everyone.

Why the guidance skews male, and what changes for women

A large share of the foundational protein-timing and dosing research was run on young men, which is part of why generic protein calculators don't mention perimenopause or postmenopause at all. The research that does exist on older women points to anabolic resistance: muscle tissue becomes less responsive to a given dose of protein as estrogen declines, so the same gram amount does less work than it used to. That's the rationale behind nudging perimenopausal and postmenopausal women toward the higher end of their activity band above, rather than a fixed extra amount.

Protein and your cycle

The gram-per-kilogram target itself doesn't need to change week to week. What does shift is appetite: progesterone rises after ovulation and is linked to increased hunger and cravings through the luteal phase, which is often when protein and overall food intake climb without any deliberate change in plan. Recognizing that the shift is hormonal, not a lapse in discipline, is usually more useful than trying to eat exactly the same every day of the month.

Spreading protein across the day

Research on muscle protein synthesis points to roughly 25 to 35 g per meal as an efficient dose, rather than saving most of a day's protein for one large dinner. Splitting your total across 3 to 4 meals tends to work better than concentrating it, though the exact number of meals is flexible and should fit your schedule, not the other way around.
